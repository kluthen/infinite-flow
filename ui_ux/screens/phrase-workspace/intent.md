## Focal Point and Header

The score is deliberately the focal point. A player should be able to sight-read the phrase without searching for where bar three begins or which chord governs a note. Four visibly equal measure regions, strong barlines, and chord symbols placed above their harmonic entry give the phrase its form at a glance. At `bp-tablet-landscape` and above, that takes the form of one unbroken staff system holding all four measures.

On phone landscape, below `bp-tablet-landscape`, the phrase is always split over two aligned staff systems of two measures each, so it still reads as one four-bar form split at a natural halfway point rather than as compressed or illegible notation. The legibility floor that keeps the score readable at a distance often leaves one phone-width system too small for four bars; the phone does not try one system first and split only when that fails, it always splits, between sessions as during one. The reason is that a session on a phone always uses the split layout, whatever the phrase contains (see The Staff During a Session), and the phrase the player reads before pressing Start should be exactly the one they play from: with the same layout at idle, Start never moves a note. It also keeps the phone predictable, since whether a phrase fits on one line depends on its notes, and a phone that sometimes showed one line and sometimes two would change shape for reasons the player cannot see. The cost is accepted knowingly: a wide phone no longer shows a short phrase on one line between sessions. The phone is the third display, after the laptop or desktop and the tablet, and its layout stays correct and legible without dictating anything to the larger screens.

On a phone the staff is also sized to the screen's height, not only its width, and it no longer keeps a fixed minimum height there. It is drawn at the largest size at which the cue row and both staff systems fit on the screen together, and never below the size at which it stops being readable. The reason is where the phone sits during a session: on a music stand, with both of the player's hands on the instrument, so nothing can be scrolled once the music starts. A staff taller than the screen would leave part of the lower system out of view with no way to bring it back mid-phrase. The height the staff is fitted to is the screen's height with the browser's toolbars showing, the smallest the browser ever gives the page: phone browsers hide and show their toolbars on their own, and a staff sized to whatever happens to be showing would redraw each time, music moving under the eyes for a reason the player never chose. The size is the same between sessions and during one, so Start moves nothing. A phone screen so short that even the smallest readable staff does not fit leaves the staff no room, exactly like a screen too narrow for it: the workspace shows a short notice saying the screen is too small to show the staff, and no session starts (see When the Staff Has No Room). On a tablet or larger nothing changes; the staff keeps its fixed minimum height.

Each of the two staff systems is given room for the tallest phrase one instrument can produce, not for every instrument at once. The saxophones' music reaches high, with chord symbols above their highest notes, and never low; the keyboard's reaches low, with ledger lines under the staff, and never high. A system sized for the saxophones' highest reach stacked over the keyboard's lowest would hold room that no phrase ever uses, and on a phone every unused staff space is height the notes cannot have: sized that way, the two systems did not fit a landscape phone with its browser toolbar showing. So the systems keep one size for every instrument, which means a profile switch never changes the staff area's size, and the staff is placed inside them for the instrument chosen: higher in its box for the keyboard, lower for a saxophone. A profile switch always brings a new phrase anyway, entering with the usual fade, so the staff lines settling at a new height inside an unchanged frame goes unnoticed. The same smaller system size also keeps the staff area on a tablet or larger close to the first release's height. On the phone, the frame around the staff also gives up part of its padding above and below: that padding was breathing room, and the notes need the height more. The sides keep theirs, since width is not what limits a phone's staff. The small triangle that marks the starting note stays inside the top system, tucked between the note and its chord symbol where there is room, so that marking the note costs the staff as little height as it can.

The header gives only essential reading context and the two actions: controller connection status, meter, tempo, the whole-phrase action and the session action. There is no separate notation-display control to place there — the score always renders in the currently selected instrument profile's own written pitch. It omits generator internals, seed data, and a performance interpretation because these do not help a player read the proposal.

**[v3.1 changed]** `control-summary`, a single muted line after the control section's title and intro copy but before the preset box and the control groups, restates written key, collection, the preset (or "Custom, based on Reel"), tension, and leapiness as one compact settings line so a player scrolling into controls does not have to re-scan every field-group to know what they are about to change; it is static text, not a live region, since it only mirrors state the controls already show. A long name of the player's own can make the line run out of room in a narrow window; it is then cut at its end rather than wrapped, since a line that changes height would push the controls under it. Instrument profile and its active range stay with the instrument-profile control itself, in `profile-range-summary`, because that information is explanatory metadata for one specific choice rather than a top-level setting a player needs restated at a glance.

The score fills the first content row on every landscape target. Controls follow it in the vertical reading order, so a player can read the staff from a distance before scrolling to shape a replacement. Wide displays orient control groups horizontally across the page; landscape phones stack them into a compact scrollable section. The character group is static rather than disclosed: it holds only Arpeggio chaos, first release has no second character-level control, and hiding a group's only control behind a toggle would add friction without protecting the player from anything.

The header takes only the space necessary for brand, controller status, meter and tempo, theme override, the whole-phrase action and the session action. The screen must not shrink notation to accommodate UI chrome. When available space is constrained, control content moves below the score before the score uses a smaller engraving scale. The defined landscape targets are a product constraint; portrait mobile and tablet views receive a rotate-device message instead of an illegible rearrangement, while desktop remains usable when it has sufficient width. A profile switch never carries a phrase forward: with a phrase shown, it always regenerates a complete replacement in the new profile's written pitch, with a fresh seed, rather than testing whether the old phrase would still fit. The UI makes that regenerated replacement clear through the normal stable-frame loading and fade sequence rather than presenting a silent pitch transposition.

## Controller Status

`controller-status` occupies the header rather than a persistent workspace panel because connecting a controller is occasional, not continuous, attention (checked once at the start of a practice sitting, revisited only rarely) and because a persistent panel would claim width from the score, the one region this screen never yields to UI chrome. It is deliberately understated until a controller has actually connected — a plain `btn`, not `btn-primary`, since connecting a controller supports the practice loop rather than being the practice loop itself — and becomes a quiet status badge once one has, or an error-toned "MIDI access blocked" badge if the browser refuses permission, the one state outside a session the player cannot fix from inside the app. Once a controller has connected, the header never falls back to the `Connect controller` button for the rest of the page session: the player already knows what it does. Every badge carries words as well as its coloured dot. Every badge form is also the same width: the label area is sized once for the longest fixed label, "Controller disconnected", and a longer device name is cut with an ellipsis (its full name shows on hover and in the sheet). A badge that changes its words — a device name, "No controller", "MIDI access blocked", "Controller disconnected" — therefore never nudges the meter, tempo, theme control or phrase action beside it, at any screen size; a header that twitches while the player reads is exactly the kind of movement this screen refuses the score. A controller unplugged between sessions is not a fault: the badge quietly reads "No controller", and the same device plugged back in simply reattaches and the badge returns, with no sheet and no new check. Between sessions it opens the connection sheet (`flows/controller-connection/`, `screens/controller-connection/`); during a session it is a status display only, because connecting, switching device, and re-running the check all happen between sessions. It stays in place during a session — disabled if it is the `Connect controller` button — so the header never shifts. Falling silent changes nothing on this screen: the phrase keeps rolling and unplayed notes simply stay unlit. A controller that is actually unplugged mid-session is different and deliberately loud: the player would otherwise keep playing into silence and conclude the app stopped listening. So an error-toned banner with a live countdown takes the session cue row above the staff, which is always there, so the staff never moves, and the header badge takes the same tone. The banner's sentence and its countdown read as one line of ordinary text, so the countdown sits inside the sentence rather than in a column of its own, and the banner is the only session text sized for the music stand: this is the one thing off the staff the player must read from there mid-session. On the phone, where the row also holds the compact `Stop` and `Volume`, the banner drops its "plug it back in to keep playing" clause, which tells the player nothing they don't know once they read "Controller disconnected", so the shorter sentence still fits the row. The banner goes the moment the same controller is plugged back in, and if the countdown runs out the session ends exactly as a silence timeout ends it. No sheet ever opens over a running session: it would hide Stop and the staff at the moment the player needs them. See `flows/controller-connection/intent.md` for the full reasoning.

**[v3 new]** The controller the player last chose is remembered across visits. When the browser already allows MIDI access, the page looks for it at load and the header shows it as connected as soon as it is plugged in, or "No controller" while it is not, exactly as when a controller comes back within a visit; the `Connect controller` button shows only when nothing is remembered or the browser has not yet allowed access, so a load never raises a browser prompt. The reasoning is in [`flows/controller-connection/`](../../flows/controller-connection/intent.md).

## The Mode Switch

**[v3.2 new]** From v3.2 the app has two modes: session mode, the app as it always was, and Jam mode, where the player plays a short idea of their own, a doodle, and the app continues it (see [`flows/jam/`](../../flows/jam/intent.md)). The header shows which one is on with a two-segment switch, `Session` and `Jam`, right after the app's name. The header is where the player looks to know what the app is doing, and a switch with both names shows the mode that is on and the one it is not, where a single "Jam mode" toggle would name only one. A row of tabs over the workspace was rejected: it would spend a whole row on one choice and push the staff down, the one thing this screen refuses. The score toolbar was rejected too: it holds the audio mix, which is never locked, while the mode is locked during a session. Below the desktop breakpoint the header already gives its actions a row of their own, so the switch takes the end of the brand row, where there is room; on a desktop, with everything on one row, it sits between the name and the rest. Its two short words never wrap.

**[v3.2 new]** The switch is a choice made between sessions, like every setting: during a session, while a phrase is being written and while a review shows, it stays in view, read-only, with no note, since `Close review` or `Stop` beside it already says what to do first. Switching is instant, with no animation, and focus stays on the segment pressed. The mode never changes by itself, not when a controller arrives or leaves and not at a session's end, and the app always opens in session mode. The switch is not the only sign of the mode: at idle the session action reads `Start Jam`, the staff shows Jam's empty staff or a phrase written from the doodle, and the controls open with "Shape your Jam", so a player who glances anywhere they act or look sees which mode they are in.

**[v3.2 new]** Jam's part of the app is loaded only when first asked for, so the first press of `Jam` in a visit takes a moment. The switch shows it at once, `Jam` checked and the switch visibly busy, while the workspace holds still as it does while a phrase is being written; session mode stays drawn until Jam mode is ready, then Jam mode appears in one step rather than piece by piece. `Session` stays pressable meanwhile, so a mistaken press is undone at once. If the load fails, which happens only offline before the app's files were kept, session mode stays as it was and the notice row says so (see The Notice Row).

## Start and Stop

The header ends with two actions side by side: the phrase action, then the session action. Primary emphasis always marks the next useful step. Before there is a phrase, that step is generating one, so the phrase action is primary and `Start session` is secondary and disabled. Once a phrase is on screen, it is playing it, so `Start session` becomes primary and `Regenerate phrase` steps back. When a generation has failed, the next step is getting the phrase that was asked for, even if the previous one is still on screen under the failure message: `Retry generation` takes the emphasis and `Start session` is disabled until a regeneration succeeds, so an error message is never on screen when a session starts and a session never begins by removing one and moving the staff. When the staff is blank after a notation failure that trying again can cure, the only useful step is `Retry notation`, and it takes the emphasis back; `Start session` is disabled and the starting note does nothing. When the staff has no room, the cure is a larger window, or, when it is the notes that are too wide, less busy settings, and neither is a header button: `Start session` keeps its emphasis but stays disabled, so nothing in the header changes when a phrase that fits comes back, and the starting note does nothing (see When the Staff Has No Room). The same holds while the rotate-device message replaces the workspace on a portrait-shaped screen: no session starts while the staff is not shown, because a session with no staff to read would end the moment it began. `Start session` is disabled whenever the phrase that would become phrase 1 is missing or about to change: while a new phrase is generating, it waits for that phrase to land, so a session never starts on one phrase and finds another under it. During a session the session action reads `Stop` and is the one primary action on the page; the phrase action stays in its place, disabled, because a gap where it used to be would move `Stop` under the player's finger.

`Start session` and `Stop` share one fixed width, so neither a label change nor an emphasis change ever nudges the header. A Start click leaves focus on the button, which becomes `Stop`, so a keyboard player stops with the same key they started with. There is no global shortcut: with both hands on an instrument, a shortcut that fires from anywhere is mostly a way to stop a session by accident. When a session ends, focus returns to the session action, reading `Start session` again, because the most likely next thing is another session.

**[v3.2 new]** In Jam mode the session action reads `Start Jam`, in the same place and at the same width, so the mode shows where the player acts, and the header keeps both actions in both modes, so switching moves nothing in it. Without a doodle there is no phrase to generate, so `Generate phrase` stays in its place, disabled, and `Start Jam` takes the emphasis; with the doodle Jam mode keeps, the phrase action writes phrase 1 from it, and every rule above applies. Without a connected controller `Start Jam` is disabled, since a doodle is played on one; the row above the staff says so in words (see The Session Cue Row), and every setting can still be set.

## When the Staff Has No Room

A window can be too small for the staff: too narrow for the staff layout at a readable size on any screen, or, on a phone, too short for it. The first release answered this with the notation-error panel and its `Retry notation` button. That was the wrong message: retrying cannot make a window larger, so the button invited a useless action, and "error" suggested that something had broken when nothing had. The workspace now shows one plain sentence in place of the score, with no button, and the sentence goes away by itself the moment the window gives the staff room. A new phrase is then generated: the phrase that was on screen before the notice, or the last one too wide to show, never comes back. The notation error stays for the failures that trying again can actually cure.

The sentence names the cure the player actually has. On a tablet or larger, a window too small for the staff can be made larger, so it says so: "The staff needs more room. Make the window larger." On a phone, below the tablet breakpoint, the screen cannot be made larger, and asking for it would send the player looking for a fix that does not exist, so the sentence only says what is wrong: "This screen is too small to show the staff." A desktop window narrowed below that breakpoint gets the same sentence, although enlarging it would help. That is accepted: the phone's two-system layout usually fits such a window, so the notice shows there only when the window is extremely narrow or short, and a player at a desktop who reads that the screen is too small enlarges the window anyway. In exchange, the sentence follows the same width test that chooses the staff layout, so it always matches the rule that took the room away. When the window has room for the staff but every fresh phrase came out too wide for it (see below), fewer notes are a second cure, and the sentence names both, at every screen size: "These notes need more room. Make the window larger, or choose a lower note density." Naming only the window there would hide the cure the player can reach from the controls right below. On a phone that case is rare, since the busiest density is already unavailable there, so one sentence for it is enough.

The notice follows the rotate-device message: one calm sentence, no action, gone by itself once it no longer applies. It differs in one way, deliberately: it replaces only the score, not the whole workspace. On a portrait screen nothing in the workspace can be used sensibly, but a window that is merely too small still has a working header and working controls, and the controls matter here, as the next paragraph shows. While the notice shows, no session starts, since a session with no staff to read would end the moment it began.

Whether a phrase fits depends on its notes as well as on the window: a busy phrase can need more width than a phone gives at a readable size while another phrase from the same settings fits easily. So at idle a phrase too wide for the room is quietly replaced by a fresh one, with the same retries a session uses for a phrase that does not fit, all inside the ordinary loading: the player just sees the phrase arrive a moment later. Only when every attempt is too wide does the notice show. By then the room really is too small for these settings, and there are two cures, a larger window or less busy settings, which is why the notice names both and why the controls stay editable beside it. The same rule means that shrinking the window at idle can replace the phrase on screen with a new one that fits, rather than hiding it, and a notice that clears always gives way to a fresh phrase rather than the one it replaced (or to the empty staff when the rhythm settings cannot form one, below): a phrase that cannot be read is worth less than a fresh one that can.

That notice clears differently from the one about the window. The staff already has room, so no change of window size clears it by itself: only a phrase that fits does. So anything that could produce one tries again at once, with no extra button to press: widening the window, changing a setting the notes are generated from, such as Note density, or `Regenerate phrase`, which stays available beside the notice because each new round of attempts is another chance. Tempo, the audio mix and the theme try nothing, since they cannot change the notes. A player who follows the sentence's advice sees the result straight away, through the ordinary loading, and the first phrase that fits replaces the notice with the ordinary entrance. If every attempt is still too wide, the notice simply stays, and the next change tries again. Narrowing the window never tries, since it can only make the notes fit less well. Widening it while a round is already running starts nothing either: the round carries on at the new size, because dragging a window's edge fires many changes in a row, and a player still dragging when the round ends starts the next one. While a round of attempts runs, the notice stays in place under the ordinary loading treatment rather than blinking off and back on, and a round that fails reads the sentence out again, so a screen-reader user who changed a setting hears that it did not help.

Either notice gives way to the empty staff instead when the rhythm settings cannot form a phrase at that moment: the window gives the staff room, or a changed setting or a window widened past the tablet breakpoint with `Sixteenth` chosen would start a round of attempts, but the rhythm settings are a combination no phrase can be built from. No phrase can come, so the workspace shows what it shows before a first phrase exists, with the rhythm alert saying why, and a generation-failure message still showing goes with the notice. A notice beside the alert would give two reasons at once, and its advice, a larger window or fewer notes, could not help until the rhythm settings change. Nothing is generated by itself afterwards: the automatic attempts belong to the notice, and once it has gone the workspace behaves as it does before a first phrase, where the player generates by hand. The same holds when the window crosses the tablet breakpoint while attempts are running, the quiet first ones or a round under the notice: each attempt uses the note density the window gives when it is made, like every phrase, and if that makes the rhythm settings impossible the attempts stop there, and the workspace shows the empty staff with the rhythm alert; a phrase left on screen only while a new one was being made goes with them. A phrase shown in its own right, with no new phrase being made, stays when the rhythm settings become impossible, as long as it fits; if it stops fitting then, it gives way to the empty staff too, not to the notice, whose advice could not help until the rhythm settings change.

On a phone, below the tablet breakpoint, the `Sixteenth` choice of Note density is unavailable, and a short line under the control says why. A phrase built mainly on sixteenth notes is the one most likely to be too wide for a phone's two-bar system at a readable size, often enough that the retries and the notice would become the phone's usual experience with that setting. The phone is the third display, after the laptop or desktop and the tablet, so it gives up one choice rather than making every phrase a gamble. It does not lose sixteenth notes: with Eighth notes as the main value, the varied rhythm settings still mix in patterns such as a dotted eighth followed by a sixteenth. A player who chose `Sixteenth` on a larger screen keeps that choice: on the phone the control shows `Eighth` as the value actually used, with the stored `Sixteenth` coming back on a larger screen. The rule follows the size the window has now, not the one it had when a phrase or a session began: crossing the tablet breakpoint never replaces the phrase on screen for this reason alone, and the next phrase uses the value that now applies. A session only ever steps down: once a window narrowed mid-session has made it generate with eighth notes, it stays with them until it ends, and the stored choice returns between sessions. The phrase on screen when the player presses Start was generated before the session began, with the density that applied then, so the session's own choice starts with its second phrase: after a window widened past the tablet breakpoint just before Start, the phrase on screen keeps its eighth notes and the phrases after it use the stored `Sixteenth`.

When a window change ends a session before its first phrase has sounded and the staff has no room at the new size, the workspace lands on the notice, and keyboard focus moves to the notice itself. The button that had focus, `Stop`, has turned into a disabled `Start session`, and the next button along would be `Regenerate phrase`, which cannot help while the window is too small: its phrase would land straight back on the notice. Focusing the notice reads its sentence to a screen-reader user and puts a keyboard user right beside the staff, one Tab away from everything else.

## The Session Cue Row

Between the score toolbar and the staff there is one row, always present, at one fixed height, where the session speaks. It holds at most one thing: the starting-note line at idle, the count-in when a session starts, and the session's notices while one runs. Because it exists before, during and after a session and never changes height, neither pressing Start, nor the count-in, nor a disconnect ever moves the staff by a pixel. The alternative, a row that appears only when needed, would push the staff down at exactly the moments the player is trying to read it. The accepted cost is an empty band at idle when no controller is connected.

The starting-note line is neutral and quiet: "Play this note to start: B♭4". It names the note in written pitch, the pitch on the staff, with its octave number, and phrase 1's first note carries a small accent-coloured triangle above it on the staff. The triangle is what makes the octave unambiguous for a player who doesn't read octave numbers: the note to play is the one it points at. The line and the triangle appear only when that note can actually start a session, so they never promise something the app won't do: without a controller attached, or while a generation-failure message is showing, the row simply stays empty; more generally, they show only while `Start session` is enabled.

**[v3 new]** After a load, the browser plays no sound until the page has had its first click, tap or key press, and a note from the controller does not count. A controller that reattached by itself at load has had no click, so until the first one the starting note starts nothing, and the line says what to do instead: "Click the page once, then play this note to start: B♭4". The instruction stays where the player already looks for the starting note, rather than in a notice of its own that would appear and then go. On the phone, where the row is narrower, it shortens to "Click once, then play to start: B♭4". One click anywhere lifts it and the line reads as usual at once. `Start session` is such a click. See [`flows/play-session/`](../../flows/play-session/intent.md) for why a silent start would be worse.

**[v3 new]** At idle the row also ends with up to two links to past sessions, after the starting-note line or alone: `Review last session`, whenever the most recent session can be reviewed, after a reload too, and `All sessions`, whenever at least one session is kept on the device. The first keeps v2's one-press way back to the session just played; the second opens the list of every kept session in the review's place. They sit in this row because it is already the place for what concerns sessions, at the edge of the staff rather than in the header, whose widths are fixed and full. On the phone the first reads `Last session`, so both still fit on one line beside the starting-note line. Both go when a session starts, since nothing about past sessions belongs on screen while one runs, and come back when it ends. Their journeys are in [`flows/kept-sessions/`](../../flows/kept-sessions/intent.md).

**[v3.2 new]** In Jam mode without a doodle there is no phrase 1 to name, so the line names the home note of Jam's key, in the octave nearest the middle of the instrument's range: "Play this note to start: G4". No triangle marks it, since there is no note on the staff to mark; the octave number carries it alone. The home note is the one note a player always knows where to find in their key, and playing it says "this is the key" before the doodle starts. While the player taps the tempo on the controller (see Jam's Controls), the line steps aside, since the note is then a tap, not a start.

**[v3.2 new]** Without a connected controller, Jam mode puts a line in the same place instead: "A Jam needs a connected controller. Connect one to start.", shortened on the phone to "Connect a controller to start.". The row is where the player already looks to know how to start, so the reason `Start Jam` is disabled is said where the starting note would be, in neutral words, and goes as soon as a controller is connected.

The count-in, "1 2 3 4", is set large, because the player may already be standing back from the screen with the instrument up. Each beat lights on its click, exactly on the audio clock and with no transition: the current beat is strong and underlined, the beats already passed stay visible, the ones to come are muted. At any glance the player can see how much of the bar is left before they play. The staff stays untouched throughout, so bar 1 is fully readable while they wait for it.

The session's notices use the same row. A lost controller's banner is error-toned, sized for the music stand, and takes priority over everything, the count-in included: unplugged before bar 1, the banner takes the row at once while the count-in keeps clicking, since the player can count one bar by ear but cannot guess a countdown. The "struggling" notice, shown when the generator has had to repeat a phrase twice in a row, is neutral and body-sized: it is information, not a fault the player caused or can fix mid-phrase. Nothing in the row animates, and nothing in it is announced, since everything in it changes in time with the music.

**[v3.2 new]** A Jam uses the same row for what happens before phrase 1. After the count-in it shows "Your doodle" and the numbers 1 to 4, one per measure of the doodle, lit like the count-in's beats as each measure begins, so a glance tells the player how much room is left before the doodle ends by itself. While the app writes phrase 1 it says so, "Writing your first phrase", still: animated dots would suggest a progress the app cannot measure. Then the read-in measure is counted as a count-in, phrase 1 already on the staff. On the phone the two lines shorten to "Doodle" and "Writing", so that they fit beside the compact `Stop` and `Volume`.

## Jam Mode

**[v3.2 new]** In Jam mode, until the player has played a doodle, the staff shows no phrase: the phrases are written from the doodle, so there is nothing to read before it. The staff shows an empty system instead, with the clef, the key signature of Jam's key and mode, and the time signature, so the player sees which key they are about to play in, and the lower system's place holds one sentence: "After the count-in, play your doodle: one to four measures. The app continues it." An empty staff is honest here in a way it is not at an ordinary idle, where it would read as music with nothing in it: in Jam mode it is the page the player is about to fill. It stays through the count-in, the doodle and the writing, unchanged, and phrase 1 replaces it with the ordinary entrance when it is ready to be read. When a Jam could not continue the doodle, a calm line under the sentence says so, never error-toned, until the player tries again or changes a setting. On a phone, where the lower system is shortest, the line takes the sentence's place rather than following it.

**[v3.2 new]** After a Jam, Jam mode keeps its doodle, and the staff shows phrase 1 already written from it, ready for the next Jam; `Jam again on this doodle` in a kept Jam session's review brings an older doodle back the same way. A bar above the staff says what is going on: "Jamming again on your doodle: phrase 1 continues it. Leaving Jam mode lets it go." It sits where a generation failure message would, between the toolbar and the staff, rather than in the row above the staff, which belongs to the starting note and the session. At its end, `New doodle` lets the doodle go and brings the empty staff back for a new idea. It is a plain button, not a primary one, since going round again is what the bar is for, and it asks for no confirmation: the doodle stays in the kept session it came from, whose review offers it again. When the doodle's key and mode suggestion has not been answered, the bar offers it too, in Jam's words: `Jam in G Dorian` and `Keep C Major`. The bar never appears or goes during a session: like the notice row at the top, it holds still, its buttons disabled, so a start never moves the staff, and it changes only between sessions, after something the player did.

**[v3.2 new]** With a doodle kept, a change of any setting phrase 1 is written from rewrites phrase 1 from the doodle at once, through the ordinary loading. In session mode a setting change leaves the phrase on screen alone and asks at Start whether to play it; in Jam mode the phrase on screen is the doodle's continuation, and showing one the settings no longer describe would make the player wonder which idea Start will play. Only the tempo rewrites nothing, since it changes no note. The reasoning for the flow is in [`flows/jam/`](../../flows/jam/intent.md).

## Jam's Controls

**[v3.2 new]** In Jam mode the controls below the staff are Jam's own, a small set for the Fledgling Composer (P3) growing an idea (P3.C3), who wants to hear it grown, not to tune a generator. The section's title says so, "Shape your Jam", and one sentence says what the set is: "Set these before you start. Jam keeps its own key, mode and tempo; the rhythm and the melody's shape come from your doodle." The summary line under it names what matters most in a Jam, the key and mode, the drift and the rhythm lock. There is no preset box and no mark on the keys and modes: presets belong to session mode, where they steer variety, and in a Jam the doodle and the drift do that job. Jam's key, mode and tempo are its own, so a Jam in G Dorian never moves session mode's C Ionian; the instrument is shared, since it describes the player, not the music.

**[v3.2 new]** The settings come in three groups, in the order a player thinks about a Jam. **Foundation** holds what the Jam is played in: the tempo, the key and mode, the instrument, and how the harmony moves and how tense it gets. **Following your doodle** holds how closely the phrases follow the idea: the drift, and Jam's own rhythm lock, which keeps one rhythm, the one nearest the doodle, so the eyes can stay on the notes. **From your doodle** comes last and holds nothing to set: it shows what the app read from the doodle.

**[v3.2 new]** What the doodle shows, how dense, varied, syncopated and silent its rhythm is, how its melody moves, how far it leaps and where it sits, is read from it, so the phrases move like it. The group shows these as a short list of seven lines, each named as the control it stands for in session mode, with its value in that control's words, "Note density: Eighth". A value the doodle does not show reads as the app's default, marked "(default)", so the list never claims to have heard what it did not. A list of words was chosen over the seven controls shown read-only: read-only controls look like settings that are locked and could be unlocked, and a player would look for the way to change them. Before any doodle the group says what will appear there, "They show here once you've played a doodle." The list fills as a fresh Jam's phrase 1 enters, silently: it is the last group, so filling it moves nothing the player is reading. On a phone, where sixteenth notes are not offered, a doodle read as sixteenth notes plays in eighth notes, and the list says so: "Eighth (Sixteenth on larger screens)".

**[v3.2 new]** Jam's harmony is few settings, not fixed ones. Harmonic motion and Tension are session mode's two controls, with Jam's own values, since a doodle says little about its chords. The seven embellishment figures become one level, `Off`, `Rarely`, `Often`, `Very often`, given to all seven alike: seven figure controls would turn a small set back into a generator to tune, and a player growing an idea wants "more decoration or less", not a choice between an appoggiatura and an enclosure.

**[v3.2 new]** Beside Jam's tempo, `Tap tempo` lets the player set it by tapping the beat. Tapping is armed by pressing the button, never by notes alone: notes at idle would otherwise count as taps all the time, and a player warming up before a Jam would keep moving the tempo without meaning to. One press arms it, so a player with both hands on the instrument presses once and taps on the controller. From the fourth tap the tempo shows at once in the stepper, and each tap after corrects it, so an uneven start is simply tapped over. The line under the button says what is happening at each step in plain words, read out politely to a screen reader, and never judges the taps. Tapping starts nothing and sounds nothing.

## The Staff During a Session

The staff area always reserves room for two systems, one above the other. At idle on a larger screen the lower system is empty space, not a placeholder or a blank staff: an empty staff would read as music with nothing in it. The space is reserved so that a session start never changes the staff area's size. During a session the player reads one system while the other is refilled with what comes next, and nothing ever scrolls: the music never moves under the eyes, the hardest thing to follow from a music stand.

A session's staff layout is set by the screen size, not by phrase 1: on a phone, below the tablet breakpoint, a session always splits each phrase over the two systems, two bars on each; on a tablet or larger it always shows one whole phrase per system. How wide a phrase needs to be depends on its notes, so choosing the layout from phrase 1 would make the same phone behave differently from one session to the next for reasons the player cannot see. The phone is the third display, and the split layout is the one that reliably holds there. A phone shows the same split layout between sessions too (see Focal Point and Header), so on every screen the layout a session keeps is the one already on screen when the player presses Start, and Start never moves a note.

Because fit depends on the notes, not only on the settings a session locks, a phrase generated mid-session may be too wide for the layout the session holds. Fitting that layout at a readable size is therefore part of what makes a session phrase usable at all: a phrase that does not fit is one more failed attempt, and the app quietly tries a fresh one, exactly as when the generator cannot find a phrase. The player sees nothing. A misfit that a retry fixes never counts toward the notice that says the generator is struggling with these settings. Only when every retry has failed does the session fall back to repeating the phrase just played, which is already on screen in this layout, and a repeat counts toward that notice whatever caused its failed attempts, misfits included. So when the screen width alone caused a run of repeats, the notice's "these settings" is slightly off; that is accepted, because it takes a run of phrases all too wide for the screen, which is rare, and a separate count or wording would be a hidden rule for a case the player will hardly ever meet. A session never shows the notation error the idle workspace can show: a phrase that does not fit is retried, a repeat is already on screen in this layout, and a change of window that leaves anything, on screen or waiting its turn, unable to fit ends the session instead.

The playhead is deliberately minor: a quiet tint behind the measure being played, stepping to the next measure at each barline. It tells a glance where the player is without moving on every beat, and it sits behind the notes so it never competes with them. The system not being played is never dimmed, because it is what the player reads ahead into.

When the player plays an expected note, the note itself lights in the acknowledgement colour, instantly, and stays lit until its system is refilled. Only the note's own parts light; a beam or ledger line it shares with its neighbours stays in the staff ink, so one lit note never half-lights another. The colour is a mid-tone green of its own rather than the success green used for text, chosen so that it differs from the staff ink, the staff's background and the playhead band by a contrast ratio of at least 3:1 in both themes. Acknowledgement is carried by colour alone, so that difference in lightness, not the hue, is what lets a player with red–green colour blindness see a lit note. A missed note shows nothing at all: acknowledgement encourages, it never grades.

## The Audio Mix

The backing and click controls live in the score toolbar, above the staff, where they are never locked and, except on a phone during a session (see The Phone and the Window During a Session), always in view. On/off is what a player reaches for mid-session, so each gets a one-click toggle with its state in words, "on" or "off", never in colour alone. Volume is set once and forgotten, so both sliders sit behind a single `Volume` button, in a small panel that opens only when asked and closes with Escape or a click elsewhere. It is the one element allowed to cover part of the staff, because the player opened it. For the same reason a session start closes it: left open from before, it would sit over the top system, and clearing it would take a hand off the instrument. On a narrow screen the toolbar wraps the mix onto a line of its own under the title rather than shrinking it.

## The Rest of the Workspace During a Session

During a session the workspace keeps its shape and changes only its behaviour. The musical controls below the score stay visible, at full strength, but read-only, with the same read-only ring they take while a phrase is generating: a session plays the settings it started with, and a control that looked editable but did nothing would be a lie. When the app itself changes a value during a session, the new value simply shows, with no highlight, because a highlight below the staff would pull the eyes off it. The theme control stays live, since it changes presentation only, and so does the audio mix. The header's controller status becomes a display only, because a controller connects only between sessions.

**[v3.1 changed]** Once the player opts into a session option that changes things by itself (see The Session Options), the controls follow the music rather than the app's plans. A setting the app varies shows its new value at the moment the first phrase built with it starts sounding, and a rising tempo shows in the header at the moment the faster phrase starts: the controls never run ahead of what the player hears, and any phrase in the review maps to one set of values. Each change is a value quietly replaced in place, with nothing that lights, moves or speaks, because the player's eyes are on the staff, reading ahead. That calls for a little care in the layout. A few texts change with the values they report: the one-line settings summary, the preset box, the tempo. Each keeps a fixed size whatever it says, so a change never pushes the fields below it, even out of sight. The texts that could not keep one size simply do not change during a session: the line describing the preset keeps the words it had at the start, the preset's buttons stay the ones the row held, and the marks on the keys and modes keep what they showed.

**[v3.2 new]** The mode switch and the kept doodle's bar follow the same rule: they keep what they showed at the start, read-only or with their buttons disabled, and change only once the session has ended. The one thing that changes below the staff in a Jam is the list of what the app read from the doodle, which fills as a fresh Jam's phrase 1 enters; it is the controls' last group, so nothing the player could be reading moves. The rhythm lock adds nothing to watch: a phrase that changes the rhythm in one of the ways the player allowed, or that takes the nearest workable rhythm because the locked one cannot make a phrase, is played and read as written, with no mark, the way a repeated phrase or a varied setting has never been marked. A mark would pull the eyes off the notes for something the player cannot act on mid-session.

## The Session Options

Three options act only while a session runs: a tempo that rises on its own, a tension that builds and settles over four phrases instead of within one, and settings that drift a step at a time. The rising tempo is mainly for the Speed Builder (P2), who wants fresh material at a steadily higher tempo (P2.S1); the other two serve the Curious Improviser (P1), whose vocabulary grows by exposure (P1.G1), with longer musical shapes and a wider spread of settings than one sitting at one setup would bring. They sit together at the end of the controls, in one group headed "During a session", rather than each beside the field it touches. A player looking for "what will the app do by itself once I start" finds all of it in one place, and the varying settings, which touch nearly every field, would have had nowhere to go. Each is a plain `Off`/`On` choice, like every other setting in the section, with one line underneath saying what it does in everyday words; all three start off, because anything that adapts to the player is opt-in and visible. They are chosen between sessions, like every setting, and shown read-only during one.

**[v3 changed]** Like every setting, the three options are kept across visits, so they start off on a first visit only, and a player who practises with a rising tempo every evening does not have to turn it back on each time. Each comes back on only if the player turned it on: nothing the app did by itself is ever remembered as a choice. The tempo a rising session reached is not kept: it belongs to that session, not to the player's settings, and the "Last session reached 135 BPM." line under the tempo goes with a reload, as before.

Rising tempo needs a connected controller, a product rule; without one the `On` choice is unavailable and a line says so: "Needs a connected controller." The choice the player made is kept, and comes back as soon as a controller is attached, the way the phone keeps a stored `Sixteenth`. During a session the control does not change if the controller is lost: the rise simply pauses, and the disconnect banner already says what matters. The rise itself shows in one place only, the tempo, in the header and in the stepper, which can then read a value the stepper's own steps would never give, such as 95 BPM. When the session ends, the tempo goes back to where the player started it; the review keeps each phrase's tempo. After a session that ran it, a quiet line under the tempo stepper says "Last session reached 135 BPM.": past tense, no colour, no target. It sits by the tempo because that is where the player decides the next session's starting tempo, and it stays until the next session ends, so nothing in the controls changes when a session starts.

**[v3.1 new]** The varying settings now also move the embellishment levels, one figure one level at a time, in the single place v2 gave to switching between Neutral and Jazz, which means nothing once the figures are settings. A move of anything the preset holds turns the preset box to "Custom, based on Reel" from the moment the first phrase built with it sounds, exactly as the setting itself shows, and the box returns to the player's own selection with the settings at the end. The key and mode moves stay inside what the preset is meant for, so the app never opts the player out of their preset by itself; the option's note says so in one sentence, since a player who chose Reel would otherwise wonder whether Vary settings could take them into Ionian. The tension option never makes Custom: it changes what each phrase is generated with, never the setting.

**[v3.2 new]** The rhythm lock joins the group, first: a switch that makes every phrase keep the rhythm of the first one, so the eyes can go to the pitches, which serves the Curious Improviser's reading (P1.G2). It comes first because it changes what the player reads from the very first phrase, where the other three act over several. The group now holds four switches, still each a plain `Off`/`On` choice with one line underneath saying what it does. Jam is not among them: it is a mode of its own, chosen in the header (see The Mode Switch), with its own rhythm lock among its own controls.

**[v3.2 new]** Two options only matter once a switch is on, so they appear under it, in its column, only then: which small rhythm changes are allowed under the rhythm lock, and which groups of settings may vary under Vary settings. A beginner sees one switch until they choose it, and what they set underneath is kept while it is hidden. The allowed changes are off by default, so the lock is strict until the player asks for variety, kind by kind; their note says plainly that what can happen depends on the rhythm settings, because with the strictest rhythm settings almost nothing can. What may vary is all on by default, which is Vary settings as it always was; turning groups off gives the Speed Builder variety they can steer, keys only for instance (P2.S1). When the groups left on, the lock and the current settings leave Vary settings nothing it could move, it cannot be turned on, and a line names the fix, "Turn on another group below.", the groups staying shown so the fix is one click away.

The tension option leaves the Tension control showing the player's own choice, even while each phrase in the group of four steps around it: the control says what the player chose, not what one phrase happened to get. The varying settings are different: they really change the settings, so the controls show each change, Tension included, and the four-phrase steps then go around the new value, and every changed setting returns to the player's own value when the session ends, as the tempo does. A player who switches the option on for one session should not have to undo its drift by hand afterwards.

## The Phone and the Window During a Session

The phone is the third display, after the laptop or desktop and the tablet, and its landscape screen is too short to hold the header, the score toolbar, the cue row and both staff systems at once. During a session only the staff and Stop are essential, so on the phone the session keeps the cue row and both systems whole and lets the rest go: when a session starts there, the page settles, with the same single instant jump the larger screens make when the staff is out of view, so that the cue row and both systems are entirely on screen. That always works whenever a session can run, because on a phone the staff is sized so that the cue row and both systems fit the screen's height together (see Focal Point and Header). The header and the toolbar may then sit above the visible area. Of what is up there, Stop and the volumes are what the player may still need, so for the length of the session a compact `Stop` and the `Volume` button sit at the trailing end of the cue row, on the phone only. They take the end of the row rather than a place of their own because every other band on screen is the staff's. The `Backing` and `Click` toggles are not repeated: on the phone they stay in the toolbar, a scroll away, still reachable but not on screen. That is accepted because the phone is the secondary display and a session never needs them; putting them in the small volume panel too would give it contents that change with the screen size, for a secondary device. Desktop and tablet keep everything on screen and show neither.

A session asks the browser to hold the screen in landscape, and lets go at the end. Where the browser refuses, as it usually does on a phone in an ordinary tab, nothing is said. After that, one rule covers every change of window shape or size: a session ends, exactly as Stop ends it, when the staff can no longer be shown. A window that becomes portrait-shaped on anything smaller than a desktop screen, whether the device turned or the player split or resized the window, would swap the workspace for the rotate-device message and hide the staff and Stop; a window narrowed until the session's staff layout no longer fits at a readable size, or on a phone shortened until it no longer fits, cannot show the staff either. The check also covers what is ready but not yet on screen, such as the second half of the next phrase in the phone's split layout: if any of it no longer fits, the session ends the same way, rather than letting a later refill bring in something that cannot be drawn. A browser toolbar hiding or reappearing is not such a change, since the phone's staff is fitted to the height with the toolbars showing. Any other change redraws both systems at once at the new size, keeping the staff layout the session started with, one system per phrase or a phrase split over two systems: changing the layout mid-session would move where the next phrase appears while the player is reading toward it. The redraw does touch the system being read, which nothing else in a session does, but the player caused it with their own hands, so it is not the music moving by itself; and it keeps what they have already seen, the same material, the playhead on the same measure, the notes already lit.

## After a Session

When a session ends after its first phrase has sounded, the workspace steps aside for the Review: the **Last session** review takes the staff's place, under the same header and score toolbar, with the audio mix and every control still usable, and the header's session button reads `Close review` where it would read `Start session`. No session starts while the Review shows, not by a click and not by a note (a controller can still be connected, its check note included), so a note played while looking back can never throw the Review away; the button itself, sitting where Start will be, says what to do first. Out of sight, the staff gets a fresh phrase, generated from the current settings with the ordinary loading, which is what the player finds when they leave. `Close review` brings the staff back exactly as between any two sessions, focus still on the same button, now `Start session`, so a keyboard player leaves with one key and starts with the same key again. The Review's own design is in [`screens/last-session-review/`](../last-session-review/intent.md); the reasoning for its shape is in [`flows/review-and-export/`](../../flows/review-and-export/intent.md).

**[v3 changed]** Leaving the Review no longer lets it go. The session is kept on the device, so `Review last session` at the end of the row above the staff brings it back until another session takes its place, after a reload too, and `All sessions` opens the list of every kept session in the same place the Review takes, under the same header, with the same `Close review` to leave. A start that ends during its count-in changes nothing that was kept, and the links return. Keeping the list inside the review, rather than on a page of its own, keeps one rule for the staff's place: either the staff, or something to look back at, never both, and never a session starting from under it. The list's design is in [`screens/session-list/`](../session-list/intent.md), and how the two levels connect in [`flows/kept-sessions/`](../../flows/kept-sessions/intent.md).

**[v3.2 new]** The review shows the same way over either mode, and `Close review` returns to the mode it opened from, still read-only in the header meanwhile. After a Jam, what the player finds on leaving is the doodle Jam mode now keeps, with a new phrase 1 written from it out of sight, rather than a fresh phrase. A kept Jam session's review, opened from either mode, offers `Jam again on this doodle`, which leaves the review onto Jam mode with that doodle kept: one press from looking at an old idea to playing on it again, the way back to session mode being the same switch as ever.

## Settings That Stay

**[v3.1 changed]** Every setting on this screen is kept on the device when the player changes it: the musical controls, the embellishment levels, the preset selection (a preset, or Custom and its base), the instrument choice, the theme, the audio mix and the session options, and with them the player's own presets. The next visit opens as this one was left. What is kept is what the player chose, not what the app used: a `Sixteenth` chosen on a laptop shows as `Eighth` on a phone, where it is unavailable, and is still `Sixteenth` on the laptop the next day. A setting the browser cannot keep any more, because the app changed and the value no longer exists, quietly shows its default, with no notice: there is nothing the player could do about it but look at the control, which already shows it.

**[v3.2 new]** The three session settings v3.2 adds are kept the same way: the rhythm lock, the allowed rhythm changes and what Vary settings may move; the lock comes back on only if the player turned it on. Jam's settings are kept too, in a record of their own, so a Jam in G Dorian at 150 BPM never changes the key or the tempo session mode opens with, and `Delete everything` resets both. The first time Jam mode opens, its key, mode and tempo start from session mode's, so the first Jam is in the key the player was practising in. Two things are not kept. The mode is not: the app always opens in session mode, since being in Jam mode is where the player is, not a setting. Nor is the doodle Jam mode keeps: it belongs to this visit's playing, and the kept Jam session's review offers it again after a reload.

**[v3.1 new]** The preset selection comes back as it was, with one rule: the settings win. If what was kept no longer matches the preset (a built-in retuned by a later version, a preset of the player's changed in another tab), the box shows Custom based on it, or Custom when the preset is gone. Loading never moves the key or mode, so the player's sound never changes behind their back. Settings kept before presets existed come back as Neutral or Jazz, or Custom based on one of them; nothing else shows that change.

Saving shows nothing: no "saved" message, no tick, nothing that moves. A setting is a setting; announcing that it stuck would teach the player to doubt the ones that did not announce it.

The saved settings are read before the screen is first drawn, so the saved theme, the controls and the instrument choice appear as the player left them from the first frame, and nothing switches under their eyes a moment later. That read is quick enough not to be noticed. A browser that cannot give the saved data within a short wait, or saved data that cannot be used at all, does not leave the page blank or half-loaded: the app starts from the defaults, keeps nothing new for the rest of the visit so it never overwrites what is kept, and says so in a notice. The first phrase of a visit is therefore always generated from what the player left, never from a default by mistake.

Between sessions the app also prepares, out of sight, the next few phrases a session would play, so that the second phrase can join the first smoothly at fast tempos. Nothing on this screen shows it: not the phrase, not the starting note, not the actions, not what a screen reader hears. An indicator would describe work the player cannot influence and does not need to know about, and would be one more thing changing on screen while they read. The player's own requests always go first.

## The Notice Row

**[v3 new]** Saving can go wrong in ways the player should hear about: a save that failed (the device is full, or the browser refused), an old tab still open after the app was updated in another, or saved data the app cannot use. Each is told in one calm notice, in a row at the very top of the workspace, above the score, where it is seen on arrival without covering anything. The notices are never error-toned: none of them is the player's fault, nothing on the page is broken, and the app keeps working in memory. Their wording, when they appear and how each is dismissed or acted on are in [`flows/saved-data/`](../../flows/saved-data/intent.md).

The row exists only while it holds a notice, so on an ordinary day it takes no room at all. That means a notice appearing or going moves everything below it, which this screen otherwise refuses. So the row never changes during a session: a notice that arises during one waits for its end, and one already showing when a session starts stays exactly as it is, its buttons disabled, until the session ends. Outside a session, the movement follows the player's own action or the page's arrival, where it is expected.

**[v3.2 new]** The row also tells the one way opening Jam mode can fail: offline, before the app's files were kept, Jam's part of the app cannot load. "Jam mode couldn't open because part of the app didn't load. Check that you're online, then try again.", with `Try again` and `Dismiss`. It sits with the saving notices because it is the same kind of news: nothing the player did, nothing broken in what they were doing, session mode still working as it was. It goes by itself once Jam mode opens.

## The Footer

**[v3 new]** The page ends with one quiet line: "Your settings and sessions are kept on this device only.", then a `Your data` button. It answers the question a player may reasonably have, where all this is kept, in the one place a page conventionally keeps such things, and the button opens the sheet that lists what is kept, how much room it takes, and how to delete it (see [`screens/your-data/`](../your-data/intent.md)). It is a footer, not a header button, because the header's widths are fixed and full, and because this is a place a player visits rarely: once to look, perhaps once to clean up. The list of kept sessions has the same button, for the player who is already looking at their sessions. During a session the button is disabled, like every control, and no sheet opens.

## My Instrument

**[v3 new]** The instrument control keeps its three built-in instruments and gains a fourth only once the player has made their own: a segment labelled with the name they gave it, selected and switched exactly like the others, with its range and its three windows shown underneath the same way. Showing an empty fourth choice before it exists would offer a selection that does nothing. Under the range line, one button makes it, `Create my instrument`, and once it exists the same button reads `Edit my instrument`; both open the sheet where it is made (see [`screens/my-instrument/`](../my-instrument/intent.md)). The button sits with the instrument control because that is where a player who finds the three instruments do not fit their own will look, and one button, rather than one per action, keeps the control the same size whatever exists. Like every control it is disabled during a session. A long name simply makes the segment wider, and the segments wrap as they already do on a narrow screen.

## The Preset Bar

**[v3.1 new]** The controls open with a preset: a dropdown under the section's title and summary, before every group of settings, because choosing where to start comes before fine-tuning. In v2 the musical profile sat inside the foundation group, among the very settings it overwrote; first in the section, the preset is read first and chosen first. The owner asked for a dropdown placed higher, and it is the browser's own: the most familiar list there is, by mouse, finger or keyboard, with no code of the app's own to learn or to load. Its list is split into practice presets, styles and the player's own, so a preset that can be changed is always told from one that cannot. The box has one width whatever it shows, "Neutral" or "Custom, based on Modal (Dorian)", so changing the selection never moves the buttons beside it.

**[v3.1 new]** Under the box, one line says what the selection is for, in plain words, because a name such as "Reel" or "Modal (Dorian)" tells a beginner little: what it sounds like, what it is good for, and, for Pop song and Bebop, why the mode control will read Major rather than Ionian. For a preset of the player's own it says so and names the keys and modes it is meant for; in Custom it names where the settings came from and how to keep them. During a session the line keeps its words, so nothing under it moves.

**[v3.1 new]** Beside the box, the buttons that keep a setup: `Save as new preset`, and, when a preset of the player's own is concerned, `Save changes`, `Rename preset` and `Delete preset`. They are plain buttons, never primary, since keeping a setup supports practice rather than being it, and a button appears only when its action applies, so a built-in preset never offers a rename it would refuse. They work between sessions and while a phrase is being generated, since saving changes no setting, and are disabled during a session like every control. The journeys are in [`flows/presets/`](../../flows/presets/intent.md), the sheet in [`screens/preset-sheet/`](../preset-sheet/intent.md).

## The Key and Mode Marks

**[v3.1 new]** Some presets are meant for particular modes: Reel for Mixolydian or Dorian, Pop song and Bebop for Major, Modal (Dorian) for Dorian, and a player's preset may be kept to one key and mode. The Written key and Collection controls show it where the player chooses: each value the preset is meant for carries a small dot in its corner, and one sentence under the control says it in words. A dot is a shape, so colour is never the only signal; it sits in a corner, apart from the selected segment's fill and from the bar a toggle uses, so a marked value never looks chosen. Every value stays choosable: the guidance suggests, and choosing outside it simply makes the settings Custom, which the sentence says beforehand. A dimmed style for the other values was rejected, because it reads as disabled. The dots and the sentence change only between sessions; during a session they hold still with everything else below the staff.

## Embellishments

**[v3.1 new]** After the four musical groups comes a group of seven embellishment figures, the decorating notes the generator can write: one control each, saying how often the app should use that figure. They come after the groups that shape the phrase because they decorate it, and before the options that act during a session, which stay last. Each figure is described in one plain sentence with an example and the rhythm it needs, so a beginner learns what an enclosure is by reading the control, and knows why one rarely appears with quarter notes. The group is always shown, never folded away: a preset changes these levels, and a hidden group would hide what it changed. On larger screens the seven sit in a row of columns, like the options that act during a session, so the figures can be compared at a glance; on a phone they stack.

**[v3.1 new]** The levels are named for what the player will hear: **Off**, **Rarely**, **Often**, **Very often**. The spec's working names describe the generator ("As today", "Favored"); "Rarely" is honest about what the earlier behaviour gave, since a figure tried only when nothing plainer fits is rarely heard. The line under the group's heading says that a level is a preference, never a promise: the rhythm decides where a figure fits, so a figure set to Very often that the rhythm leaves no room for is not a broken promise.

## Seventh Chords

**[v3.3 new]** From v3.3 a chord can be a seventh chord: the plain three-note chord with one more note stacked on top, the richer sound of jazz. One control sets how often: **Seventh chords**, with **Off**, **Sometimes**, **Often** and **Mostly**. It sits in the foundation group right after Harmonic motion, because both shape the chords: a player who has just chosen how the chords move chooses next how rich they are, and a reader who sees "G7" over the staff and wonders where it came from looks among the settings that make the chords. It was kept out of the Embellishments group, whose heading and line speak of decorating the melody: a seventh chord is not a decoration. It looks like a figure level, four segments in a row, because it is the same kind of choice, and its labels say what the player will hear, in the same plain words: "Often" means the same rank in both. "Mostly" says honestly that a seventh comes on nearly every chord, a plain chord only where none fits. Off is the default and every built-in preset holds it, so nothing sounds different until the player asks.

**[v3.3 new]** Its note explains the term by example, from the lexicon's own popup lines, and repeats the Embellishments group's promise: a preference, never a promise. A phrase with fewer seventh chords than the level suggests is never reported, since the player can do nothing about it but play. Like every setting, raising the level changes the next phrase, not the one on screen; the player hears it with `Regenerate phrase`. Vary settings never moves it, and its note says so, because a player who left seventh chords off and turned Vary settings on must never hear one they did not choose.

**[v3.3 new]** Jam mode has a Seventh chords level of its own, in Jam's Foundation group right after Harmonic motion, for the same reason it sits there in session mode. It is Jam's own, like Jam's other harmony settings, so a player who wants jazzier chords in a Jam does not change what session mode plays, and it is `Off` until they raise it, so a Jam sounds as before. With a doodle kept, changing it rewrites phrase 1 from the doodle at once, as every Jam setting but the tempo does.

**[v3.3 new]** Three built-in presets, Jazz, Bebop and Modal (Dorian), end their description with one sentence suggesting the level, the way Reel's suggests trying Ionian. They do not raise it themselves: a built-in preset sounds the same in v3.3 as before, and the suggestion lets a beginner find the richer sound where they are most likely to want it.

**[v3.3 new]** The chord symbols get longer: `Cmaj7`, `Dm7`, `G7`, `Bm7♭5`. They are written in letters, as `m` and `dim` already are, on one line at the size every symbol has, so they stay legible at the smallest size the staff allows; a raised, smaller suffix would fall under that size first. The longest one must fit twice in one measure, with room between, at every width the app supports, and the engraving is checked for it; a symbol is never shrunk, shortened or overlapped to make room. The score's spoken description names each new chord in words ("G dominant seventh", "B half-diminished seventh"), the qualities as the lexicon names them.
