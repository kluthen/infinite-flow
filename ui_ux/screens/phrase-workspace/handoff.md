## Components and Content

- The root uses `layout-app`; the header uses `layout-header`; the body uses `layout-workspace`.
- **[v3 new]** **Notice row.** `app-notice-row` is the first child of `layout-workspace`, before `score-panel`, full width, its notices `--spacing-2` apart. It is not rendered while it holds no notice. Each notice is `inline-alert alert-notice app-notice`: its sentence, then at most one action `btn` and, when it can be dismissed, a `btn` reading "Dismiss", in one row that wraps, the buttons at the inline end. Never error-toned and not a live region. The three notices (a save that failed, an outdated tab, saved data that cannot be used), their copy, actions, triggers and lifetimes are in `flows/saved-data/`; this screen only places the row and holds it still during a session:
  - A notice never appears, changes or leaves during a session, count-in included. One that arises during a session is shown once the session has ended, with the review or, after a count-in end, at idle. One already showing when a session starts stays exactly as it is until the session ends, so nothing on the page moves; its buttons are disabled for the session's length (no sheet opens and no reload happens during a session, and a dismissal would move the staff).
  - At idle and while the review mode shows, a notice appears and leaves at once, with no animation, and the workspace under it moves with it.
  - A notice's `Your data` action opens `screens/your-data/`, as the footer's button does; its `Reload` action reloads the page.
- **[v3 new]** **Footer.** `layout-footer` is the page's last block, after `layout-workspace` (so after `control-section`), at every breakpoint and in every state, the review mode included: one row that wraps, holding `footer-note`, "Your settings and sessions are kept on this device only.", then a plain `btn` reading `Your data`, which opens the Your data sheet (`screens/your-data/`, `flows/saved-data/`). The button is disabled during a session, count-in included, with `btn`'s ordinary disabled look, and enabled at idle and in the review mode. The session list's header has the same button (`screens/session-list/`).
- `score-panel` uses `score-panel`; its header uses `score-toolbar`. The visible title is `Four-measure phrase`.
- `staff-frame` holds the two staff system positions in every state (see Notation Contract). Each position that shows notes holds one `staff-content` unit, its own engraving as a semantic SVG music score, with its own `measure-slot` elements: four in the one-system layout, two in the split layout. At idle in the one-system layout, the top position holds phrase 1 and the bottom one is reserved and empty; in the split layout, phrase 1 is two units, bars 1–2 on top and bars 3–4 below, both drawn at the phrase's one scale. The fade and the refill apply per unit, and each unit carries its phrase's engraving scale, so a session start redraws nothing and each system can fade on its own.
- **[v3.2 new]** **Jam mode's staff.** In Jam mode without a held doodle, and in a fresh Jam until phrase 1 enters, `staff-frame` takes `staff-jam` (`flows/jam/`). The top system position holds one `staff-content` unit engraved as an empty system (see Notation Contract, **The empty system**); the bottom position holds `jam-instruction`, "After the count-in, play your doodle: one to four measures. The app continues it.", centred in the position's block, muted, static. After a Jam whose phrase 1 could not be written, `jam-notice` (`inline-alert alert-notice jam-notice`) follows it in the same block: "The app couldn't continue your doodle with these settings. Try again, or change a setting." Never error-toned and not a live region: the session action, `Start Jam`, names it with `aria-describedby` while it shows, and focus lands there when the Jam ends. It stays until the next start, by either kind, or a change to any control in `control-section` (the tempo and the session options included; the theme and the audio mix are not), and goes with `staff-jam` when Jam mode leaves. No generation runs in this state, so `generation-status` announces nothing; a change of key, mode or instrument redraws the empty system at once.
- **[v3.2 new]** **The held doodle's bar.** `jam-bar` is rendered in `score-panel`, after `score-toolbar` and before the generation-failure banner's place, only while a doodle is held (`flows/jam/`): its sentence, "Jamming again on your doodle: phrase 1 continues it. Changing any setting except the tempo, Rising tempo, the audio mix or the theme lets it go.", then `suggestion-row` when the doodle's suggestion is offered or has just been used. `suggestion-row` takes the review's forms and words (`screens/last-session-review/handoff.md`, `suggestion-row`) except the no-fit form, which is never shown here:
  - **Offering:** "Your doodle sounds like {answer}.", `Use {answer}`, `Keep {key} {mode}` (plain `btn`s, neither primary), and the restriction line when the answer lies outside the restriction in force.
  - **Set:** after `Use`, "Key and mode set to {answer}.", no button, until the key or mode changes again, the doodle is let go, or a session starts on it; held still during that session, it is absent once the session has ended.
  - **Focus.** After `Use {answer}`, focus moves to the set sentence; after `Keep {key} {mode}`, to the bar's own sentence. Both `p`s carry `tabindex="-1"` for this alone, never in the tab order, and take the `:focus-visible` ring. What each button changes is in `flows/jam/handoff.md`.
  - **Held still.** During a session started from the held doodle, count-in included, the bar is unchanged and its buttons are disabled with `btn`'s disabled look, so a start never moves the staff; it appears, changes and leaves only at Idle and in the review mode, after the player's own action (a setting changed, `Jam again on this doodle`, a session deleted, the controller unplugged), with no animation, the staff moving with it then. While the review mode shows it is hidden with the generation-failure banner, kept, and seen again on leaving.
  - It is not a live region; nothing in it is announced.
- `chord-symbol` uses `--font-music` or `--font-ui` only when the notation engine cannot engrave chord symbols; it is never placed independently from the harmonic event it labels.
- **[v3.1 changed]** Every single-select `segmented-control` has `role="radiogroup"` and an accessible group label; each `segment` has `role="radio"` and `aria-checked="true"` or `"false"`. Exactly one segment is checked in every group, at every moment, and the checked segment also receives `is-selected` for its visual state. If the player edits a control after the failure and before pressing it, the retried request reflects that edit; for the four typed failure codes whose alert string names specific settings (harmony, rhythm, pitch, register window), this is how that hint takes effect. `search_limit_exceeded`'s hint to "try different settings" works the same way if the player edits a control, but its "trying again" half can also succeed from the fresh seed alone with no control changed. `Retry notation` (the notation-error state's action) is unrelated: it re-engraves the already-generated phrase's stored request and never builds a new generation request.
- The `alert-error` banner is not re-validated against ordinary control edits: once shown, its content and the `Retry generation` label stay exactly as they are through any number of field-group changes, and change only when the next generation attempt starts — the banner leaves while that attempt is in flight, the workspace moves to Ready on success, and another failure shows the banner again with the new attempt's failure string. The one exception is an instrument-profile switch made while the current rhythm combination (rhythmic variety, note density, syncopation, silence density) is already unsupported (see Notation Contract; States, "Unsupported rhythm combination"): that switch clears the retained phrase and returns the workspace to **Empty** without running a generation request, regardless of whether the new profile's range would otherwise have fit it — which leaves the "Generation error" state — and its `alert-error` banner and `Retry generation` label — behind in favor of the empty-state `Generate phrase` action and the unsupported-rhythm `inline-alert alert-notice`.
- The generation-failure `alert-error` banner's presence condition is independent of a notation error: it stays visible, unchanged, if a notation redraw on the retained score fails while the banner is already showing (see States, "Notation error over a retained generation failure"). While that holds, the workspace-header action reads `Retry notation`, not `Retry generation`, even though the banner is still on screen: only one workspace-header action exists at a time, and a co-active notation error always takes it. The banner becomes actionable again, unchanged, as soon as the notation error clears.

## Notation Contract

- `staff-content` renders in the currently selected instrument profile's own written pitch, transposed from concert-pitch events by that profile's own fixed chromatic and diatonic transposition, always in treble G2 clef. There is no separate notation-display selector and no Concert override: C MIDI keyboard is a unison transposition, B-flat tenor saxophone a major ninth, E-flat alto saxophone a major sixth. The transposition transforms written notes, key, written-key label, chord roots, and the slash bass, if a chord symbol ever carries one (current harmony produces none), while preserving chord quality, alterations, extensions, additions, and omissions, and never alters the concert-pitch events themselves.
- **[v3 new]** My instrument renders the same way, in its own written pitch, transposed by the transposition the player gave it (any constant interval, `flows/my-instrument/`), in treble G2 clef, under every rule of this contract. It has no Concert override either.
- The key signature is spelled from the player's written-key selection directly, not re-derived from the concert tonal center.
- The score includes the applicable clef, key signature, 4/4 signature, rhythmic glyphs, rests, beams, ties, accidentals, barlines, and chord symbols. Chord symbols — root and, if a chord symbol ever carries one (current harmony produces none), slash bass — follow the same written-pitch transposition as every other written element above.
- `measure-slot` boundaries represent real measure extents from engraving layout. They do not invent beat positions or vertically place pitch glyphs in CSS.
- The layout refuses to show a phrase until all four measure durations validate. A phrase must not be cut off, horizontally scrolled, shrunk below its legibility floor (the minimum engraving scale at which the score stays readable), or split into more than two staff systems at supported breakpoints. The staff area's height is the two reserved system positions below, not the phrase's content.
- At `bp-tablet-landscape` and above, the score always engraves as exactly one staff system holding all four measures; if it cannot fit at the legibility floor, **Room for the staff** below applies, never the notation-error state.
- Below `bp-tablet-landscape`, the score always engraves in the split layout, at idle as during a session: two staff systems of two measures each within the same staff area — bars 1-2 on the top system, bars 3-4 on the bottom — with clef and key signature repeated on the second system and the 4/4 time signature shown only on the first system. It never first attempts one system, however short the phrase. All four `measure-slot` regions stay visibly equal in width in both the one-system and split layouts, and in the split layout the two systems' measure columns stay aligned with each other. If the split layout cannot fit at the legibility floor, **Room for the staff** below applies, never the notation-error state.
- The staff area always reserves two staff systems, one above the other, in every state (**Empty**, **Loading**, **Ready**, the error states, and every session state), so a session start never changes its size. Each system position has one block size for the current viewport, set by the notation layout and the same for both positions, never by a phrase's content (below `bp-tablet-landscape` it follows the height-fit scale in the next item); at idle, a phrase whose content does not fit its system position at the legibility floor is regenerated (see **Room for the staff** below). At `bp-tablet-landscape` and above, where one system holds all four measures, the idle phrase sits on the top system and the bottom system position is blank: no staff lines, no placeholder. Below `bp-tablet-landscape`, in the split layout, the idle phrase already fills both. During a session the systems refill as defined in `flows/play-session/`; a cleared system is blank until its refill enters.
- **Scale below `bp-tablet-landscape`.** The engraving scale is the largest that fits both the width and the height, capped at the notation maximum and never below the legibility floor. Height: `session-cue-row` and both system positions, with `staff-frame`'s block padding and border, fit together inside the small viewport height (browser toolbars showing); the two positions' share of it is `--staff-systems-phone-block-budget`. Below `bp-tablet-landscape`, `staff-frame`'s block padding is `--staff-frame-padding-block-phone`, smaller than its usual `--staff-frame-padding`, which it keeps on the inline sides (see Responsive Behavior). `staff-frame` has no fixed minimum block size there: its block size is the two system positions at the height-fit scale, which depends on the viewport and never on a phrase, so it is the same in every state, at idle and in a session, and a refill never changes it; a phrase whose width needs a smaller scale draws smaller within its positions. Because the budget uses the small viewport height, a browser toolbar hiding or reappearing never changes the scale and never redraws the staff. If even the legibility floor cannot fit the height, the staff has no room: at idle the workspace shows **Needs more room** (see **Room for the staff** below and States), no session can start, and a running session ends (see Responsive Behavior). At `bp-tablet-landscape` and up nothing changes: `staff-frame` keeps its fixed minimum block size and the scale fits the width only.
- **System position size and staff placement.** The two system positions share one block size in staff spaces, the same for every instrument profile and at every breakpoint, so neither a phrase nor a profile switch ever changes the staff area's size; only the scale turns it into a block size on screen.
  - **Size.** A position holds the tallest ink any one profile's written range can produce, with about one staff space clear at each end: chord symbols over the profile's highest written notes, the ledger lines under its lowest, stems, beams, ties, and `start-note-marker` (see **Start marker**). It is not sized for the highest ink of one profile stacked over the lowest ink of another, which no phrase can reach: the saxophones reach high (written up to C6, with chord symbols above) and never below written C4; the C MIDI keyboard reaches low (written down to C3, with its ledger lines) and never above written C5.
  - **Placement.** Within the position, the staff is placed for the selected profile's written range: a range that reaches high gets more room above the staff, one that reaches low more room below. The placement is derived from the profile's written range, never from a table of profiles, so a future profile needs no new rule; a profile whose range would need a taller position makes the one shared size taller for every profile.
  - The placement changes only with the profile, never with a phrase, so a refill, a repeat or a session start never moves a staff line. A profile switch, which always replaces the phrase, may move the staff within its positions; the new phrase enters with the usual `staff-content` fade and the staff area keeps its size.
  - Whether every profile's ink stays inside the position is checked by the engraving calibration, which measures real engraved phrases for every profile, start marker included.
  - **[v3 new]** My instrument is placed by the same rule, from its written range. Its range counts toward the one shared size like any profile's, so an instrument whose range needs a taller position makes the staff area taller for every choice; the size changes only when my instrument is saved or deleted, never with a phrase or a selection, and the phone's height-fit scale applies to the new size.
- **Room for the staff.** At idle, a phrase that cannot be drawn at the legibility floor fails for one of two reasons, handled apart; neither ever takes the notation-error state, which is kept for failures a retry can cure.
  - **No room.** The viewport cannot hold the staff layout for its breakpoint at the legibility floor, whatever phrase it holds: it is too narrow for the layout's fixed parts (clef, key signature for the current written key, time signature) with four measures at the engraver's minimum measure width, or, below `bp-tablet-landscape`, too short for the floor (see **Scale below `bp-tablet-landscape`**). The workspace shows **Needs more room** with its no-room sentence (see States) and runs no fresh-seed retries, since no phrase could fit.
  - **Too many notes.** The viewport has room, but this phrase's notes need more width than the legibility floor allows. The phrase is never shown: the app quietly generates a replacement with a fresh seed, up to three times, the retries a session's look-ahead takes (at idle there is no carry-in and no deadline; see `flows/play-session/handoff.md`, Look-ahead), all inside one **Loading**, which simply lasts longer. Each attempt takes the note density the viewport gives when it is generated (see Components and Content, Note density), as every generation does. If a crossing of `bp-tablet-landscape` during these retries makes the rhythm combination unsupported (a widening, with a stored `Sixteenth`), the retries stop, no attempt is drawn, and the workspace shows **Empty** with the unsupported-rhythm alert, as a round under the notice does (see States, **Needs more room**, **Under an unsupported rhythm combination**). The first phrase that fits lands in **Ready**; the retries show nothing and announce nothing beyond the Loading's own `Generating phrase` and `Phrase ready`. If every attempt produced a phrase too wide to fit, the workspace shows **Needs more room** with its too-many-notes sentence, and no attempt is drawn. An attempt that finds no phrase at all ends the run with the ordinary generation-failure banner and its failure string, as any idle generation failure does: **Generation error** when a phrase stayed on screen through the Loading, **Initial generation error** when the Loading showed no score (see States).
  - This applies to every idle engraving: a generation, a regeneration, a `Retry generation`, a profile-switch regeneration, the new phrase generated when **Needs more room** clears, a round of retries started under its too-many-notes sentence, a session ended during the count-in (see States), and a viewport change at idle that leaves the phrase on screen too wide. While the rhythm combination is unsupported no generation can run, so a phrase on screen that stops fitting is not drawn, and the workspace shows **Empty** with the unsupported-rhythm alert (see States, **Needs more room**, **Under an unsupported rhythm combination**); one widening past `bp-tablet-landscape` can do both at once, switching to one system per phrase and making a stored `Sixteenth` combination unsupported. A window with no room at all still shows **Needs more room** with its no-room sentence.
- **Session staff layout.** A session keeps the layout the rules above give at the breakpoint where it starts, and holds it until it ends, even across `bp-tablet-landscape` (see Responsive Behavior): below `bp-tablet-landscape`, the split layout (bars 1–2 on the top system, bars 3–4 on the bottom); at `bp-tablet-landscape` and up, one system per phrase. Phrase 1 is already shown in that layout at idle, so a session start re-engraves nothing and moves no note. During a session, engraving in the held layout at the legibility floor at the current viewport size is part of what makes a look-ahead phrase valid: a phrase that does not fit is a failed generation attempt and takes the normal fresh-seed retries (see States, **Session running**), invisibly. The notation-error state is never shown during a session: a look-ahead phrase that does not fit is retried; a repeat is the phrase just shown, already in the held layout; and on a viewport change, if the systems on screen or any material already checked and waiting to be drawn no longer fit, the session ends rather than showing it (see Responsive Behavior).
- **Start marker.** At idle, whenever `start-note-line` is shown, the notation pipeline draws `start-note-marker` above phrase 1's first attacked note: a filled isosceles triangle, apex down, 1.2 staff spaces wide and 1 space tall, centred horizontally on the notehead. It is sized in staff spaces by the engraver, coloured by the class, and removed when `start-note-line` goes, including at the count-in's first click. Its base sits half a space above the higher of two edges: the top of the note's own ink (notehead, stem, flag, accidental, ledger lines, and a beam over its stem) and the staff's top line, so it never sits inside the staff, even over a low note whose stem ends within it. Over a note that carries a chord symbol, it sits there only where the gap from that edge up to the chord symbol's bottom is at least 2 spaces (half a space clear above and below it); otherwise its base sits half a space above the chord symbol's top. The chord symbol is never moved for it. It stays inside the top system position; the shared position size allows for it (see **System position size and staff placement**).
- **[v3.2 new]** **The empty system.** In `staff-jam` the notation pipeline engraves one `staff-content` unit in the top system position: the treble clef, the key signature spelled from the written key and collection selected, as for any phrase, and the 4/4 signature, the staff lines running to the system's end, with no barline, no measure, no chord symbol and no `start-note-marker`. It is placed in its position by the selected instrument's placement rule, at the scale the current breakpoint gives (the phone's height-fit scale below `bp-tablet-landscape`), in both layouts, so phrase 1 enters at the read-in measure in the same place, with no staff line moving. It is redrawn at once, with no fade, when the written key, the collection or the instrument changes, and re-engraved at the new size on a viewport change, as any idle content is. A window with no room for the staff layout shows **Needs more room** with its no-room sentence instead (no too-many-notes cause arises: there are no notes); a failure a retry can cure shows **Notation error**, `Retry notation` redrawing it.
- **Playhead band.** During **Session running** the notation pipeline draws one `playhead-band` rectangle for the measure being played: an opaque `--color-playhead-band` fill, never an opacity, drawn behind every glyph of its system, staff lines and barlines included. Horizontally it runs from the measure's opening barline to its closing barline; on a system's first measure it starts where the measure's notes start, after the clef, key signature and time signature, so it never tints them. Vertically it runs from one staff space above the top line to one space below the bottom line, and, where a notehead on that system lies further out, to half a space past that notehead's outer edge, never beyond the system position. The vertical extent is taken over the whole system, not measure by measure, so the band keeps one height while it steps along a system and only its horizontal position moves with the music. Stems, beams, ties and chord symbols that reach beyond it are not covered. On each barline, on the audio clock, it is moved to the next measure, on whichever system that measure sits; it never glides and has no transition. It is absent at idle, during the count-in, and once the session ends.
- **Acknowledgement.** When an expected note is matched, the notation pipeline adds `note-ack` to that note's own notehead, stem, flag, accidental and augmentation dot, and nothing else: a beam shared with other notes, its ledger lines, a tie arc and the chord symbol attached to the note stay in the staff ink, including where the engraver draws the chord symbol inside the note's own group. A tied continuation's notehead, stem, flag, accidental and augmentation dot take `note-ack` together with the note's attack; the tie arc joining them stays in the staff ink, as a beam does. The change is instant, with no animation, and holds until the note's system is cleared for refill. A missed or wrong note changes nothing, and the played note is never drawn.
- On profile selection while a phrase exists, never validate the old phrase against the new active range and never retain or redraw it: keep the written key, collection, and every other control, take a fresh seed, and regenerate the complete phrase as a replacement under the new profile. The one exception is a rhythm-control combination (rhythmic variety, note density, syncopation, silence density) that is already unsupported at the moment of the switch: the switch still applies, but the phrase clears and the workspace returns to the empty state instead of regenerating — see States, "Unsupported rhythm combination." With no phrase shown yet, a profile switch only changes the selected profile; nothing generates.
