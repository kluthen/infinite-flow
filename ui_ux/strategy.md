## Problem Alignment

**[v2 changed]** Infinite Flow is a practice partner for musicians who can already read and play, and who want to get better by playing generated material rather than by studying it. The personas are defined in [`specs/personas.md`](../specs/personas.md); this interface serves them in this order:

- **[v2 new]** **The Curious Improviser (P1, primary).** A beginner at improvisation who wants to get to know musical ideas by playing them (vocabulary by exposure, G1) and to read and react in real time, gradually faster (real-time reading, G2). Between sessions the workspace still offers the deliberate loop of the first release: set a musical direction, read all four measures, regenerate the whole phrase when another idea is wanted. That loop serves G1 at the player's own pace. The continuous session serves G2 with mild pressure: phrases keep coming without being asked for, over a backing track, and the notes played right are quietly acknowledged.
- **[v2 new]** **The Speed Builder (P2).** Wants an endless supply of fresh material they cannot memorize, read at a steadily higher tempo (S1). The continuous session and the opt-in rising tempo serve them; nothing ever scores them.
- **[v2 new]** **The Fledgling Composer (P3).** Wants to look back at a session, mark what they liked (C1) and take it out to other tools (C2). The review at the end of a session and its export serve them: the export gives the generated notes or the notes actually played. Marking favorites, and growing an idea from something they played (C3), are beyond this version.

**[v2 new]** None of them is graded, ranked or shown a wrong note, anywhere, ever: the app acknowledges and encourages, and anything that adapts to the player is opt-in and visible.

The phrase is the primary object. Mobile and tablet support is landscape-only; desktop supports either orientation when sufficient width is available. The full staff receives the complete primary row, and controls remain beneath it without claiming score width.

## Context of Use

**[v2 new]** The conditions the design has to survive, as confirmed by the product owner and recorded in [`specs/personas.md`](../specs/personas.md#context-of-use). Each is followed by what it forces on the design.

- **Device.** Mainly a laptop or desktop running Chrome or Edge. A tablet on the music stand is a good fit; a landscape phone is used occasionally, outside with a wind controller, and accepted as a lesser experience. The supported browsers are the Chromium ones, because only they can reach a MIDI controller; a tablet therefore means a Chromium tablet, and an iPad falls outside this version's scope. → Desktop and tablet landscape are the layouts to optimize; the phone is the third display, and its layout must stay correct and legible but never dictates a choice that costs the larger screens. A landscape phone cannot hold the header, the toolbar, the session's cue row and both staff systems at once, so during a session it keeps what the session cannot do without: the cue row and both systems are entirely on screen, the header and toolbar may scroll out of view, and a compact Stop and Volume join the cue row, on the phone only. Because nothing can be scrolled with both hands on the instrument, the phone's staff is sized to its height as well as its width, so the cue row and both systems always fit the screen together, never below a readable size; the height used is the one with the browser's toolbars showing, so a toolbar hiding or reappearing never redraws the staff.
- **Distance.** The screen stands where a music stand would be, read from arm's length or further, often standing up. → Engraving size wins over interface chrome: the staff keeps the full width. The one thing off the staff the player must read mid-session, the disconnect countdown, has to be legible from the stand too; everything else off the staff is secondary (see the next point).
- **Hands and eyes during a session.** Both hands are on the instrument, and on a saxophone or wind controller the mouth too; the eyes stay on the staff, reading ahead. → Between its start and its end, nothing in a session may require a pointer or a key press. With a controller connected, the ends need none either: from the idle workspace (never while the review of the last session shows) the session starts when the player plays the note phrase 1 begins on, ends when they go silent, and recovers from an unplug when the controller is plugged back in; Stop stays on screen but is never needed. Without a controller, as with an acoustic saxophone, the session starts from Start and only Stop ends it: one action at each end, none in between. The staff and Stop are the only essential things on screen during a session, with one exception: the disconnect countdown, which must show the moment a controller is lost. Everything else (audio levels, tempo, other status) is secondary: glanceable and never needed. Everything that appears or moves on or near the staff belongs to the session itself: the rolling phrases and their read-ahead, the playhead, the count-in, the starting-note line, the acknowledgements on the notes, the session's own brief notices, and the disconnect countdown. Nothing else pops up or moves there.
- **Hands between sessions.** Free: the player sets controls and reviews with a pointer or a finger. → Controls need no enlargement for distance. The accessibility floor's minimum target size is enough, and is already right for touch on a tablet or phone.
- **Session length.** About 5 to 30 minutes, alone, usually at home; the phone outdoors is the exception. → Sessions are practice-length, not performance-length: the review at the end must stay readable for a half-hour session, and nothing is designed for an audience or a second person.
- **Sound.** An acoustic saxophone is loud in the room; a wind controller or keyboard plays through the same speakers or headphones as the backing. → The balance between backing, click and the player's own sound is a real problem, and the right levels are not known yet: they need listening tests before the audio controls' defaults and ranges are fixed.

## Mental Model

The player approaches the screen as a music stand with a compact set of musical directions beside it. Chord symbols sit above the staff where lead-sheet readers expect them; barlines make the four-bar form immediately countable; the score simply reads in the selected instrument profile's own written pitch, with no separate notation-display concept to surface next to it or hide in a settings area.

Controls are grouped by the musical decision they influence: foundation, rhythm, melodic shape, and character. They follow the score in a downward-scrolling section and reorient from a horizontal set of groups on wide screens to compact stacked groups on mobile landscape. Each presents named musical outcomes rather than unexplained numeric values. The character group is placed last in reading order but is always visible and never collapsible — it holds only Arpeggio chaos in first release, and a static group with one field needs no progressive-disclosure interaction.

## Visual Direction

(proposed) The workspace has the quiet authority of a marked-up music desk: warm off-white paper with graphite notation and a restrained brass action color in light mode; deep ink with parchment notation and muted brass in dark mode. The interface avoids decorative instrument imagery and gradients that could reduce staff contrast. Editorial display type gives the title personality; controls use a highly legible sans serif. The staff itself always uses a dedicated SMuFL notation font.

## Trade-offs

- A full-width horizontal staff is chosen over individual measure cards. It preserves phrase-level rhythm, harmonic movement, and the relationship between question and answer.
- A scrollable, below-score control section is chosen over a desktop side rail. The score needs the entire width for readable engraved detail, especially when viewed at a distance.
- Named segmented options are chosen over freeform knobs or continuous sliders. The generator has discrete first-release policy values, and labels make the consequences inspectable.
- Chord symbols transpose with the selected instrument profile's own written pitch. The underlying harmony remains concert-pitch data, but a displayed lead sheet must keep written notes, key, chord labels, and slash bass in the same written-pitch reading.
- Every instrument profile uses treble G2 clef. Only that profile's own fixed transposition changes what is written; it never changes the concert phrase or requires a separate notation-display selection.
- **[v2 new]** During a session the staff area shows two systems, one above the other: the player reads one while the other is refilled with what comes next, top then bottom then top, like reading down a page. Each system holds four measures on a tablet or larger and two on a phone, where a phrase is always split over the two systems, between sessions as during one, so the layout a session keeps never depends on what one phrase happens to contain and is already on screen when the player presses Start: Start never moves a note. The phone therefore no longer shows a short phrase on one line between sessions, a cost accepted because it is the third display. This is chosen over replacing a single system in place, which leaves nothing to read ahead, and over a scrolling strip or lines that move up, which move the music under the player's eyes, the hardest thing to follow from a music stand. Only the system not being read ever changes, so nothing scrolls or shifts; the system being played is marked by the playhead alone, and the other is never dimmed, since it is what the player reads ahead into.
- **[v2 new]** Where the personas pull in different directions, the Curious Improviser's experience is the default, and the others are served inside it:
  - **Progress and pressure.** The Speed Builder pulls toward tempo tracking and a visible measure of progress. The Improviser's mild, never-scored pressure wins: a rising tempo exists only as an opt-in, visibly announced, and no number is ever presented as a result to beat.
  - **Review and flow.** The Fledgling Composer needs a review at the end of each session; the Improviser and the Speed Builder want to get straight back to playing. The review appears between sessions only, never interrupts one, and starting the next session is one action away from it, with no confirmation: the next session's review simply replaces it. The player leaves the review before a session can start: while it shows, it takes the place of the staff and the controls, the button that would start a session reads `Close review` instead, and no note starts anything, so a note played while looking back can never throw the review away. Leaving keeps it, reachable from a button above the staff, until the next session starts. The way out and the way back in are one press each, the same key twice for a keyboard player.
  - **Reading the phrase and seeing feedback.** Acknowledging the notes played right serves all three, but must never pull the eyes off the staff for the Improviser and the Speed Builder, who are reading ahead. Acknowledgement is quiet and sits on the notes themselves; a note that misses produces nothing.
  - **Phone and larger screens.** The phone is a real but occasional device, used for playing rather than for looking back. Where a layout choice would help the phone at the cost of the desk or stand screen, the larger screen wins. The phone's review keeps the review's functions but carries the least information and annotation that space allows, and gains no tooling of its own; any richer review tooling belongs to the tablet and desktop layouts. A player who wants a real look back at a phone session exports it and continues on a larger screen. The phone's review is the minimum: the phrases (and, once they are shown, the notes played) and the export, nothing else. On larger screens the review has no side tools yet; tools that act on the session or on one phrase will come later, inside the review, never in a side rail beside the staff.

## Engraving Policy

The renderer must engrave a semantic event timeline, not approximate notes with CSS or arbitrary vertical offsets. It must calculate pitch placement from the active clef and the selected instrument profile's own fixed transposition; choose a key signature from the player's written-key selection; calculate accidentals from the displayed key and local measure state; and render duration, rests, dots, beams, ties, tuplets if later supported, barlines, and the 4/4 signature according to conventional engraving rules.

Each displayed measure has a visible chord symbol above its first harmonic event. A harmony change within a measure receives its own correctly aligned chord symbol. The system validates that every measure occupies exactly four beats before it becomes visible. A rejected or incomplete result replaces no existing phrase.

## Edge-Case Policy

- **Initial empty:** Explain that a four-measure idea will appear here. The workspace-header generation action remains the single primary action; keep the control sheet usable.
- **[v2 changed]** **Generating:** Disable the phrase-generation action, and `Start session` with it until the new phrase lands, since a session must never start on a phrase about to be replaced; retain controls as read-only context; preserve the previous phrase until a complete new result is ready; and announce generation status non-visually. Some generations have no phrase to preserve and show no score while they run: the new phrase that follows the no-room notice, and the replacement for a phrase a window change left too wide. A round of attempts under the too-many-notes notice keeps that notice in view instead.
- **Generation failure:** Keep the previous valid phrase visible. Put a concise error message beside the regeneration action and offer retry; do not show a partial staff.
- **Notation failure:** Preserve the generated phrase data but replace the score with an error panel. The workspace-header action changes to retry notation; never render a misleading fallback of letter names as the score.
- **[v2 changed]** The notation error covers only a failure that trying again can cure. A lack of room for the staff is never shown as a notation error; it has its own policy below.
- **[v2 changed]** **No room for the staff:** when the window is too narrow for the staff layout at a readable size, on any screen, or, on a phone, too short for it, the score is replaced by one plain sentence with no action, naming a cure only where the player has one: "The staff needs more room. Make the window larger." on a tablet or larger, and "This screen is too small to show the staff." on a phone, whose screen cannot be made larger. The choice follows the same width test as the staff layout, so a desktop window narrowed to a phone's width, a rare case, gets the phone's sentence. The rest of the workspace stays usable, and the sentence goes away by itself once the window gives the staff room, making way for a new phrase, never the one it replaced. No session starts while it shows. Because a phrase's width depends on its notes, a phrase too wide for the room between sessions is quietly replaced with fresh attempts inside the ordinary loading, and the notice shows only when every attempt is too wide; fewer notes are then a second cure, so its sentence there names both, on every screen: "These notes need more room. Make the window larger, or choose a lower note density." That notice clears only when a phrase fits, so anything that could cure it (a wider window, a change to a setting the notes are generated from, `Regenerate phrase`) tries again at once, through the ordinary loading, and it stays if every attempt is still too wide; narrowing the window never tries. Either notice, or a phrase on screen that stops fitting, gives way to the empty staff and the rhythm alert instead when the rhythm settings cannot form a phrase at that moment, and attempts already running stop there; nothing is then generated until the player asks. On a phone, the busiest note density, the one most likely to overflow, is unavailable, with a short reason beside it; the rule follows the current screen size, and a player's stored choice is kept for larger screens.
- **Co-active generation and notation failure:** When an out-of-band notation redraw on the phrase retained after an earlier generation failure itself fails, keep both indicators visible — the generation-failure message above the score, and the notation-error panel replacing the score itself — rather than dropping either, even though the one action that can address either of them sits separately in the workspace header. The single workspace-header action goes to the notation failure, since it is the narrower, phrase-preserving fix and does not depend on current control values the way a fresh generation attempt does. Resolving the notation failure restores the generation-failure message's own retry action automatically; it never clears or re-validates the generation-failure message itself.
- **Instrument-profile switch:** Never validate the outgoing phrase against the new profile's range. With a phrase shown, always take a fresh seed, keep every other control, and regenerate a complete replacement immediately under the new profile — unless the current rhythm-control combination is already unsupported, in which case the switch still applies but clears the phrase to Empty instead of regenerating. With no phrase shown, the switch only changes the selection. Never transpose source events to fit.
- **[v2 changed]** **Optional MIDI:** connecting a controller is always optional and never blocking — a session is always startable with no controller connected (`flows/controller-connection/`), and the ordinary connection states (unsupported browser, no device found, no controller) are quiet, neutral statuses, never error-colored ones: absence of a controller is a normal, fully supported way to use the app, not a fault. Two states are the exception and use the error tone, always with words so that color is never the only signal: MIDI access blocked by the player's own browser choice, which the app cannot undo and the player must change in the browser; and a controller unplugged mid-session, which the player must see at once because the session ends if it is not back within 30 seconds. A controller that simply falls silent is not an unplug and shows nothing at all. A controller connects only between sessions, never during one. A note that fails to match the expected pitch produces no visible change of any kind — the same "no mismatch hint, ever" principle carried through from v1 — and the played note itself is never rendered.
- **[v2 new]** **Generation failure before a session:** no session starts while a generation-failure message is showing, even over a phrase still on screen, whether by Start or by the starting note; the retry is the primary action, and the player regenerates successfully first. So an error message is never on screen when a session starts, and a session start never removes one and shifts the staff.
- **[v2 new]** **Generation failure during a session:** never a blocking error and never a reason to stop. The app retries silently, counting a phrase too wide for the session's staff layout as one more failed try, and, past its deadline, repeats the previous phrase, unmarked; only a run of repeats earns a small, neutral notice, never error-toned. The idle workspace's generation-failure banner and notation error panel belong between sessions only.
- **[v2 new]** **Interruptions end a session, never pause it:** a hidden tab, a window that can no longer show the staff, a silence of more than 30 seconds with a controller attached, or an unplugged controller not back in time ends the session exactly as Stop does, and the review opens. There is no pause state. A session ended during its count-in, before phrase 1 has sounded, has nothing to review: the workspace returns to idle with phrase 1 still shown, and no review opens. The same rule covers any problem not listed here: a session never asks the player to act while it runs. A problem that can be put right, as an unplugged controller is by plugging it back in, gets the 30-second window with its countdown on screen; anything else ends the session at once, as Stop does.
- **[v2 new]** **Orientation and window size during a session:** a session asks the browser to hold the screen in landscape from its start to its end, and says nothing if refused. Beyond that, one rule: a session ends, as above, when the staff can no longer be shown, that is when the window becomes portrait-shaped on anything smaller than a desktop screen (a turned device, a split screen, a resized window alike), where the workspace would give way to the rotate-device message, or too narrow for the session's staff layout at a readable size, or, on a phone, too short for it. The same test covers material already prepared and waiting for its turn on screen: if any of it no longer fits, the session ends too, so a refill never has to bring in something that cannot be drawn, and a session never shows the notation error. A browser toolbar hiding or reappearing is not such a change, since the phone's staff is fitted to the height with the toolbars showing. Any other change of window shape or size redraws the staff at once at the new size, keeping the staff layout the session started with, so the next phrase never moves while the player reads toward it. That redraw is the one change a session makes to the system being read, and it is allowed because the player made it with their own hands. No session starts while the staff is not shown, under the rotate-device message or while the staff has no room, whether the window is too narrow or, on a phone, too short: by this rule it would end the moment it began. A session that ends this way before its first phrase has sounded, in a window still too small at idle, returns to the no-room sentence, never to the notation error.
- **[v2 new]** **Screen kept awake:** the screen is kept awake for the length of a session. If the browser refuses or cannot, the session runs normally and nothing is shown: a message the player cannot act on mid-session is not worth pulling the eyes off the staff.
- **[v2 new]** **The review:** it opens only after a session in which at least one phrase sounded, and it always holds at least one phrase, so it has no empty state. No session starts while it shows. Out of sight, the staff gets a fresh phrase for the next session through the ordinary loading and its ordinary outcomes, which the player finds on leaving the review. A long review draws its phrases only as they come near the screen, in space set aside for them, so it never jumps and has no loading indicator. When the window has no room for the staff, the review's phrases give way to the same plain sentence, and its export keeps working; a single phrase too wide for the window says so in its own place, since a review is a record and cannot replace a phrase. A failed export shows one plain line under the export and the button tries again. The review, like everything about a session, lives in memory only: it is replaced by the next session and lost when the page closes, and it says so, except on the phone, where the review is kept to the phrases and the export.
- **Offline:** Required behavior remains available because generation and notation are browser-local. If a browser capability is unavailable, explain the affected optional capability without blocking phrase generation.

## Flow Index

| Flow                               | Goal                                                                                                                                                                    | Design                         |
| ---------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| **[v2 changed]** Generate phrase   | Set musical character and explicitly create or replace one complete phrase; between sessions, the app also replaces a phrase by itself when it does not fit the window. | `flows/generate-phrase/`       |
| **[v2 new]** Controller connection | Connect a MIDI controller, resolve which device to use, and confirm it agrees with the instrument template's written pitch before a session relies on it.               | `flows/controller-connection/` |
| **[v2 new]** Play session          | Play continuously from the phrase on screen, with new phrases arriving on their own, until the session ends and the review opens.                                       | `flows/play-session/`          |
| **[v2 new]** Review and export     | Look back at every phrase the last session played, export its generated notes, and leave the review for a fresh phrase to start the next session from.                  | `flows/review-and-export/`     |
