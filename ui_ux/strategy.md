## Problem Alignment

Infinite Flow is a practice partner for musicians who can already read and play, and who want to get better by playing generated material rather than by studying it. The personas are defined in [`specs/personas.md`](../specs/personas.md); this interface serves them in this order:

- **The Curious Improviser (P1, primary).** A beginner at improvisation who wants to get to know musical ideas by playing them (vocabulary by exposure, G1) and to read and react in real time, gradually faster (real-time reading, G2). Between sessions the workspace still offers the deliberate loop of the first release: set a musical direction, read all four measures, regenerate the whole phrase when another idea is wanted. That loop serves G1 at the player's own pace. The continuous session serves G2 with mild pressure: phrases keep coming without being asked for, over a backing track, and the notes played right are quietly acknowledged.
- **The Speed Builder (P2).** Wants an endless supply of fresh material they cannot memorize, read at a steadily higher tempo (S1). The continuous session and the opt-in rising tempo serve them; nothing ever scores them.
- **[v3 changed]** **The Fledgling Composer (P3).** Wants to look back at a session, mark what they liked (C1) and take it out to other tools (C2). The review serves them, now for every session they chose to keep and not only the last one: they mark whole phrases as favorites, write a comment on a session, and export the whole session or the favorites only, as the generated notes or the notes actually played.
- **[v3.2 new]** **Growing an idea (P3.C3).** The Fledgling Composer plays a short idea, a doodle, at the start of a Jam, and the app's phrases continue it: a rhythm close to it, the same ups and downs, phrase 1 joined to its last note. The review opens with the doodle as the app heard it, can suggest the key and mode it sounds in, and offers to jam again on the same doodle, so the player can go round as many times as they like without having to play it again.
- **[v3.1 new]** **More to hear, chosen in one step (P1.G1, P1.G2, P2.S1).** Embellishment figures, each with a level, let the Curious Improviser actually hear passing tones, enclosures and chromatic approaches instead of almost never, and add them one at a time from plain lines. Presets give a whole setup in one choice: a gentle warm-up, a speed drill for the Speed Builder, or a style with the mode or chord plan it needs; a player keeps what they found under a name of their own.
- **[v3.2 new]** **One rhythm, or chosen variety (P1.G2, P2.S1).** The rhythm lock lets either of them keep one rhythm so the eyes can go to the notes (P1.G2), and choosing what Vary settings may move gives the Speed Builder variety they steer (P2.S1).
- **[v3.3 new]** **Richer chords, by choice (P1.G1, P1.G2).** The Curious Improviser can let the chords take a seventh, the everyday chords of jazz, and hear it in the backing, see it in the chord symbols and land on it in the melody, without studying theory: one control, off until they raise it. Reading the longer symbols at speed serves real-time reading too (P1.G2); the Speed Builder (P2.S1) and the Fledgling Composer growing an idea in a Jam (P3.C3) get the same richer chords if they raise the level.
- **[v3 new]** **What keeping things serves for the other two.** The Curious Improviser and the Speed Builder find their settings as they left them, so the next practice sitting starts where the last one ended (G1), and a player whose instrument matches none of the three built-in ones describes it once, so the written part matches what they actually play (G2).

None of them is graded, ranked or shown a wrong note, anywhere, ever: the app acknowledges and encourages, and anything that adapts to the player is opt-in and visible.

The phrase is the primary object. Mobile and tablet support is landscape-only; desktop supports either orientation when sufficient width is available. The full staff receives the complete primary row, and controls remain beneath it without claiming score width.

## Context of Use

The conditions the design has to survive, as confirmed by the product owner and recorded in [`specs/personas.md`](../specs/personas.md#context-of-use). Each is followed by what it forces on the design.

- **Device.** Mainly a laptop or desktop running Chrome or Edge. A tablet on the music stand is a good fit; a landscape phone is used occasionally, outside with a wind controller, and accepted as a lesser experience. The supported browsers are the Chromium ones, because only they can reach a MIDI controller; a tablet therefore means a Chromium tablet, and an iPad falls outside this version's scope. → Desktop and tablet landscape are the layouts to optimize; the phone is the third display, and its layout must stay correct and legible but never dictates a choice that costs the larger screens. A landscape phone cannot hold the header, the toolbar, the session's cue row and both staff systems at once, so during a session it keeps what the session cannot do without: the cue row and both systems are entirely on screen, the header and toolbar may scroll out of view, and a compact Stop and Volume join the cue row, on the phone only. Because nothing can be scrolled with both hands on the instrument, the phone's staff is sized to its height as well as its width, so the cue row and both systems always fit the screen together, never below a readable size; the height used is the one with the browser's toolbars showing, so a toolbar hiding or reappearing never redraws the staff.
- **Distance.** The screen stands where a music stand would be, read from arm's length or further, often standing up. → Engraving size wins over interface chrome: the staff keeps the full width. The one thing off the staff the player must read mid-session, the disconnect countdown, has to be legible from the stand too; everything else off the staff is secondary (see the next point).
- **Hands and eyes during a session.** Both hands are on the instrument, and on a saxophone or wind controller the mouth too; the eyes stay on the staff, reading ahead. → Between its start and its end, nothing in a session may require a pointer or a key press. With a controller connected, the ends need none either: from the idle workspace (never while the review of the last session shows) the session starts when the player plays the note phrase 1 begins on, ends when they go silent, and recovers from an unplug when the controller is plugged back in; Stop stays on screen but is never needed. Without a controller, as with an acoustic saxophone, the session starts from Start and only Stop ends it: one action at each end, none in between. The staff and Stop are the only essential things on screen during a session, with one exception: the disconnect countdown, which must show the moment a controller is lost. Everything else (audio levels, tempo, other status) is secondary: glanceable and never needed. Everything that appears or moves on or near the staff belongs to the session itself: the rolling phrases and their read-ahead, the playhead, the count-in, the starting-note line, the acknowledgements on the notes, the session's own brief notices, and the disconnect countdown. Nothing else pops up or moves there.
- **Hands between sessions.** Free: the player sets controls and reviews with a pointer or a finger. → Controls need no enlargement for distance. The accessibility floor's minimum target size is enough, and is already right for touch on a tablet or phone.
- **Session length.** About 5 to 30 minutes, alone, usually at home; the phone outdoors is the exception. → Sessions are practice-length, not performance-length: the review at the end must stay readable for a half-hour session, and nothing is designed for an audience or a second person.
- **Sound.** An acoustic saxophone is loud in the room; a wind controller or keyboard plays through the same speakers or headphones as the backing. → The balance between backing, click and the player's own sound is a real problem, so both levels stay within reach in the audio mix, and their ranges and default levels were set by ear in real sessions.
- **[v3 new]** **Across visits.** The same player comes back to the same browser, practice sitting after practice sitting, usually on the same device. Everything the app keeps stays on that device, in that browser profile, and is there for whoever opens the app in it (master spec §15: still one local user, no accounts). → Keeping is silent: settings, the last session and the controller simply come back, with no "saved" message and no sign-in. What is kept is shown in one place, with deletion everywhere and one way to delete everything. The player's own played notes, the most private thing the app holds, are kept only when the player asks.
- **[v3.2 new]** **Playing an idea to the app.** A doodle is played on the controller, so Jam needs one, and it is played with both hands (and the mouth) busy like the rest of a session. → A Jam starts by playing the home note as well as by Start, the doodle ends by itself when a measure stays silent or after four measures, and nothing is asked or offered while a Jam runs: the key and mode the doodle sounds in is suggested only afterwards, in the review, where the hands are free. Only the doodle's quantized form, a generation input the player gave on purpose, travels with the music; the notes as played stay played notes, private.

## Mental Model

The player approaches the screen as a music stand with a compact set of musical directions beside it. Chord symbols sit above the staff where lead-sheet readers expect them; barlines make the four-bar form immediately countable; the score simply reads in the selected instrument profile's own written pitch, with no separate notation-display concept to surface next to it or hide in a settings area.

Controls are grouped by the musical decision they influence: foundation, rhythm, melodic shape, and character. They follow the score in a downward-scrolling section and reorient from a horizontal set of groups on wide screens to compact stacked groups on mobile landscape. Each presents named musical outcomes rather than unexplained numeric values. The character group is placed last in reading order but is always visible and never collapsible — it holds only Arpeggio chaos in first release, and a static group with one field needs no progressive-disclosure interaction.

**[v3.1 new]** The controls open with a preset, a named starting point, before any of the musical groups: a player picks where to start, then fine-tunes. Changing a setting the preset holds turns the selection to Custom, which still names the preset it came from, so the player always knows what they started from and that saving would keep it. After the four musical groups comes an Embellishments group: one control per decorating figure, each saying in plain words what the figure is and what rhythm it needs, and how often the app should use it. The options that act only once a session runs stay last, together.

**[v3.3 new]** The foundation group gains one setting after Harmonic motion: Seventh chords, how often a chord takes one more note stacked on top. The two settings that make the chords read together, where a player looking at a chord symbol over the staff would look for its cause.

## Visual Direction

(proposed) The workspace has the quiet authority of a marked-up music desk: warm off-white paper with graphite notation and a restrained brass action color in light mode; deep ink with parchment notation and muted brass in dark mode. The interface avoids decorative instrument imagery and gradients that could reduce staff contrast. Editorial display type gives the title personality; controls use a highly legible sans serif. The staff itself always uses a dedicated SMuFL notation font.

## Trade-offs

- A full-width horizontal staff is chosen over individual measure cards. It preserves phrase-level rhythm, harmonic movement, and the relationship between question and answer.
- A scrollable, below-score control section is chosen over a desktop side rail. The score needs the entire width for readable engraved detail, especially when viewed at a distance.
- Named segmented options are chosen over freeform knobs or continuous sliders. The generator has discrete first-release policy values, and labels make the consequences inspectable.
- Chord symbols transpose with the selected instrument profile's own written pitch. The underlying harmony remains concert-pitch data, but a displayed lead sheet must keep written notes, key, chord labels, and slash bass in the same written-pitch reading.
- **[v3 changed]** The built-in instrument profiles use treble G2 clef; my instrument uses treble G2 or bass F4 clef, as its player chose, offered because a low instrument read in treble clef would hang on a ladder of ledger lines and make the staff tallest. The clef is display only: it changes no written note and nothing generated, and each phrase keeps the clef it was made with. Only the instrument's own transposition changes what is written; it never changes the concert phrase or requires a separate notation-display selection.
- During a session the staff area shows two systems, one above the other: the player reads one while the other is refilled with what comes next, top then bottom then top, like reading down a page. Each system holds four measures on a tablet or larger and two on a phone, where a phrase is always split over the two systems, between sessions as during one, so the layout a session keeps never depends on what one phrase happens to contain and is already on screen when the player presses Start: Start never moves a note. The phone therefore no longer shows a short phrase on one line between sessions, a cost accepted because it is the third display. This is chosen over replacing a single system in place, which leaves nothing to read ahead, and over a scrolling strip or lines that move up, which move the music under the player's eyes, the hardest thing to follow from a music stand. Only the system not being read ever changes, so nothing scrolls or shifts; the system being played is marked by the playhead alone, and the other is never dimmed, since it is what the player reads ahead into.
- Where the personas pull in different directions, the Curious Improviser's experience is the default, and the others are served inside it:
  - **Progress and pressure.** The Speed Builder pulls toward tempo tracking and a visible measure of progress. The Improviser's mild, never-scored pressure wins: a rising tempo exists only as an opt-in, switched on between sessions where the other session options sit, and no number is ever presented as a result to beat. During the session the rise shows only as the tempo itself; afterwards the tempo control goes back to where the player started, and one quiet line beside it states the tempo the last session reached, in the past tense, with no comparison and no target.
  - **[v3 changed]** **Review and flow.** The Fledgling Composer needs a review at the end of each session; the Improviser and the Speed Builder want to get straight back to playing. The review appears between sessions only, never interrupts one, and starting the next session is one action away from it, with no confirmation: the next session's review simply replaces it. The player leaves the review before a session can start: while it shows, it takes the staff's place, the audio mix, tempo and settings staying usable, the button that would start a session reads `Close review` instead, and no note starts a session, so a note played while looking back can never throw the review away. The way out and the way back in are one press each, the same key twice for a keyboard player.
  - **[v3 new]** **Keeping and starting again.** Leaving the review keeps it, reachable from a button above the staff, and from v3.0 it survives a reload too. The next session replaces it unless the player pinned it, and starting that next session still never asks anything: favorites pin their session, so nothing the player marked can be lost by playing on, and an "are you sure?" before Start would tax every session for the rare one worth keeping.
  - **Reading the phrase and seeing feedback.** Acknowledging the notes played right serves all three, but must never pull the eyes off the staff for the Improviser and the Speed Builder, who are reading ahead. Acknowledgement is quiet and sits on the notes themselves; a note that misses produces nothing.
  - **[v3 changed]** **Phone and larger screens.** The phone is a real but occasional device, used for playing rather than for looking back. Where a layout choice would help the phone at the cost of the desk or stand screen, the larger screen wins. The phone's review keeps the review's functions but carries the least information and annotation that space allows, and gains no tooling of its own; any richer review tooling belongs to the tablet and desktop layouts. A player who wants a real look back at a phone session exports it and continues on a larger screen. On larger screens the review has no side tools; tools that act on the session or on one phrase sit inside the review, never in a side rail beside the staff.
  - **[v3 new]** **The phone's review.** The phone keeps every function the review has on any device and nothing more: the phrases, the notes played (on request), the export with its scope, favorites, the comment, pinning and deleting, and the list of kept sessions. It drops the summary line and the line saying how the session is kept, which the pin button's own label already says, except when the session could not be saved, and shows the favorite star without its word.
- **[v3 new]** **Kept sessions live inside the review.** The review gains a second level, the list of every kept session, drawn in the same place and left by the same `Close review`. A separate page was rejected: it would add a top-level place for something the Improviser and the Speed Builder rarely visit, and a second way in and out of "not playing". A modal list was rejected too: a long list scrolls badly inside a dialog, and the review it opens is not a dialog.
- **[v3 new]** **The music is kept by itself; the player's own notes only on request.** The Fledgling Composer wants their material kept; every persona's played notes are private. The last session is kept with no question asked, and a pinned session for good, but the notes the player played are kept only once they switch on "Keep my played notes", and can be deleted, one session's or all, without touching the sessions. The switch sits where the played notes are seen, in the review, as well as with the rest of the kept data.
- **[v3 new]** **A favorite is a whole phrase, marked in one press.** The star sits at the end of each phrase's number-and-tempo line, the place the review kept for per-phrase tools. Marking a range of measures was left out of this version (master spec §2), and a whole phrase is what the export's phrase numbers already name. The first favorite pins the session in place, with no message: the pin button's label changes beside it.
- **[v3 new]** **"Your data" is a sheet behind a footer link.** It is visited rarely, so it takes no room in the header, whose widths are fixed and full: a one-line footer under the controls says the data stays on this device and opens it, and the session list opens it too. A sheet suits it: it is a short, self-contained task the player finishes and closes.
- **[v3 new]** **My instrument is a fourth instrument choice, made in a sheet.** The three built-in profiles stay as they are; a fourth segment appears once the player has made their own, named as they named it. Making it is a short form opened from a button under the instrument control, starting from one of the three as a template, so the player only adjusts what differs. The range is entered as notes in written pitch, the way a player knows their instrument, a semitone per step, on the treble or bass staff the player reads, and the transposition as how a written note sounds, counted in semitones; its interval name is shown beside the count as the result ("a major ninth lower"), never chosen from a list a beginner would have to translate. Every kept session belongs to the instrument it was played with: a change to my instrument's range or transposition, or deleting it, erases its sessions after a warning naming how many, rather than leaving reviews of music the instrument no longer plays; a name or clef change keeps them.

- **[v3.1 new]** **The preset is a dropdown at the top of the controls.** The owner's direction. Eight built-in presets and up to fifty of the player's own fit no row of buttons, and a native dropdown is the most familiar list there is, by mouse, finger or keyboard. Its list is split into practice presets, styles and the player's own, so a built-in preset is always told from one the player can change. Rejected: keeping the old two-button choice inside the foundation group, where a starting point sat among the settings it overrides.
- **[v3.1 new]** **A preset's key and mode are suggested, never imposed.** A style preset wants its mode, while the player's key is often chosen for their instrument or piece. Choosing the preset moves the key or mode only as far as it must; afterwards the key and mode controls mark, with a dot and a sentence, the values the preset is meant for, and every value stays choosable. Choosing outside simply turns the selection to Custom. Rejected: disabling the other values (it would forbid what the player may well want), and colour alone (unreadable for some, and invisible on the selected value).
- **[v3.1 new]** **Four plain levels per figure: Off, Rarely, Often, Very often.** The working names in the spec ("As today", "Favored") describe the generator; a beginner needs to know how often they will hear the figure. "Rarely" is honest about what the old behaviour gave. A level is a preference, never a promise, and the group says so, so a figure that the rhythm leaves no room for is not a broken promise.
- **[v3.2 new]** **Jam is a session option, and Start says so.** A Jam is a way of starting a session, chosen before it like the other session options, and it sits first among them. While it is on, the session button reads `Start Jam` and the empty staff explains what to do, so the mode is visible where the player acts and looks. A second start button was rejected: two starts would compete for one played note.
- **[v3.2 new]** **Options that only matter when a switch is on appear under it.** Drift under Jam, the allowed rhythm changes under the rhythm lock, and what may vary under Vary settings appear in the same column only while their switch is on, and keep their values while hidden. A beginner sees one switch until they choose it.
- **[v3.2 new]** **What the app does to the rhythm by itself is never marked.** A rhythm change the player allowed, or a forced bend that keeps a phrase possible under the lock, is played and read as written, the way a repeated phrase or an automatic setting change has never been marked: a mark on the staff would pull the eyes off the notes, for something the player cannot act on mid-session.
- **[v3.3 new]** **One level for seventh chords, beside the harmony, in plain words.** A level, not a switch, because styles differ in how often they use sevenths (now and then in pop, on nearly every chord in jazz); one level for the family, not one per kind of seventh, because in a key and mode each kind sits on fixed chords, so a level per kind would mostly choose chords. It sits beside Harmonic motion rather than among the embellishment figures, which decorate the melody, and shares their four-segment look with labels for what the player hears: Off, Sometimes, Often, Mostly. Every built-in preset keeps it off, so the presets sound as they did; Jazz, Bebop and Modal (Dorian) suggest raising it in their description. Vary settings never moves it: seventh chords are only ever the player's choice.

## Engraving Policy

The renderer must engrave a semantic event timeline, not approximate notes with CSS or arbitrary vertical offsets. It must calculate pitch placement from the active clef and the selected instrument profile's own fixed transposition; choose a key signature from the player's written-key selection; calculate accidentals from the displayed key and local measure state; and render duration, rests, dots, beams, ties, tuplets if later supported, barlines, and the 4/4 signature according to conventional engraving rules.

Each displayed measure has a visible chord symbol above its first harmonic event. A harmony change within a measure receives its own correctly aligned chord symbol. The system validates that every measure occupies exactly four beats before it becomes visible. A rejected or incomplete result replaces no existing phrase.

**[v3.3 new]** Seventh chords make longer symbols (`Cmaj7`, `Dm7`, `G7`, `Bm7♭5`). They are written in letters, as the triads' `m` and `dim` are, on one line at the one symbol size, transposed like every symbol. The longest must fit twice in a measure at every supported width, with room between; a symbol is never shrunk, shortened or allowed to overlap, and the engraving calibration checks the case. The spoken description names each chord in words.

## Edge-Case Policy

- **Initial empty:** Explain that a four-measure idea will appear here. The workspace-header generation action remains the single primary action; keep the control sheet usable.
- **Generating:** Disable the phrase-generation action, and `Start session` with it until the new phrase lands, since a session must never start on a phrase about to be replaced; retain controls as read-only context; preserve the previous phrase until a complete new result is ready; and announce generation status non-visually. Some generations have no phrase to preserve and show no score while they run: the new phrase that follows the no-room notice, and the replacement for a phrase a window change left too wide. A round of attempts under the too-many-notes notice keeps that notice in view instead.
- **Generation failure:** Keep the previous valid phrase visible. Put a concise error message beside the regeneration action and offer retry; do not show a partial staff.
- **Notation failure:** Preserve the generated phrase data but replace the score with an error panel. The workspace-header action changes to retry notation; never render a misleading fallback of letter names as the score.
- The notation error covers only a failure that trying again can cure. A lack of room for the staff is never shown as a notation error; it has its own policy below.
- **No room for the staff:** when the window is too narrow for the staff layout at a readable size, on any screen, or, on a phone, too short for it, the score is replaced by one plain sentence with no action, naming a cure only where the player has one: "The staff needs more room. Make the window larger." on a tablet or larger, and "This screen is too small to show the staff." on a phone, whose screen cannot be made larger. The choice follows the same width test as the staff layout, so a desktop window narrowed to a phone's width, a rare case, gets the phone's sentence. The rest of the workspace stays usable, and the sentence goes away by itself once the window gives the staff room, making way for a new phrase, never the one it replaced. No session starts while it shows. Because a phrase's width depends on its notes, a phrase too wide for the room between sessions is quietly replaced with fresh attempts inside the ordinary loading, and the notice shows only when every attempt is too wide; fewer notes are then a second cure, so its sentence there names both, on every screen: "These notes need more room. Make the window larger, or choose a lower note density." That notice clears only when a phrase fits, so anything that could cure it (a wider window, a change to a setting the notes are generated from, `Regenerate phrase`) tries again at once, through the ordinary loading, and it stays if every attempt is still too wide; narrowing the window never tries. Either notice, or a phrase on screen that stops fitting, gives way to the empty staff and the rhythm alert instead when the rhythm settings cannot form a phrase at that moment, and attempts already running stop there; nothing is then generated until the player asks. On a phone, the busiest note density, the one most likely to overflow, is unavailable, with a short reason beside it; the rule follows the current screen size, and a player's stored choice is kept for larger screens.
- **Co-active generation and notation failure:** When an out-of-band notation redraw on the phrase retained after an earlier generation failure itself fails, keep both indicators visible — the generation-failure message above the score, and the notation-error panel replacing the score itself — rather than dropping either, even though the one action that can address either of them sits separately in the workspace header. The single workspace-header action goes to the notation failure, since it is the narrower, phrase-preserving fix and does not depend on current control values the way a fresh generation attempt does. Resolving the notation failure restores the generation-failure message's own retry action automatically; it never clears or re-validates the generation-failure message itself.
- **Instrument-profile switch:** Never validate the outgoing phrase against the new profile's range. With a phrase shown, always take a fresh seed, keep every other control, and regenerate a complete replacement immediately under the new profile — unless the current rhythm-control combination is already unsupported, in which case the switch still applies but clears the phrase to Empty instead of regenerating. With no phrase shown, the switch only changes the selection. Never transpose source events to fit.
- **Optional MIDI:** connecting a controller is always optional and never blocking — a session is always startable with no controller connected (`flows/controller-connection/`), and the ordinary connection states (unsupported browser, no device found, no controller) are quiet, neutral statuses, never error-colored ones: absence of a controller is a normal, fully supported way to use the app, not a fault. Two states are the exception and use the error tone, always with words so that color is never the only signal: MIDI access blocked by the player's own browser choice, which the app cannot undo and the player must change in the browser; and a controller unplugged mid-session, which the player must see at once because the session ends if it is not back within 30 seconds. A controller that simply falls silent is not an unplug and shows nothing at all. A controller connects only between sessions, never during one. A note that fails to match the expected pitch produces no visible change of any kind — the same "no mismatch hint, ever" principle carried through from v1 — and the played note itself is never drawn on the staff during a session; afterwards it can appear only in the review, on request, in a lane of its own (see Played notes in the review).
- **[v3 new]** **The remembered controller:** the controller the player last chose is reselected at load as soon as it is plugged in, with no click and no check, but only when the browser already allows MIDI access: a load never raises a browser prompt.
- **Generation failure before a session:** no session starts while a generation-failure message is showing, even over a phrase still on screen, whether by Start or by the starting note; the retry is the primary action, and the player regenerates successfully first. So an error message is never on screen when a session starts, and a session start never removes one and shifts the staff.
- **Generation failure during a session:** never a blocking error and never a reason to stop. The app retries silently, counting a phrase too wide for the session's staff layout as one more failed try, and, past its deadline, repeats the previous phrase, unmarked; only a run of repeats earns a small, neutral notice, never error-toned. The idle workspace's generation-failure banner and notation error panel belong between sessions only.
- **Interruptions end a session, never pause it:** a hidden tab, a window that can no longer show the staff, a silence of more than 30 seconds with a controller attached, or an unplugged controller not back in time ends the session exactly as Stop does, and the review opens. There is no pause state. A session ended during its count-in, before phrase 1 has sounded, has nothing to review: the workspace returns to idle with phrase 1 still shown, and no review opens. The same rule covers any problem not listed here: a session never asks the player to act while it runs. A problem that can be put right, as an unplugged controller is by plugging it back in, gets the 30-second window with its countdown on screen; anything else ends the session at once, as Stop does.
- **Orientation and window size during a session:** a session asks the browser to hold the screen in landscape from its start to its end, and says nothing if refused. Beyond that, one rule: a session ends, as above, when the staff can no longer be shown, that is when the window becomes portrait-shaped on anything smaller than a desktop screen (a turned device, a split screen, a resized window alike), where the workspace would give way to the rotate-device message, or too narrow for the session's staff layout at a readable size, or, on a phone, too short for it. The same test covers material already prepared and waiting for its turn on screen: if any of it no longer fits, the session ends too, so a refill never has to bring in something that cannot be drawn, and a session never shows the notation error. A browser toolbar hiding or reappearing is not such a change, since the phone's staff is fitted to the height with the toolbars showing. Any other change of window shape or size redraws the staff at once at the new size, keeping the staff layout the session started with, so the next phrase never moves while the player reads toward it. That redraw is the one change a session makes to the system being read, and it is allowed because the player made it with their own hands. No session starts while the staff is not shown, under the rotate-device message or while the staff has no room, whether the window is too narrow or, on a phone, too short: by this rule it would end the moment it began. A session that ends this way before its first phrase has sounded, in a window still too small at idle, returns to the no-room sentence, never to the notation error.
- **Screen kept awake:** the screen is kept awake for the length of a session. If the browser refuses or cannot, the session runs normally and nothing is shown: a message the player cannot act on mid-session is not worth pulling the eyes off the staff.
- **[v3 changed]** **The review:** it opens only after a session in which at least one phrase sounded, and it always holds at least one phrase, so it has no empty state. No session starts while it shows. Out of sight, the staff gets a fresh phrase for the next session through the ordinary loading and its ordinary outcomes, which the player finds on leaving the review. A long review draws its phrases only as they come near the screen, in space set aside for them, so it never jumps and has no loading indicator. A review never cuts a phrase: a session played in a clef my instrument no longer has gets the room its phrases need. When the window has no room for the staff, the review's phrases give way to the same plain sentence, and its export keeps working; a single phrase too wide for the window says so in its own place, since a review is a record and cannot replace a phrase. A failed export shows one plain line under the export and the button tries again.
- **[v3 new]** **Keeping the review:** a session is saved once, at its end, when its review opens; nothing is ever written during a session. The review says, in one line under its summary, how the session is kept: pinned, or deleted when the next session is saved, or, when its save failed, not saved and best exported. That line is where a failed session save is told, since the player is already looking at the review; the phone, which otherwise drops the line, keeps it in that one form. When the save failed because storage is full, the notice row says so too, since freeing space is something the line cannot offer.
- **[v3 new]** **Saving shows nothing; its problems are told outside a session:** a setting is saved when the player changes it, with no "saved" message ever. A save that fails, a tab left open while a newer version of the app saved data (or while another tab deleted everything), and saved data that cannot be used at all are each told by one calm notice in a row at the top of the workspace, never error-toned, never during a session: a notice that arises during one waits for its end, and one already showing when a session starts stays as it is, so nothing moves. A failed save leaves the app working in memory. Whether the browser protects the kept data is shown only where the data is listed, never as a notice: the player can do nothing about it there but export.
- **[v3 new]** **Loading saved settings:** the workspace is drawn only once the saved settings are read, so the theme and every control show the player's values from the first frame and nothing changes under their eyes; the read takes a moment at most, and meanwhile only the page's background shows. A saved value that is no longer valid quietly loads as its default. If the saved data cannot be read in a short wait, the visit starts from the defaults, saves nothing and says so in a notice, so a failed read never overwrites what is kept. Like an outdated tab, such a visit offers no deletion in the session list or a review; `Delete everything`, in Your data, is the only deletion it offers, the way out when a reload does not help. Data written by a newer version of the app wins over a failed read: once it is known to exist, the tab offers no deletion of any kind, since deleting could destroy what the newer version saved.
- **[v3 new]** **Deleting:** every deletion of kept data asks once, in a short confirmation naming what goes with it and saying it cannot be undone, with the deleting action in the error tone and `Cancel` beside it. Nothing else asks: not starting a session, not pinning, not marking a favorite, and unpinning only when the session holds favorites. Deleting everything reloads the page into its first visit.
- **[v3 new]** **Kept sessions at the edges:** the list has an empty state, a sentence saying the next session will be kept there; a kept session this version of the app cannot read stays listed with a line saying why and that a reload is the cure, and can be neither opened nor deleted from that tab, which is outdated; opening one takes a moment at most and shows only the opening button working.
- **[v3 new]** **My instrument at the edges:** a range under two octaves, or outside what its clef can show legibly (written C2 to C7 in treble clef, E0 to E5 in bass clef), is refused as it is entered, a clef change included, with the reason in words and note names, and the instrument cannot be saved until it is fixed; nothing is refused after the fact. Saving a range or transposition change, or deleting it, first warns that the sessions played with it go, and how many; with none kept, nothing is asked. A tab that must be reloaded offers neither while it has kept sessions, and says why. Deleting it returns the instrument choice to its template when it was selected.
- **[v3 new]** **Sound after a load:** a browser lets a page play sound only after the player's first click, tap or key press, and a MIDI note does not count. A controller reattached at load has had no click, so until the first one the starting note starts nothing, and the line that offers it says to click once first. `Start session` is such a click.
- **[v3 new]** **Pre-generation:** phrases prepared in the background between sessions show nothing, change no action's availability and announce nothing. The player never waits for them.
- **Automatic changes during a session:** apart from the note density stepping down to eighth notes when the window narrows to phone size, a product rule, only what the player opted into before the session changes during it (a rising tempo, the tension across phrases, varying settings), and the session never asks anything about it. The read-only controls and the tempo follow the music, except that the tension across phrases is never shown: the Tension control keeps the tension in effect, the player's own or one the varying settings set, while each phrase's step around it stays hidden. A new value, a stepped-down density included, appears when the first phrase built with it starts sounding (for a raised tempo, the first phrase that plays at it, a repeat included), replacing the old one in place, with no highlight, announcement or movement, so nothing shifts on screen. At the end, the tempo returns to where the player started it, and so do the settings the app varied. The rising tempo needs a controller: without one it is shown unavailable, with the reason in words, the player's choice kept for when a controller is attached.
- **Played notes in the review:** shown only on request, beside the music in a lane of their own and never on the staff, unquantized, in one neutral colour; no note is ever marked, matched or not. When nothing was played there is nothing to show or export, and neither the switch nor the export is offered, rather than offered and disabled.
- **[v3 new]** **Very short notes:** the in-between notes some controllers send while the fingers move are left out of the played-notes lane, with no switch, and out of the played-notes file unless the player includes them for that export.
- **[v3.1 new]** **Custom and presets at the edges:** changing a setting a preset holds, or choosing a key or mode outside what it is meant for, never asks and never blocks: the selection turns to Custom, naming the preset it came from, and only choosing a preset leaves it. A name a preset cannot take is refused as it is typed, with the reason, and the sheet waits; at the limit of fifty presets, saving a new one is unavailable with the reason beside it. A preset the browser refuses to keep stays usable for the visit, and one calm notice says so at once, since presets are managed outside a session. Where saving is off, presets made there last for the page and none can be deleted. A preset saved with rhythm settings that cannot form a phrase shows the usual rhythm alert when chosen.
- **[v3.2 new]** **Jam at the edges:** without a controller, Jam is unavailable with the reason in words, the player's choice kept, and Start works as with Jam off. A Jam that ends before its first phrase sounds keeps nothing and opens no review, as a count-in end; only one cause is told, at Idle, in a calm line: the app could not write a phrase from the doodle with these settings. A held doodle is let go by any setting change except the tempo, Rising tempo, the audio mix and the theme, which the line holding it says beforehand; the kept session's review offers it again. A doodle whose notes no mode of the app fits is said so plainly in one sentence, never naming a wrong note. Vary settings with nothing it may move is unavailable with the reason and the fix in words.
- **[v3.3 new]** **Seventh chords at the edges:** the level is a preference, never a promise, and the control says so; a phrase with fewer seventh chords than the level suggests, or none, is never reported. Raising the level changes the next phrase, not the one on screen, as for every setting. A session kept before v3.3 shows its chords as they were.
- **Offline:** Required behavior remains available because generation and notation are browser-local. If a browser capability is unavailable, explain the affected optional capability without blocking phrase generation.
