# Play Session Intent

> **New in v2.**

## Whose Flow This Is

This is the flow the second version exists for: playing continuously from the phrase on screen, with new phrases arriving without being asked for, until the session ends. It serves, first, the Curious Improviser (P1) in their real-time reading goal (P1.G2): reading and reacting in real time, gradually faster, under mild pressure that never scores them. It serves the Speed Builder (P2) in their endless-supply goal (P2.S1): fresh material they cannot memorize, at a tempo they choose. The Fledgling Composer (P3) is served at the flow's end, by the Review it opens, which is designed separately. Personas are defined in [`specs/personas.md`](../../../specs/personas.md).

Where the two pull apart, the Curious Improviser's experience is the default: nothing in this flow measures, ranks or corrects the player, and every signal on the staff is quiet.

The flow lives on the same screen as phrase generation, `screens/phrase-workspace/`, as that screen's session states. A session is not a place the player goes to; it is the workspace starting to move. Keeping it on one screen means the phrase the player was just reading becomes the first phrase they play, in the same position, at the same size, with nothing to re-find. It is a flow of its own, rather than a branch of `flows/generate-phrase/`, because the two have different drivers: phrase generation is driven by the player's clicks, the session by the audio clock, and folding one into the other would bury both.

The flow ends where the Review opens. The Review is designed separately and is not described here.

## Starting

The context of use settles the shape of the start. During a session both hands, and on a saxophone or wind controller the mouth too, are on the instrument, and the eyes are on the staff. So a session needs at most one action at each end and none in between, and with a controller connected it needs none at all.

**Start session** is a real, always-visible button at the end of the header, beside the phrase action, and it is the primary action as soon as a phrase is on screen and nothing has gone wrong since. Primary emphasis always sits on the next useful step: before there is a phrase, the next step is generating one; once there is one, it is playing it. `Start session` is disabled whenever the phrase that would become phrase 1 is missing or in doubt: with no phrase yet, while a new phrase is being generated (the phrase on screen is about to be replaced, and a session must never start on one phrase and find another under it), after a notation failure (the staff is blank), while the window leaves the staff no room, and when the rhythm settings cannot form a phrase at all.

No session starts while the staff is not shown, either. When the window is portrait-shaped on anything smaller than a desktop screen, the rotate-device message replaces the workspace, `Start session` with it, and the starting note does nothing; when the staff has no room at a readable size in the layout a session would use, because the window is too narrow or, on a phone, too short for it, the workspace shows a plain notice in place of the score, and `Start session` is disabled. On a tablet or larger it reads "The staff needs more room. Make the window larger."; on a phone, whose screen cannot be made larger, "This screen is too small to show the staff.", a sentence a desktop window narrowed to a phone's width also gets, since the sentence follows the same width test as the staff layout and that case is rare. That notice is not an error: nothing has failed and no button can fix it, so it offers no action and goes away by itself once the window is large enough, and a new phrase is then generated rather than the one it replaced. When the window has room but every fresh phrase came out too wide for it, the same notice names fewer notes as a second cure; there anything that could help (a wider window, a change to a setting the notes are generated from, `Regenerate phrase`) tries again at once, and `Start session` stays disabled until a phrase that fits is on screen. A session started in either case would have nothing to read and would end the moment it began, under the same rule that ends a session when the window stops showing the staff. As soon as the staff can be shown again, the ordinary start conditions apply.

No session starts over a generation failure either, even when the previous phrase is still on screen beneath the failure message. The player asked for a new phrase and didn't get one; the next useful step is getting it, so `Retry generation` takes the primary emphasis and `Start session` waits, disabled, until a regeneration succeeds. The starting note starts nothing meanwhile. This also means an error message is never on screen when a session starts, so a session never begins by removing one and shifting the staff up at the very moment the player needs it still. The phrase action and the session action keep fixed slots and one shared width for `Start session` and `Stop`, so a label or emphasis change never moves the header.

A Start click leaves focus where it was, on the button, which now reads `Stop`: a keyboard player who started with Space stops with Space. There is deliberately no global keyboard shortcut. Hands are on the instrument, and a shortcut that fires from anywhere is a shortcut a stray key press fires by accident.

**The starting note.** With a controller connected, the player can start without touching the screen at all, by playing the note phrase 1 begins on. Only that exact note, octave included, starts anything; any other note starts nothing and changes nothing on screen, the same "no mismatch hint, ever" rule the whole product keeps. A rule the player cannot see is a trap, so the note that starts the session is always named: a neutral line above the staff reads "Play this note to start: B♭4", in written pitch, the pitch the player reads. Octave numbers are a convention not every player knows, so the note itself also carries a small accent-coloured triangle above it on the staff: the octave is where the triangle points. The line and the triangle show only when a starting note could actually start a session, so neither ever promises something the app won't do: never without a controller attached, never without a phrase, never while a generation-failure message is showing, and, more generally, never while `Start session` is disabled or out of reach. Nothing played while the controller connection sheet is open starts anything: connecting is a step of its own.

**Where the eyes land.** A player who had scrolled down into the controls and then pressed Start would have the staff off screen at the moment it matters. So if the staff is not in view when a session starts, by either kind of start, the page jumps to the top of the workspace, instantly and once, before the count-in. It never scrolls again during the session. The jump is instant, not smooth, because a smooth scroll is music moving under the eyes for its whole length.

On a phone the screen is too short for the header, the score toolbar, the cue row and both staff systems at once, and the phone is the third display, after the laptop or desktop and the tablet. There the session keeps what it cannot do without: when a session starts, the page settles, by the same single instant jump, so that the cue row and both staff systems are entirely on screen, and the header and the toolbar may sit above the visible area. Of what is up there, Stop and the volumes are what the player may still need, so for the length of the session a compact `Stop` and the `Volume` button also sit at the end of the cue row, on the phone only. The `Backing` and `Click` toggles stay in the toolbar, a scroll away: still reachable, as the mix must always be, but not on screen. That is accepted because the phone is the secondary display and a session never needs them. Desktop and tablet keep everything on screen and need neither.

That settle only works if the cue row and both systems fit on the phone's screen at all, so on a phone the staff is sized to the screen's height as well as its width: it is drawn at the largest size at which the cue row and both systems fit together, never below the size at which it stops being readable. The reason is the phone's place in the session. It stands on a music stand with both of the player's hands on the instrument, so nothing can be scrolled once the music starts: a staff taller than the screen would push part of the second system out of view with no way to bring it back. The height it is fitted to is the screen's height with the browser's toolbars showing, the smallest the browser ever gives the page. Phone browsers hide and show their toolbars on their own, and a staff sized to whatever the browser shows at the moment would redraw every time they did, which is music moving under the eyes for a reason the player never chose. The size is the same between sessions and during one, so pressing Start still moves nothing. If a phone's screen is so short that even the smallest readable staff cannot fit beside the cue row, the staff has no room, exactly as in a window too narrow for it: the workspace shows the notice saying the screen is too small to show the staff, and no session starts. On a tablet or larger nothing changes: the screen is tall enough, and the staff keeps its fixed minimum height.

**Holding the screen still.** A session also asks the browser to lock the screen in landscape, from its start to its end. Where the browser refuses, which on a phone in an ordinary browser tab is the usual answer, nothing is shown.

**Which staff layout a session keeps.** A session's staff layout is set by the screen size when it starts and kept to its end, never chosen from what phrase 1 happens to contain. On a phone, below the tablet breakpoint, a session always splits each phrase over the two systems, bars 1–2 on top and bars 3–4 below; on a tablet or larger, it always shows one phrase per system. The reason is predictability. How wide a phrase needs to be depends on its notes, so a layout picked from phrase 1 would give a phone player one layout on one day and another the next, for no reason they could see, and would leave every later phrase to fit a width chosen by chance. The phone is the third display, and the split layout is the one that always holds there. The phone shows that same split layout at idle too, between sessions, so the layout a session keeps is simply the one already on screen: pressing Start never moves a note, and bar 3 is where the player saw it while getting ready. The cost is that a wide phone no longer shows a short phrase on one line between sessions; it is accepted because the phone is the third display, and a Start that rearranges the phrase the player has just read would cost more.

**When the window changes.** One rule covers every change of window shape or size during a session: if the staff can no longer be shown, the session ends, exactly as Stop does. That happens in two ways. The window can become portrait-shaped on anything smaller than a desktop screen, because the device turned or because the player put the browser in a split screen or resized it; the workspace would then give way to the rotate-device message, hiding the staff and Stop. Or the window can become too narrow for the session's staff layout at a readable size, or, on a phone, too short for it. The same check covers what the player has not seen yet: material already prepared and waiting for its turn on screen, such as the second half of the next phrase in the phone's split layout, is checked again at the new size, and if any of it no longer fits, the session ends the same way; otherwise a refill could later bring in something that cannot be drawn. A browser toolbar hiding or reappearing is not such a change, since the phone's staff is fitted to the height with the toolbars showing. Any other change redraws both systems at once at the new size, with the layout the session started with, one system per phrase or a phrase split over two systems: switching layout mid-session would change where the next phrase goes while the player is reading toward it. The redraw touches the system being read, which the session otherwise never does, and that is acceptable here because the player caused it: resizing a window takes a hand off the instrument on purpose, so it is not music moving under the eyes on its own. The redraw keeps everything the player has already seen: the same material in each system, the playhead on the same measure, the notes already lit.

## The Count-In

Every session starts with one measure of click, for both kinds of start. For a Start click, it gives the tempo before bar 1. For the starting note, it is also the only way the app can know where the player's beat 1 falls: the starting note only starts the count-in and belongs to no phrase. The count-in always clicks, even with the click turned off, because nothing else sets the tempo before bar 1.

The count-in is shown as well as heard: "1 2 3 4" in large digits in the row above the staff, each lit on its click. The current beat is strong, the beats already passed stay visible, and the ones still to come are muted, so a glance at any moment tells the player how long they have left. The digits change exactly on the audio clock, with no transition: a beat that eased in would be late by the length of its easing. The staff is untouched throughout, so bar 1 stays fully readable while the player gets ready to play it. The start marker is removed as the count-in begins, since its job is done. If the controller is unplugged during the count-in, its disconnect banner takes the row at once and the count-in keeps clicking: the player can still count the bar by ear, and the countdown matters more than the digits. On larger screens the second phrase fades into the lower system as soon as it is ready, usually during the count-in, so the player can already see what comes after phrase 1. After beat 4 the row empties and the playhead appears on bar 1.

## The Rolling Display

During a session the staff area shows two systems, one above the other. The player reads one while the other, never the one being read, is refilled with what comes next; reading goes top, bottom, top, like going down a page. The design refuses anything that moves music under the player's eyes, the hardest thing to follow from a music stand: no scrolling strip, no lines moving up. Only the system not being read ever changes.

On a tablet or larger, where one system holds a whole phrase, whole phrases alternate between the top and bottom systems. On a phone, where a session always splits a phrase over two systems, bars 1–2 are always on top and bars 3–4 always below, and each system is refilled with half of the next phrase. The idle workspace already reserves the second system's space and already shows phrase 1 in the layout the session keeps, so pressing Start neither changes the size of the staff area nor moves a note, on any screen.

**The playhead** is a quiet tint behind the measure being played. It steps to the next measure at each barline, on the audio clock, and never glides. A gliding line would be more precise, but it would be music moving under the eyes on every beat, and the player's own playing already says where in the measure they are; what a glance needs is which measure. The band sits behind the notes, so it never competes with them or with the acknowledgement on them. The system being played is marked by the playhead alone; the other system is never dimmed, since it is what the player reads ahead into.

**Refilling.** The moment the playhead leaves a system, that system clears, with the same fast fade the workspace already uses when a phrase is replaced, and fills with the next material as soon as it is engraved, with the same entrance fade. Clearing at once means a glance up never lands on the phrase just played and mistakes it for what comes next. Engraving is always ahead of the deadline the app keeps for itself, so the next material is on screen before the player's last measure on the other system.

**Every phrase fits.** Whether a phrase fits the session's layout at a readable size depends on its notes, not only on the settings, which a session locks: one crowded measure can make a phrase too wide. So during a session, fitting the layout is part of what makes a new phrase usable, like being a valid phrase at all. A phrase that does not fit is simply another attempt that failed, and the app tries again with a fresh seed, as it does when the generator cannot find a phrase. The player never sees any of it. A misfit that a retry fixes never counts toward the "struggling" notice: the player saw nothing, and nothing went wrong that they need to know about. Only when every retry fails does the ordinary fallback below repeat a phrase, and only repeats count toward the notice; a repeat counts whatever caused its failed attempts, misfits included, because from the player's side a repeat is a repeat. That makes the notice's wording, "The generator is struggling with these settings.", slightly off in one case: when the repeats came from the screen width alone rather than from the settings. The trade-off is accepted: it needs a run of phrases that are all too wide for the screen at a readable size, which is rare, and a separate count or a second wording would add a rule the player cannot see for a case they will hardly ever meet. A session never shows the notation error the idle workspace can show: a phrase that does not fit is retried, the repeated phrase is the one just played, already on screen in this layout, and a change of window that leaves anything, on screen or waiting, unable to fit ends the session instead.

**Acknowledgement.** When the player plays an expected note, its notehead, stem, flag and its own accidental switch to the acknowledgement colour, instantly, and stay lit until the system is refilled. Beams and ledger lines a note shares with its neighbours stay in the staff ink, so one lit note never half-lights another. A tied note's continuation lights with its attack, because it is one sounding note. The colour is a mid-tone green of its own, chosen to be clearly lighter or darker than the staff ink, not just a different hue: it differs from the staff ink, the staff's background and the playhead band by a contrast ratio of at least 3:1, in both themes. Acknowledgement is carried by colour alone, so that difference in lightness is what lets a player with red–green colour blindness still see a lit note. A missed or wrong note produces nothing: no mark, no colour, no drawing of what was played. A note that simply stays in the staff ink means nothing, which is the point; acknowledgement encourages and never grades.

## The Session Workspace

For the whole session, including the count-in, the workspace keeps its shape and changes only its behaviour:

- the phrase action stays in place, disabled;
- the session action reads `Stop`, primary;
- the controller status becomes a display only, because a controller connects only between sessions;
- the theme control stays live, because it changes presentation only;
- the tempo readout shows the tempo in effect;
- the musical controls below the score become read-only exactly as they are while a phrase is generating, with their values at full strength behind a read-only ring. When the app itself changes one of them, the new value simply shows, with no highlight to pull the eyes;
- the audio mix is never locked: the player may reach for it at any time, and it is the one thing they may reasonably want mid-session;
- on a phone, a compact `Stop` and the `Volume` button sit at the end of the cue row, since the header and toolbar may be out of view.

Everything on or near the staff belongs to the session. One row between the score toolbar and the staff, the session cue row, is where the session speaks: the starting-note line at idle, the count-in, and the session's notices. It is always present, at one fixed height, at idle, during a session and in the Review, so neither Start, nor the count-in, nor a notice ever moves the staff. The accepted cost is an empty row at idle when no controller is connected.

## The Audio Mix

The backing and the click each have an on/off toggle and a volume. The toggles sit in the score toolbar, never over the staff, with their state in words ("Backing on", "Click off"), not only in colour. The two volumes are behind one `Volume` button that opens a small panel with two sliders; only the player opens it, so it is the one element allowed to cover part of the staff, and Escape or a click outside closes it. A session start closes it too: left open from before, it would sit over the top system, and clearing it would take a hand off the instrument. Volume is a set-once-and-forget adjustment; on/off is what a player reaches for mid-phrase, so on/off gets the one-click position. The same pattern holds at every screen size; on a larger screen the mix sits at the toolbar's trailing end, and on a phone the toolbar wraps it onto its own row under the title. During a phone session the `Volume` button is repeated at the end of the cue row, which stays on screen when the toolbar may not; the toggles are not repeated, and stay a scroll away there (see Starting).

The click and the backing are both on by default. The click's volume slider never reaches silence: turning the click off is its toggle's job, which is also what keeps the count-in, played at the click volume, always audible. The backing is different: it is accompaniment the player may want gone entirely, so its slider goes all the way down to silence, as well as having its toggle. The exact ranges and default levels are placeholders until listening tests settle them; the layout does not depend on them.

## Ending

Stop, more than 30 seconds without a note (with a controller attached, counted only once the player has played a first note), a disconnect countdown reaching zero, a hidden browser tab, and a change of window that leaves no room for the staff all end the session the same way: the backing and click fade out briefly rather than cutting, the playhead goes, and the Review opens. There is no confirmation and no summary step. A confirmation would ask the player to take a hand off the instrument to agree with what they just did, and the Review is the summary. Focus goes to the session action, which reads `Start session` again, so the next session is one action away.

A hidden tab ends the session rather than pausing it: browsers throttle a hidden tab's timers, so a clock paused there could never resume in time, and a session with no pause state is simpler to trust.

**Ending before phrase 1 sounds.** Stop, a hidden tab or a window that can no longer show the staff, during the count-in, ends a session in which nothing was played, so there is nothing to review. The workspace goes straight back to where it was, with phrase 1 still on screen and ready to start again, and no Review opens. A Review left from an earlier session is not brought back either: the player moved on from it by starting. A window narrowed, or on a phone shortened, so far that it ended the session may leave the staff no room at idle either; the workspace then shows the notice that the staff has no room, and a new phrase comes once the window is large enough. Focus goes to the notice itself: the button that had focus is now a disabled `Start session`, and the next one along, `Regenerate phrase`, cannot help while the window is too small, since its phrase would land straight back on the notice; on the notice, a screen reader reads what to do. The window may also leave room for the staff but not for phrase 1's notes, whose width depends on what they are: phrase 1 is then quietly replaced by one that fits, as at any idle moment, and focus goes to the controller status, since both header actions wait while the new phrase loads. Both are rare cases, mostly a desktop window dragged narrow.

## Branches

**With or without a controller.** With a controller connected, the player can start with the starting note or with Start, and the session ends on Stop, a silence or an unplug that isn't recovered. Without one, as with an acoustic saxophone, the session starts from Start and only Stop ends it, apart from a hidden tab or a window that can no longer show the staff: nothing can be acknowledged and nothing can time out. Both run the same display; a session without a controller simply never lights a note and never shows the starting-note line.

**Controller falls silent.** Nothing changes on screen. The phrase keeps rolling at tempo, notes that fall due stay unlit, and after more than 30 seconds without a note the session ends. Catching a breath or losing one's place is part of playing, not a fault.

**Controller lost.** An unplugged controller is different and deliberately loud, because the player would otherwise keep playing into silence and conclude the app had stopped listening. An error-toned banner takes the cue row: "Controller disconnected — plug it back in to keep playing. Session ends in 30 s.", its countdown large and bold. It is the one piece of session text sized for reading from the music stand, because it is the one thing off the staff the player must read mid-session. On a phone, where it shares the row with the compact `Stop` and `Volume`, it drops the middle clause and reads "Controller disconnected. Session ends in 30 s.": what to do about an unplugged cable is obvious, and the shorter sentence keeps to the row's height. The session keeps rolling. Plugging the same device back in removes the banner and carries on; the countdown reaching zero ends the session like a silence. The banner takes priority over any other notice, and over the count-in if the unplug happens before bar 1. The full reasoning is in [`flows/controller-connection/intent.md`](../controller-connection/intent.md).

**Generation struggling.** The player never sees a blocking error during a session: that would stop them for something they didn't cause. The app retries quietly, counting a phrase that does not fit the session's layout as one more failed try, and, if it still runs out of time, repeats the previous phrase, which is musically harmless. A repeat fills its system like any other phrase and is not marked. If the generator keeps struggling, from the second repeat in a row, a small neutral notice takes the cue row: "The generator is struggling with these settings." It is never error-toned, it never stops the session, and it goes as soon as a freshly generated phrase starts sounding. A disconnect banner replaces it while a countdown runs.

**Hidden tab, or no room for the staff.** Ends the session as described above; during the count-in, it returns to the idle workspace instead.

**Screen kept awake.** A session involves no touch for 5 to 30 minutes, so a tablet, phone or laptop would dim and lock partway, and on a phone a locked screen hides the tab and so ends the session. The app keeps the screen awake from the moment a session starts, the count-in included, until it ends, however it ends. If the browser refuses or doesn't support that, the session runs normally and nothing is shown: a message about it would pull the eyes off the staff for something the player can't act on mid-session.

## What This Flow Deliberately Leaves Out

- No pause. A session runs or it has ended.
- No score, streak, accuracy figure or miss marker, anywhere in the session.
- No staff-only view hiding the header and toolbar: with this layout everything near the staff already belongs to the session, and the audio mix has to stay reachable at all times. On a phone the header and toolbar may scroll out of view during a session, but Stop and the volumes come with the cue row, and the on/off toggles stay a scroll away.
- No confirmation before a session ends, however it ends.
