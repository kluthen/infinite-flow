This flow serves the Curious Improviser (P1) first, in their vocabulary-by-exposure goal (P1.G1): between sessions the player sets a musical direction, reads all four measures, and asks for another whole phrase whenever they want another idea, at their own pace. It also sets up every session: the phrase it leaves on screen is the one a session starts from (`flows/play-session/`). Personas are defined in [`specs/personas.md`](../../../../../specs/personas.md).

The player needs one complete musical thought, not four piecemeal surprises. The flow therefore makes generation a whole-phrase act. Any control change updates the pending musical direction but leaves the visible phrase intact until the player explicitly requests a replacement.

The workspace opens with a ready-to-generate empty staff when no result exists, and `Generate phrase` is the primary action: the next useful step is getting a phrase. Once a result exists, the same action says `Regenerate phrase`, making the scope of replacement explicit, and steps back to a secondary action: the next useful step is now playing the phrase, so `Start session`, beside it, takes the primary emphasis. The phrase action takes the emphasis back whenever it is the next step again: `Retry generation` after a generation failure, `Retry notation` after a notation failure.

**[v3.1 changed]** The workspace opens with the settings the player left, not the defaults. Every control the phrase is generated from, the embellishment levels, the preset selection, the instrument choice and the theme are saved the moment the player changes them, and a visit picks up where the last one ended, which is what a practice partner should feel like from one evening to the next. The saved value is the one the player chose, not the one a phrase happened to use: a `Sixteenth` chosen on a laptop is still `Sixteenth` on the next visit, even after a visit on a phone, where phrases use eighth notes. The saved settings are read before the workspace is first drawn, so the theme and every control appear with their saved values and nothing changes under the player's eyes, and nothing can be generated from the defaults by mistake in the meantime: the first phrase of a visit always comes from what the player left. Reading takes a moment the player does not notice; if it takes longer than a short start-up wait, or what is saved cannot be used, the visit starts from the defaults and says so in a notice, rather than leaving a blank page or quietly overwriting what is kept (see [`flows/saved-data/`](../saved-data/intent.md)). Saving itself shows nothing and never starts a generation.

The action is deliberately near the score header rather than buried in controls: the player looks at the phrase, decides whether to seek another idea, and acts there.

The score always reads in the current instrument profile's own written pitch. There is no separate notation-display control and no Concert override.

**[v3 changed]** Every built-in instrument profile renders in treble clef (the G clef on the second line), and my instrument in the treble or bass clef its player chose, which changes where notes are drawn and never which notes; only the instrument's own transposition (none for the C MIDI keyboard, a major ninth for the B-flat tenor saxophone, a major sixth for the E-flat alto saxophone, the player's own for my instrument) changes what is written.

The manual theme override is the one remaining control adjacent to the score, and it changes only the presentation palette; changing it never creates a generation request or changes the phrase, control values, seed, selected profile, or result data. Controls follow the score in a vertically reached section: a compact horizontal group layout on wide displays and a stacked layout on mobile landscape. This gives all four measures the full available width without a horizontal scroll.

The tonal-context control — written key and collection — is read and set in the selected instrument profile's own written pitch, not in concert pitch: the player picks the key the way it is written for their instrument. Selecting C Ionian on the B-flat tenor saxophone profile shows an empty key signature and sounds a concert B-flat Ionian; on the C MIDI keyboard, written and concert pitch are identical. The control's label always shows the written selection; request construction, not the player, converts it to the concert tonal context the generator receives.

**[v3.1 new]** A preset, chosen at the top of the controls, sets many of these settings in one step, and may move the mode, or the key, into the ones it is meant for (see [`flows/presets/`](../presets/intent.md)). It is still a change of settings like any other: the phrase on screen stays until the player asks for another, so choosing a preset to try never throws away a phrase they were reading, and the next `Regenerate phrase` plays the preset. It cannot be chosen while a phrase is being generated, when every control waits. Every preset carries its embellishment levels, which the request now holds in place of a style's name: renaming a preset changes nothing a phrase depends on.

**[v3.1 changed]** Instrument profile is a foundation choice separate from the preset: no preset sets it, and changing it never makes the settings Custom.

The player selects the declarative C MIDI keyboard profile (concert MIDI 48-72; Low 48-60, Middle 54-66, High 60-72), B-flat tenor saxophone profile (46-70; Low 46-58, Middle 52-64, High 58-70), or E-flat alto saxophone profile (51-75; Low 51-63, Middle 57-69, High 63-75). A profile description makes the active range and three available whole-phrase windows inspectable without asking the player to reason from MIDI values during ordinary generation. `Varying` is a register choice, not a fourth window: the seeded planner selects one feasible Low, Middle, or High window for the whole phrase and does not switch it between measures or notes.

**[v3 new]** The player can also make their own instrument from one of the three: a name, a range and a transposition (see [`flows/my-instrument/`](../my-instrument/intent.md)). Once it exists, it is a fourth choice in the same control, labelled with its name, with its range and windows shown the same way, so it is selected exactly like a built-in profile and the player never has to think of it as a different kind of thing. Selecting it is a profile switch, with every rule below; so is creating it (it is selected when saved), saving a change to it while it is selected, and deleting it while it is selected, which selects the profile it was made from. Each of these changes what the phrase is written and generated for, and the same decisive replacement keeps the staff honest about it.

Switching profile is intentionally decisive: it keeps the written key and collection but always replaces the phrase. When a phrase is shown, the switch never validates the old phrase against the new active range and never retains or transposes it — it keeps every other control, takes a fresh seed, and regenerates the complete four-measure phrase under the new profile immediately. The one exception is a rhythm-control combination (note density, syncopation, silence density) that is already unsupported at the moment of the switch: the switch still applies and the new profile stays selected, but the phrase is cleared to the empty workspace instead of regenerating, and the unsupported-rhythm notice stays visible explaining why. With no phrase shown yet, the switch only changes the selected profile; nothing generates.

The phrase is shown only after generation has produced a valid, complete result that fits the room the window gives the staff at a readable size. During regeneration, the current phrase remains readable; the action becomes unavailable only long enough to prevent accidental duplicate requests, and `Start session` waits with it, so a session never starts on a phrase about to be replaced.

The next score replaces it as a cohesive unit rather than measure by measure. If generation fails, the prior valid score stays visible with a retry beside the action.

If notation fails after valid phrase data exists, for a reason that trying again can cure, the score region instead becomes a notation-error panel with retry; it does not pretend letter names are a score. A window too small for the staff is not such a failure, and has its own answer below.

Between sessions the app also replaces a phrase by itself, and only for one reason: the phrase cannot be read in the room the window gives it. Whether a phrase fits depends on its notes as well as on the window: a busy phrase can need more width than the window gives at a readable size, while another from the same settings fits easily. So a phrase too wide for the room is never shown. The app quietly tries a fresh one, up to three more times, inside the same loading, and the player just sees the phrase arrive a moment later. The same happens when a narrower window leaves the phrase on screen too wide: it is replaced rather than hidden, since a phrase that cannot be read is worth less than a fresh one that can. Each attempt uses the note density the window allows when it is made, since the busiest one, `Sixteenth`, is unavailable on a phone.

Some of these loadings have no phrase to keep readable. After a window change has left the phrase too wide, or once the notice below clears because the window has room again, the phrase that was shown is gone for good, so the loading shows no score until the new phrase lands. A round of attempts under the notice about the notes keeps that notice in place instead, dimmed, rather than blinking it off and on.

When no phrase can be shown, the workspace says why, in one plain sentence in place of the score, rather than showing an error. A window with no room for the staff at a readable size gets a sentence about the window. A window with room, where every fresh phrase came out too wide, gets one that names both cures, a larger window or a lower note density. Neither is a failure a retry button can cure, so neither takes the notation-error panel, and no session starts while either shows. The first clears by itself when the window gives the staff room, and a new phrase is generated. The second clears only when a phrase that fits lands, so anything that could produce one tries again at once: a wider window, a change to a setting the notes are generated from, or `Regenerate phrase`. The full reasoning is in [When the Staff Has No Room](../../screens/phrase-workspace/intent.md#when-the-staff-has-no-room).

When the rhythm settings cannot form a phrase at all, nothing can be generated. So a notice that would clear or try again, a phrase that stops fitting, or attempts already running give way to the empty staff, with the rhythm alert saying why, and the app generates nothing by itself afterwards: once the rhythm settings change, the player generates by hand, as from the empty workspace. A window widened past the tablet breakpoint can bring this about on its own when `Sixteenth` is the chosen note density, since that choice, unavailable on a phone, applies again on a larger screen.

**[v3 new]** Between sessions, once a phrase a session could start from is on screen, the app quietly prepares the next few phrases a session would play after it, so that when the player presses Start the second phrase can join the first smoothly even at the fastest tempos (master spec §8). None of this shows. The phrase on screen, its starting note, the actions and their labels, and what a screen reader hears never change because of it, and the player never waits for it: anything the player asks for at that moment, a new phrase, a profile switch, a changed setting, goes first, and the preparation in progress is dropped without a word. A change that would make the prepared phrases wrong discards them and starts again after the new phrase. Nothing is prepared while the review shows, and nothing prepared is kept past the visit. An indicator was considered and rejected: it would tell the player about work they cannot influence and do not need, and it would be one more thing changing on screen while they read.
