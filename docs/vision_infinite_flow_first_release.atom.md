# Infinite Flow Product Scope

## INTENT
Establish Infinite Flow as a browser-based, client-side practice partner for a monophonic musician that acknowledges and encourages, never penalizing, grading, ranking or scoring the player.

## THE RULE / LOGIC
The shipped first release proposes a harmonic context and a playable melodic response as one four-measure staff-notated phrase for a selected C MIDI keyboard, B-flat tenor saxophone, or E-flat alto saxophone profile, under player-selected musical controls, which the player explicitly regenerates to hear a new variation. Notation always shows the selected instrument's written pitch.

Version 2, delivered as one release, v2.0, whose scope is named in the parts below (v2.1, v2.2 and v2.4 name parts of it, not releases of their own; the number v2.3 is not used), turns that static phrase into a continuous practice session for one local player, entirely in the browser:

- v2.0 core: a rolling sequence of phrases joined so the ear does not notice a seam; a simple, deterministic backing track (bass and block chords) with a metronome click and a one-measure count-in; sessions that start, run, and stop or time out; reading one MIDI controller and acknowledging only the notes the player gets right, with a one-time controller-transposition check; a review of the last session; export of the session's generated notes to MusicXML or MIDI.
- v2.1: played notes shown in the review on a toggle and exportable as their own MIDI file; an opt-in, visible settings-mutation flag that lets the app quietly vary its own generation settings during a session.
- v2.2: opt-in adaptive tempo rising on a fixed schedule; an opt-in multi-phrase tension arc.
- v2.4: the checks that need the reference hardware or real programs, which block the v2.0 tag: the controller-transposition check on the reference breath controller, the acknowledgement windows and latency tuned with it, and the playhead and main-thread timing measured in a real Chromium browser. The same checks with a USB keyboard, and real exports opened in a notation program, a MIDI tool and a digital audio workstation, follow the tag as v2.0.x hand checks.

Version 3 begins with v3.0, Storage, which keeps things on the player's device, and only there: their settings and preferences; one instrument they describe themselves, My instrument, which the controller-transposition check can set; the controller they last chose; the most recent session, kept automatically, and the sessions they pin, each reviewable again and each with an optional comment; favorite phrases, which pin their session, with an export of favorites only; and the notes they played, kept only after an explicit opt-in. The player sees what is kept, and deletes any of it or all of it, in one place. v3.0 also generates up to four phrases ahead while the app waits at Idle, without ever making Start wait, and leaves very short stray controller notes out of the review and, by default, out of the played-notes export. Nothing is ever sent anywhere: data leaves the device only through an export the player triggers.

v3.1, Generation choices, lets the player choose how the melody is decorated and keep the settings they like: seven embellishment figures written as ordinary notes (passing tone, accented passing tone, neighbor tone, appoggiatura, diatonic enclosure, chromatic approach, chromatic enclosure), each with a level from Off to Strongly favored that only reorders the generator's choices and never sets a quota; and presets, named sets of the generation controls and figure levels chosen in one step, eight built in and the player's own, each possibly meant for certain keys and modes, which the key and mode controls highlight without ever blocking. Neutral and Jazz at their defaults sound exactly as before.

v3.2, Jam, lets the player start a session from a short idea of their own, the doodle, played on the controller over the app's click after a count-in; the app continues it with rolling phrases that match its shape, at a distance the player chooses, without ever changing a setting or quoting it. After a Jam the review may suggest the key and mode the doodle sounds in, which the player uses or declines. A rhythm lock makes every phrase of a session repeat one rhythm while the pitches change, with small one-phrase rhythm changes the player allows kind by kind; and the player chooses which groups of settings Vary settings may move.

v3.3, Richer chords, is direction only and not yet designed: the player may allow chords richer than triads, seventh chords first and extended chords (ninths and beyond) possibly later, through flags they set in the app for the chord families the generator may use. It is opt-in: by default the chords stay root-position diatonic triads, so a player who allows nothing richer sees, hears and exports what they do today, and their replay keys, saved settings, presets and kept sessions reproduce as before. The chords stay generated: a flag allows a chord family and never chooses a progression. A research phase with cited sources comes before any design, so that chord generation uses sourced data.

Anything that adapts during a session is opt-in and visible. A session continues from the app's own generated material; from v3.2 a Jam also takes its shape from a doodle the player plays on purpose, which only orders the generator's choices.

Out of scope through v3.3: generation that quotes the player's notes, develops them by named operations or chooses chords to fit them; a new doodle during a running Jam; a doodle taken from a file or a past session; the doodle's exact rhythm as played, rhythm ratios and a rhythm editor; altered dominants and harmonic substitutions; ornaments (grace notes, mordents, trills, turns, jazz articulations); richer presets (rhythm patterns, range or note restrictions, tempo or instrument preferences); exporting or sharing a preset; a backup file of everything kept, to move it to another browser; learning the player's taste from how they play; marking a range of measures as a favorite; remembering a controller's latency, or a player-facing latency calibration step; editing, cutting or splicing generated material; richer review tooling such as passage replay or expected-against-played views; a quiet per-measure acknowledgement of off-script playing that fits the harmony; accompaniment with musical roles (harmonizing, responding, contrasting); playback of the generated lead line; a staff-only session view; microphone capture or acoustic pitch detection; accounts, sharing or server-side state; browsers other than Chromium-based ones. Permanently out of scope: any grading, scoring, ranking or wrong-note signal, in any view, at any point.

## TECHNICAL INTERFACE
At Idle the app accepts a selected supported instrument profile, player-selected musical controls with the key chosen as written for that instrument, and an explicit regeneration action; notation always shows the selected instrument's written pitch, with no separate display-mode choice. From v2 it adds starting and stopping a session, the audio mix, an optional "Connect controller" step, the review of the last session with its export actions, and opt-in flags for settings mutation, adaptive tempo and the multi-phrase tension arc. From v3.0 it adds a place that lists and deletes what is kept, a list of kept sessions with their reviews, favorites and comments, a switch for keeping played notes, and My instrument. From v3.1 it adds seven figure-level controls, a preset selector with saving, renaming and deleting of player presets, and the restriction's highlight on the key and mode controls; from v3.2, the Jam switch and its drift, the review's doodle section and key and mode suggestion, the rhythm lock and its allowed rhythm changes, and Vary settings' allowed groups; from v3.3, the flags that allow richer chord families.

## EXPECTATION
Scope decisions keep the product a non-evaluative, browser-only practice partner. Every business atom falls within the first release, the v2 scope (release v2.0, with its parts v2.1, v2.2 and v2.4) or the v3.0, v3.1, v3.2 or v3.3 scope above; nothing listed as out of scope enters a release without this scope being changed first; nothing ever grades, scores, ranks or marks the player wrong.
