# Neutral Profile

Status: draft v0.1

## Player Promise

The neutral profile makes a four-measure phrase from one selected key and scale or mode. It stays inside that collection, moves through mostly diatonic harmony, and uses rhythm, shape, space, and resolving scale notes to create movement. It does not introduce jazz substitutions, altered dominants, chromatic approaches, or chromatic enclosures.

## Four-Measure Harmonic Plan

(proposed) The harmony planner selects a phrase function before assigning exact chords.

- With low harmonic motion, sustain the tonic or use tonic prolongation across most bars.
- With gentle motion in tonal major or minor, use a tonic-oriented departure and return such as tonic - tonic/prolongation - predominant - tonic or dominant.
- With active motion in tonal major or minor, use tonic - predominant - dominant - tonic for a closed phrase, or tonic - tonic/prolongation - predominant - dominant for an open phrase.
- In a modal selection, derive all chord choices from the selected collection and privilege the modal tonic. Do not add a raised leading tone merely to force a dominant cadence.
- Use one harmony event per bar by default. Active harmonic motion may use two only when the rhythm plan leaves each change legible.

The final bar is intentional: tonic is a closed arrival, dominant is an open arrival, and a softer diatonic return is a less final close. The initial profile ends open so the player can continue rather than treat the phrase as final. A closed ending may become a later option.

## Tension and Release

(proposed) The profile maps tension to observable constraints:

- Low tension favors chord tones on beat 1 and at chord changes, stable durations, stepwise movement, and space after a completed gesture.
- Medium tension permits a diatonic passing or neighbor tone on a weak subdivision when it resolves by step within the next one or two subdivisions.
- High neutral tension permits an accented diatonic appoggiatura: arrive by leap on a metrically strong place and resolve by step. Limit it to one per bar or two per phrase.
- A closed final bar targets a tonic-chord tone, preferably scale degree 1, 3, or 5. An open final bar targets a chord tone of the active dominant.

A passing tone moves stepwise in one direction between two chord tones. A neighbor tone steps away from a stable pitch then returns to it. An appoggiatura arrives by leap and resolves by step; it feels tense because it is accented before it resolves.

## Rhythm and Space

(proposed) Bars 1-2 form a rhythmic gesture; bars 3-4 answer it by repeating a cell then varying its ending, onset, or pitch contour. A bar must fill exactly four beats using the selected subdivision grid.

- At low syncopation, include an onset or arrival on beat 1 and favor beats 1 and 3.
- At higher syncopation, move selected events to offbeats or sustain through a stronger beat, while retaining at least one metrical anchor per bar.
- Silence density inserts actual rests rather than merely reducing note count. Prefer a rest after a completed gesture, cadence, or long note rather than at a random interruption.
- (proposed) For wind-friendly defaults, leave a breath opportunity after no more than roughly one and a half to two bars of continuous fast material. This is a practical heuristic, not a universal physical limit.

## Pitch and Contour

(proposed) The pitch planner favors steps and diatonic thirds, chooses chord-tone targets near the preceding pitch, and preserves a common tone where possible. If no common tone exists, it prefers a step or third toward the next target.

- Low leapiness permits mainly steps and thirds.
- Medium leapiness may add fourths and fifths.
- High leapiness may use sixths or larger at a phrase peak, subject to the active instrument range.
- After a leap larger than a fourth, recover with an opposite-direction step or short contrary motion.
- Consecutive leaps must outline a recognizable chord/arpeggio rather than create arbitrary zig-zags.
- Contour establishes direction early, reaches its most pronounced point around the phrase middle or later, then moves gently toward the final target.
- Arpeggio chaos changes the order and interval contrast of eligible chord tones. At low chaos, trace nearby tones in a predictable direction; at high chaos, permit reordered eligible tones and stronger interval contrast, but retain range, contour, and leap-recovery constraints.
