# Neutral Profile

Status: curated profile summary

## Player Promise

The neutral profile makes a four-measure phrase from one selected key and scale or mode. It stays inside that collection, moves through mostly diatonic harmony, and uses rhythm, shape, space, and resolving scale notes to create movement. It does not introduce jazz substitutions, altered dominants, chromatic approaches, or chromatic enclosures.

## Four-Measure Harmonic Plan

The settled finite chord/event vocabulary and static, gentle, and active templates are in [Harmony Planner Policy](harmony-planner-policy.md). Major and Minor use tonal functional candidates; the seven named modes use collection-derived modal-color candidates and never gain a raised leading tone. All candidates are diatonic triads, Jazz uses the same plan, and every first-release phrase ends open. Closed tonic templates are historical context only and are not legal candidates.

## Tension and Release

The profile maps tension to observable constraints:

- `Settled` favors chord tones on anchors and prompt local resolution.
- `Lift`, `Build`, and `Edge` schedule progressively later or more concentrated legal diatonic activity without changing another selected control.
- Passing, neighbor, approach, and appoggiatura roles are chord-relative and reserve a concrete chord-tone resolution onset before acceptance.
- All local tension resolves before the final sounding event; the endpoint follows the open-ending policy even under static or modal harmony.

A passing tone moves stepwise in one direction between two chord tones. A neighbor tone steps away from a stable pitch then returns to it. An appoggiatura arrives by leap and resolves by step; it feels tense because it is accented before it resolves.

## Rhythm and Space

Bars 1-2 form a rhythmic gesture and bars 3-4 answer it with measurable recurrence plus variation. Exact grids, attack/rest budgets, legal displacement, ties, and answer transforms are defined in [Rhythm and Phrase-Structure Policy](rhythm-phrase-structure.md).

- At low syncopation, include an onset or arrival on beat 1 and favor beats 1 and 3.
- At higher syncopation, move selected events to offbeats or sustain through a stronger beat, while retaining at least one metrical anchor per bar.
- Silence density inserts actual rests rather than merely reducing note count. Prefer a rest after a completed gesture, cadence, or long note rather than at a random interruption.
- Breath-heavy and conversational settings reserve explicit rests under the documented phrase-level budgets. Whether these budgets are comfortable at every BPM remains a listening/player-test question, not a physiological fact.

## Pitch and Contour

The pitch planner favors nearby legal chord tones, then complete resolving diatonic figures. Exact candidate order, contour checkpoints, leap state, and validation are defined in [Pitch Planner and Validation Policy](pitch-planner-validation.md).

- Leapiness uses the settled `Smooth`, `Shaped`, `Leaping`, and `Angular` caps/budgets; these are ceilings, not quotas.
- Large leaps and recognized arpeggio runs incur the documented contrary-motion recovery obligation.
- Contour is evaluated at bar-level metrical checkpoints while decorative notes may move locally in either direction.
- Arpeggio chaos only orders chord-tone candidates by distance from the previous pitch. `Predictable` prefers the nearest chord tone; `Chaotic` prefers the farthest; `Varied` leaves chord-tone order to the seeded tie-break. It retains range, contour, and leap-recovery constraints.
