# Define Tension, Shape Leaps, Preserve Agency

Infinite Flow exposes **Tension arc** as `Settled`, `Lift`, `Build`, and `Edge`, defaulting to **Lift**, and **Leapiness** as `Smooth`, `Shaped`, `Leaping`, and `Angular`, defaulting to **Shaped**. Tension schedules diatonic instability and local resolution; leapiness bounds the size, count, and recovery of successive pitch intervals. Both operate inside the exact four-measure 4/4 contract, selected profile range/window and collection, deterministic seed, and open endpoint ([First-release specification](../../specs/first-release-master-spec.md)). The implementation principle is **named constraints before expressive preference**: tension ranks only what remains. Exact behavior is consolidated in the [harmony](harmony-planner-policy.md), [rhythm](rhythm-phrase-structure.md), and [pitch](pitch-planner-validation.md) policies.

## Four named choices make phrase character audible

The two controls should be segmented, named choices, not sliders. The workspace already selects named discrete outcomes because its first-release generator uses discrete policy values and because labels make effects inspectable ([Interface strategy](../../ui_ux/strategy.md)). `Tension arc` belongs in the existing foundation group, while `Leapiness` remains in melodic shape; `Arpeggio chaos` stays in the disclosed character group ([Phrase workspace handoff](../../ui_ux/screens/phrase-workspace/handoff.md)). This placement preserves the interface's principle that a player can make a usable phrase from foundational choices before opting into advanced character settings. Each choice needs one plain-language promise, an accessible detail line with the policy it activates, and no visible probability or internal tension score.

| Control and default | Player-facing choices | Audible promise | Bounded generator policy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Tension arc: Lift** | `Settled`, `Lift`, `Build`, `Edge` | From promptly settled motion to the strongest permitted pull and release. | Schedules active diatonic targets and local resolution timing; never widens another control's bounds. |
| **Leapiness: Shaped** | `Smooth`, `Shaped`, `Leaping`, `Angular` | From mostly next-door motion to bold interval shapes and short chord-tone runs. | Sets maximum adjacent interval, phrase leap budget, and recovery obligation. |

`Settled` prioritizes chord tones on metrically strong locations and resolves any active scale degree by the next strong onset. `Lift`, the default, allows a small amount of active diatonic motion in measures 2-3 and one resolution deferred to the following beat or bar. `Build` concentrates active notes and a limited two-note diatonic approach in the middle of the phrase. `Edge` makes measure 3 the point of greatest permitted instability and uses the latest valid local resolution. The underlying research supports modelling tension as a changing trajectory made from tonal attraction, melodic expectation, rhythmic activity, and surface cues, rather than as an inherent property of one pitch ([Lerdahl and Krumhansl](https://doi.org/10.1525/mp.2007.24.4.329); [Farbood](https://online.ucpress.edu/mp/article/30/2/121/62827/A-Parametric-Temporal-Model-of-Musical-Tension)). The labels are product language, not experimentally validated universal categories; musician listening tests must calibrate their perceived spacing.

The archived tension notes propose a final stable tonic/triad arrival. Infinite Flow must not adopt that superseded recommendation: first-release phrases must end **open**, and a closed tonic ending is explicitly deferred ([Open-ending rule](../rule_open_phrase_ending.atom.md)). At every tension level, including `Edge`, the cadence validator therefore requires that any local dissonance, appoggiatura, Jazz approach, or enclosure has already resolved before the final sounding event, but the last event must preserve an open continuation. With generated tonal motion, it should be a chord tone of the final dominant; with static tonic prolongation or modal material, it should be a non-final tonic-area continuation such as scale degree 5 or another profile-approved open target, never an asserted tonic closure. This substitutes **resolved local motion plus open phrase endpoint** for a falsely universal "stable final landing," while retaining key clarity.

## Leap budgets preserve direction within the selected register window

Leapiness should use adjacent sounding-note intervals, excluding intervening rests: a step is 1-2 semitones, a skip 3-4, a leap 5-6, and a large leap 7-12. No result may exceed an octave. The absolute filter is the selected profile's inclusive active concert range and resolved whole-phrase window, independent of Concert, B-flat, or E-flat display notation ([Instrument profile ranges](../rule_instrument_profile_range.atom.md)). These deliberately constrained working ranges make the choices govern legible melodic direction and compensation rather than claim a universal saxophone technique limit. The names describe melody character rather than quality rankings; they replace abstract "low/medium/high" with vocabulary a player can predict.

| Leapiness level | Maximum interval | Maximum phrase budget | Required treatment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Smooth** | 4 semitones | No leaps; steps and skips only | No arpeggio-chaos-created leap. |
| **Shaped** (default) | 7 semitones, a fifth | At most 2 leaps; at most 1 large leap | Immediate recovery after a large leap; one recognized two-note chord-tone skip pair is permitted. |
| **Leaping** | 10 semitones, a minor seventh | At most 4 leaps; at most 2 large leaps | Recover each large leap, or recover after one recognized run of at most 3 chord tones. |
| **Angular** | 12 semitones, an octave | At most 6 leaps; at most 3 large leaps | Same recovery; no more than one 3-note arpeggio run per measure or two runs per phrase. |

These are ceilings, not quotas: a high setting authorizes a leap when phrase shape permits it but never compels one. After a large leap, the next sounding note must make an opposite-direction step of 1-2 semitones or short contrary motion of 3-4 semitones. A chord-tone arpeggio exception applies only to a coherent run of 2-3 sounding chord-tone notes connected by 1-2 adjacent same-direction skip/leap intervals under one harmony; its recovery debt occurs after the run. Two same-direction leaps therefore require the complete recognized three-note run. An unrecognised chain is invalid rather than an accidental arpeggio. This operationalizes the project's existing rule that larger leaps recover and consecutive leaps must read as an arpeggio, not arbitrary zig-zags ([Leap recovery](../rule_leap_recovery.atom.md)). It is a conservative melodic-writing convention, not a claim that jazz or contemporary practice universally forbids unrecovered leaps ([Open Music Theory](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory/chapter/species-counterpoint/)).

The planner must reserve range for the selected contour before accepting an outward leap. A rising phrase, for example, accepts an upward fifth only when its mandatory contrary recovery still leaves a route to the remaining rise; otherwise it chooses a smaller move or defers the leap. `Shaped` is the correct default because it realizes the project's stated preference for steps and diatonic thirds while allowing the occasional fourth or fifth that gives a short phrase structural profile ([Neutral profile](neutral-profile.md)). Its compact policy description should read "A singable line with an occasional fifth"; expanded detail can say "largest jump: fifth; up to 2 jumps; big jumps turn back by step." This makes the control teachable without making semitone thresholds its primary language.

## Explicit controls outrank tension at every decision

Tension is not a macro that turns other controls up. It owns only the placement of permitted instability and the timing of its resolution. The project’s tension research correctly identifies pitch height, onset frequency, and accent placement as perceptual contributors, but those dimensions already have dedicated user controls; automatically changing them would make cause and effect unknowable ([Farbood](https://online.ucpress.edu/mp/article/30/2/121/62827/A-Parametric-Temporal-Model-of-Musical-Tension)). The generator should instead use this lexicographic order: **hard feasibility and cadence policy; selected profile, tonal collection, range, and contour; jointly feasible density and silence budgets; syncopation onset plan; leapiness and arpeggio-chaos candidates; tension ranking; seeded deterministic tie-break**. Constraint-based sequence generation is an established way to retain user-selected constraints while steering musical alternatives ([Pachet and Roy](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jc.2010.07.004)).

This order translates into clear boundaries. `Syncopation` alone chooses offbeat attacks and ties across beats; tension may put an active note at an already available offbeat and defer its resolution to the next existing strong onset, but cannot add an onset. `Silence density` alone reserves actual rests and tension cannot fill or remove them. `Register` alone sets the octave centre and active range; tension cannot create a high-register climax. `Leapiness` alone sets interval caps and budgets; tension may place an already permitted leap before a resolution but cannot enlarge it. `Arpeggio chaos` chooses among eligible chord-tone orderings and interval contrast; tension can rank active roles only inside its documented arc schedule and cannot change that ordering policy. High syncopation is not inherently "more tension": controlled listening research found an inverted-U relationship between syncopation and groove pleasure and wanting-to-move, not a monotonic tension dial ([Sioros et al.](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0094446)). Every bar must still retain a metrical anchor ([Metrical-anchor rule](../rule_preserve_metrical_anchor.atom.md)).

Profile policy constrains what "active" means. In the Neutral profile, active material is a diatonic non-chord tone whose preparation, metric location, and stepwise resolution make its pull legible; increased tension can allow the sanctioned accented diatonic appoggiatura ([Neutral profile](neutral-profile.md)). It must never introduce chromatic pitches, substitutions, altered dominants, or enclosures ([Neutral diatonic-pitch rule](../rule_neutral_profile_diatonic_pitches.atom.md)). In Jazz, tension does not request chromaticism: an approach or enclosure is allowed only when its own profile rule can resolve immediately to a chord-tone target and never remains unresolved at phrase end ([Jazz chromatic-resolution rule](../rule_jazz_chromatic_resolution.atom.md)). This chord-relative definition is more musically accurate than the archived note's fixed 1/3/5-versus-2/4/6/7 partition whenever generated diatonic harmony changes from bar to bar, and it respects modal material without inventing tonal dominant behavior.

## Inspectable policy makes constraints feel intentional

The interface should disclose that Tension means "How strongly the phrase seeks contrast and unresolved momentum within your other settings," and Leapiness means "How often and how far the melody moves beyond a step." Before generation, the control summary can state the selected values alongside the existing phrase request; after generation, a "Why this setting?" disclosure should show the applied level, active-note and deferred-resolution counts by measure, leap budget used, recoveries, and the endpoint class. It should not show a pseudo-scientific tension number or claim to score performance. Action-linked, partial explanations are more useful than exhaustive model internals because they let a person connect an instruction to a result ([Google PAIR](https://pair.withgoogle.com/guidebook-v2/chapters/explainability-trust/)). The result contract already permits musical and validation annotations, so this policy can travel with the immutable concert-pitch timeline rather than live only in UI state ([Generator contract](../../specs/generator-contract.md)).

When a requested character has little room to act, report the request, the one or two binding named constraints, the applied policy, and its phrase-specific consequence. For example: "`Edge` tension kept intervals mostly stepwise because Leapiness is `Smooth`; it used later diatonic resolution within the existing rhythm." Or: "`Angular` leapiness was limited by High register and a falling contour; the phrase uses the largest downward moves that preserve recovery." Such wording preserves agency and makes the result inspectable without calling valid constraints an error. It also matches the product's explicit, whole-phrase generation model: changes affect only the next Generate or Regenerate request, do not learn from the player, and do not imply playback, accompaniment, scoring, continuation, or persistence ([Generate-phrase intent](../../ui_ux/flows/generate-phrase/intent.md)).

Implementation should assert the policy on every returned phrase: all source pitches remain within the selected active range and resolved register window; all neutral notes remain diatonic; jazz chromatic notes are authorized and immediately resolved; measure duration totals four beats; each bar has a metrical anchor; leap caps, budgets, arpeggio-run limits, and recoveries hold; no unresolved tension figure ends the phrase; and the final endpoint is open. Log the selected levels and applied counts for deterministic test fixtures, not as user-visible evaluation. The numerical budgets and the perceptual distance between named levels remain hypotheses: the cited work validates controllable contributors and constraint-based steering, not these exact four-bar probabilities. Audition representative phrases with players while holding all other controls constant, then revise only the per-profile policy values rather than changing the player-facing control contracts.

## Conclusion

This design makes tension a phrase-level narrative and leapiness a physically and melodically accountable shape control. Their defaults, `Lift` and `Shaped`, produce forward movement and occasional profile without presuming that either rhythmic busyness, high notes, or random large intervals equals expressive tension. More importantly, the hierarchy turns a difficult generative-music problem into an inspectable promise: users choose the musical boundaries first, then the generator finds the most compelling legal phrase inside them.

The open-ending requirement changes the usual “rise then tonic release” model into a more appropriate rehearsal prompt: local tensions resolve cleanly, while the phrase leaves a musically coherent door open for the player. That distinction keeps the research useful without importing accompaniment-dependent harmonic claims or contradicting the first-release scope.
