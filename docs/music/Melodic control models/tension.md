# Controllable melodic/rhythmic tension models for a browser-based algorithmic melody generator

> Historical research note. The evidence remains useful, but tonic-only stability and a stable/tonic measure-4 landing are superseded by [Harmony Planner Policy](../harmony-planner-policy.md): stability is relative to the active chord, every local figure resolves, and every first-release endpoint remains open.

## Which melodic, rhythmic, harmonic, and registral factors are accepted contributors to perceived tension and release?

### Takeaway
Perceived musical tension is multi-cue and time-varying: tonal stability/attraction, melodic expectation, rhythmic activity and accent placement, and pitch height can all contribute. In this release, the most defensible controllable cues are diatonic tonal instability and delayed melodic resolution; rhythmic activity should be a constrained secondary cue because the product already exposes the most audible rhythmic parameters.

### Cited Findings
- Lerdahl and Krumhansl's tested tonal-tension model combines prolongational structure, tonal pitch-space distance, surface tension, and attraction, and reports that its predictions can be compared with listeners' continuously reported tension in tonal excerpts. This supports treating tension as a trajectory, not an isolated note score. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1525/mp.2007.24.4.329)
- Lerdahl distinguishes tension from tonal distance and tension from attraction: instability, proximity, and directed motion jointly shape melodic attraction. This supports a generator that raises tension by delaying a stable destination, then releases it by arriving stepwise on it. [Source](https://doi.org/10.2307/40286174)
- Farbood's parametric temporal model experimentally treats pitch height, loudness, tempo, onset frequency, and harmonic consonance as manipulable contributors to continuous musical tension. The result does not establish a universal fixed weight for every genre, but it is strong evidence that tension is jointly shaped by surface and tonal cues. [Source](https://online.ucpress.edu/mp/article/30/2/121/62827/A-Parametric-Temporal-Model-of-Musical-Tension)
- A computational/experimental study links melodic expectation to perceived tension and expressive timing. This supports using expectation-related note choice and resolution timing as musically meaningful levers rather than relying only on note density. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1037/xhp0000141)
- Predictive expectation is a central account of musical affect: Huron describes expectations generated by learned regularities and how confirmation or violation produces emotional responses. For a diatonic phrase generator, this supports conventional preparation and resolution rather than arbitrary surprise. [Source](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262582780/sweet-anticipation/)
- In experiments on melodic and rhythmic accents, listeners treated accents as salient temporal landmarks and were sensitive to their higher-order relationships. Metric placement therefore changes perceived structure, but this is evidence about accent perception rather than a direct one-dimensional tension scale. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1525/mp.2003.20.4.431)
- In groove-music listening experiments, syncopation had an inverted-U relationship with pleasure and wanting to move. More syncopation is therefore not reliably synonymous with more tension or better output; extreme settings can reduce the desired response. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0094446)
- An information-dynamic model of melody finds that both learned statistical expectations and local melodic context influence expectation. This supports calculating a lightweight relative-surprise score from scale-degree/interval probabilities, but not presenting that statistic to users as the control. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.113.4.786)

### Inferences
- **Melodic factors.** Larger or less locally expected moves, directional reversal after a leap, and delayed arrival at a stable scale degree are suitable internal tension signals. For this product, use the existing leapiness setting as a constraint and vary only *where* its allowed intervals occur; do not make the Tension control silently increase interval size. This follows the expectation literature and avoids duplicate ownership of leap size. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.113.4.786)
- **Rhythmic factors.** Onset density, off-beat accents, and delayed resolution across a metrically strong position are plausible tension cues. Only the last should be owned by a new tension feature; syncopation and silence already own accent displacement and event density. [Source](https://online.ucpress.edu/mp/article/30/2/121/62827/A-Parametric-Temporal-Model-of-Musical-Tension) [Source](https://doi.org/10.1525/mp.2003.20.4.431)
- **Superseded implementation inference.** The fixed tonic/pedal model predates the adopted harmony planner. First release evaluates stability against the active diatonic chord: chord tones are candidate targets, while collection members outside that chord may be active when they have a complete local resolution.
- **Registral factors.** Pitch height is a validated surface-tension cue, but it should remain exclusively controlled by the existing register setting. Tension may choose more active degrees within the selected register but must not shift the phrase's octave center or range. [Source](https://online.ucpress.edu/mp/article/30/2/121/62827/A-Parametric-Temporal-Model-of-Musical-Tension)

### Gaps
- The cited models were not validated specifically on four-bar tenor-sax phrases with no accompaniment, so numeric weights and exact default probabilities need listening tests with representative generated phrases. [Source](https://online.ucpress.edu/mp/article/30/2/121/62827/A-Parametric-Temporal-Model-of-Musical-Tension)
- I found no source that validates a universal four-level UI label set for musical tension. The labels below are product-language recommendations, not experimental categories.

## Which factors can be exposed as 3-4 discrete user-facing levels without making an opaque control?

### Takeaway
Expose one four-level phrase-level **Tension arc** control, not separate controls for all contributing cues. Its observable contract should be increasingly active diatonic targets and later, more emphatic resolution; the existing syncopation, silence, register, leapiness, and arpeggio-chaos controls retain ownership of their own dimensions.

### Cited Findings
- The tonal-tension literature models rises and falls over the course of a passage, rather than assigning a categorical emotion to each pitch. A phrase-level arc is therefore closer to the underlying phenomenon than a per-note "tense" toggle. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1525/mp.2007.24.4.329)
- Farbood's work supports independent manipulation of several cues. It does not imply that a consumer UI should expose every cue independently; its multi-factor construction is evidence for keeping the UI's causal mapping explicit. [Source](https://online.ucpress.edu/mp/article/30/2/121/62827/A-Parametric-Temporal-Model-of-Musical-Tension)
- Learned and contextual melodic expectation both matter, so naming an internal probability, entropy, or tonal-distance algorithm as a user setting would conceal rather than explain the musical result. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.113.4.786)

### Inferences
- **Recommended control:** `Tension arc` with four labels: `Settled`, `Lift`, `Build`, and `Edge`. Default: `Lift`. These labels describe an audible phrase behavior, avoid falsely promising a particular emotion, and leave room for future profile-specific mappings.
- **Superseded detail:** The original recommendations required a stable/tonic measure-4 landing and classified fixed degrees `1/3/5` as stable. Do not implement those endings or that coarse partition. Use the chord-relative [abstract tension requirements](../harmony-planner-policy.md#abstract-tension-requirements) and open endpoint in Harmony Planner Policy.
- Do not expose separate `harmonic tension`, `rhythmic tension`, or `register tension` sliders in v1. They either require absent context (a progression), duplicate present controls, or combine cues whose relationship to tension is context-dependent. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1525/mp.2007.24.4.329) [Source](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0094446)

### Gaps
- Superseded: the adopted first-release planner already calculates stability against the current diatonic chord and voice-leading target. The old 1/3/5 versus 2/4/6/7 partition remains here only to document the research path. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1525/mp.2007.24.4.329)
- Whether `Edge` is desirable for the established neutral/jazz profile is a product/listening-test question; the research supports controllable expectation and attraction, not a genre-specific preference threshold. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1037/xhp0000141)

## How should tension avoid overlapping or fighting the existing syncopation, silence, register, leapiness, and arpeggio-chaos controls?

### Takeaway
Make Tension a scheduler with a narrow ownership boundary: it chooses *when* the already-permitted material is most unstable and when it resolves. It must respect the note/rhythm/range candidates produced by existing controls, use a deterministic budget, and never add chromatic harmony or override an explicit user setting.

### Cited Findings
- Tension models are multi-cue, so changing several surface cues at once can confound what a listener hears as the cause of a rise in tension. The experimental approach of independently controlling parameters is a useful engineering precedent for clear ownership boundaries. [Source](https://online.ucpress.edu/mp/article/30/2/121/62827/A-Parametric-Temporal-Model-of-Musical-Tension)
- Listeners use melodic and rhythmic accents together as temporal structure, so independently generated pitch and rhythm decisions still need a final phrase-level coordination pass. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1525/mp.2003.20.4.431)
- More syncopation is not a monotonic tension dial in groove experiments, reinforcing the case against having a Tension macro automatically turn up syncopation. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0094446)

### Inferences
- **Pipeline and precedence:** generate rhythm from `syncopation` and `silence`; generate the allowed pitch pool/range from `register`, `leapiness`, and `arpeggio chaos`; then apply `Tension arc` as a constrained note-selection and resolution scheduler. Existing explicit controls win on conflict. Open-endpoint and local-resolution validation wins over all tension levels.
- **Syncopation:** Tension must not create extra off-beat onsets or change onset locations. It may select an active degree at an already-existing off-beat onset and reserve its resolution for the next existing metrically strong onset. If there is no valid resolution onset, reduce that local tension request instead of altering rhythm.
- **Silence:** Tension must not fill rests or remove user-requested rests. At `Build`/`Edge`, it may place a resolution immediately after an existing rest only if that event already exists; silence remains phrasing space, not an automatic source of suspense.
- **Register:** Tension must not raise the octave center, expand the permitted range, or force a high-note climax. The phrase may feel more tense through scale-degree instability within the selected tenor-sax register.
- **Leapiness:** Tension must not increase the maximum interval or leap probability. At most, it may position a leap the user has already allowed before an active-degree resolution; if the existing leapiness setting disallows it, choose stepwise motion.
- **Arpeggio chaos:** Tension must not increase arpeggio rate, chord-outline randomness, or non-diatonic material. When chaos is high, prefer fewer simultaneous surprise mechanisms while retaining the open endpoint; exact quantitative interaction remains listening-test calibration.
- **Jazz-profile chromaticism:** keep chromatic approaches/enclosures behind the profile's existing bounded rule. A chromatic note may be used only as its already-authorized one-step approach/enclosure into a diatonic target, must resolve immediately as prescribed by that rule, and must not be added merely to satisfy `Build` or `Edge`. No secondary dominants, substitutions, or generated chord tones are introduced.
- **Observable acceptance checks:** for every generated phrase, log the selected level, per-measure active-role count, deferred-resolution count, and endpoint class. Assert that all notes remain in the diatonic pool except authorized Jazz ornaments, all local obligations resolve, all explicit controls hold, and the final sounding event passes the open-ending rule.

### Gaps
- No cited study supplies probability caps for a browser generator's active degrees or delayed resolutions. Start with conservative, profile-tuned values and validate with A/B listening sessions that hold all existing controls constant. [Source](https://online.ucpress.edu/mp/article/30/2/121/62827/A-Parametric-Temporal-Model-of-Musical-Tension)
- With no playback or accompaniment, users cannot audit the full harmonic meaning of a line. The UI should therefore describe this setting as phrase "Tension arc," not as chord progression or harmony authoring.
