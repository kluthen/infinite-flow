# Deterministic register-window policies for conservative saxophone and C-keyboard profiles

> Historical research note with adopted outcome. Placeholder references to future/undecided tenor and alto endpoints are superseded: first release adopts tenor `46-70` and alto `51-75`, with exact windows listed in [Adopted Saxophone Range and Display Profiles](../saxophone-profile-ranges.md).

## How should a multi-octave active range be divided into overlapping or non-overlapping register targets without stranding contour/recovery?

### Takeaway
Use three overlapping hard windows, each at least an octave wide, derived from the profile's approved concert-MIDI endpoints. Do not divide the range into three disjoint thirds: the overlap is the route that lets an arch, rising, falling, or wandering phrase begin and end near a register boundary while retaining room for an allowed leap and its mandatory contrary recovery.

### Cited Findings
- Infinite Flow defines a phrase as one four-measure musical object and requires register, contour, range, leapiness, and recovery to remain higher-priority constraints than expressive ranking. [Project melodic-control model](../melodic-control-models.md)
- The existing melodic policy requires a direction change after a leap larger than a fourth and requires consecutive leaps to outline a triad; it explicitly frames these as Bach-chorale-derived conventions with known later exceptions. [Music Theory for the 21st-Century Classroom: Rules of Melody](https://musictheory.pugetsound.edu/mt21c/RulesOfMelody.html)
- The project operationalizes recovery as an opposite-direction 1-2-semitone step or 3-4-semitone contrary motion after a large leap or recognized chord-tone run, and requires the planner to reserve active range for contour and recovery before accepting an outward leap. [Project leap-recovery rule](../../rule_leap_recovery.atom.md)
- The C MIDI-keyboard profile is already specified as concert MIDI 48 through 72 inclusive, a 24-semitone span; MIDI note 60 is conventionally middle C, although octave labels differ between manufacturers. [First-release specification](../../../specs/first-release-master-spec.md); [Inspired Acoustics MIDI table](https://www.inspiredacoustics.com/en/MIDI_note_numbers_and_center_frequencies)
- Tenor saxophone is a B-flat transposing instrument and alto saxophone an E-flat transposing instrument, while a piano supplies the concert-pitch reference; this supports storing a common concert-pitch range policy rather than deriving it from displayed notation. [Yamaha: The saxophone is a transposing instrument](https://www.yamaha.com/en/musical_instrument_guide/saxophone/play/play003.html)

### Inferences
- Define a profile active range as inclusive integers `[lo, hi]`, with span `W = hi - lo`. Adopted instances are C keyboard `[48,72]`, B-flat tenor `[46,70]`, and E-flat alto `[51,75]`. [First-release specification](../../../specs/first-release-master-spec.md); [Project instrument-profile entity](../../entity_instrument_profile.atom.md)
- Derive one common policy version, `overlapping-three-window-v1`, for all profiles. Let `S = max(12, ceil(W / 2))`, measured in semitones; construct inclusive targets as `Low = [lo, lo + S]`, `Middle = [lo + floor((W - S) / 2), lo + floor((W - S) / 2) + S]`, and `High = [hi - S, hi]`. Every target therefore holds at least 13 chromatic positions, and adjacent targets overlap rather than strand boundary pitches. This is a deterministic product-policy inference from the project's contour and recovery requirements, not a published universal register taxonomy. [Project melodic-control model](../melodic-control-models.md)
- With `S = 12`, the adopted windows are keyboard `48-60`, `54-66`, `60-72`; tenor `46-58`, `52-64`, `58-70`; and alto `51-63`, `57-69`, `63-75`. Adjacent windows share seven pitch positions. [First-release specification](../../../specs/first-release-master-spec.md)
- Treat the selected window as a hard pitch filter for a complete phrase, in addition to the profile active range. Before notes are committed, the planner must test the complete selected window against source pitch range, profile grammar, selected contour, leap budget, recovery obligations, and cadence target. It must reject an outward candidate if its required recovery and remaining contour target have no legal route; it must not silently widen the window or relax recovery. This converts the pedagogical leap convention into a tractable feasibility constraint rather than a post-hoc preference. [Music Theory for the 21st-Century Classroom: Rules of Melody](https://musictheory.pugetsound.edu/mt21c/RulesOfMelody.html); [Project leap-recovery rule](../../rule_leap_recovery.atom.md)
- Keep all three windows the same width. A narrow disjoint-third design would make `Low` and `High` materially less usable for octave-level jumps and the recovery they require, while asymmetric edge windows would make a register label mean different available melodic freedom. The proposed 50%-of-range width leaves at least a quarter-range overlap on a two-octave profile and scales predictably if an approved profile later becomes wider. [Project melodic-control model](../melodic-control-models.md)
- Test the policy as pure integer data: validate `W >= 24`; validate every derived endpoint lies in `[lo, hi]`; validate each window has span `>= 12`; validate adjacent windows intersect; and, for C `[48,72]`, assert the exact three windows above. Add generated-fixture assertions that every sounding source pitch lies in both the profile range and the resolved register window, all selected hard constraints hold, and a failed feasibility search reports failure rather than emitting an out-of-window fallback. [First-release specification](../../../specs/first-release-master-spec.md)

### Gaps
- No source found establishes a universal numeric division of a saxophone's practical range into Low, Middle, and High for all players, mouthpieces, styles, and dynamics. The window proportions are deliberately an inspectable generator policy to audition with players, not instrumental-pedagogy fact.
- Superseded: tenor and alto endpoints are adopted as stated above. Player testing may inform future profile variants but cannot silently change first-release values.

## How should Varying choose a phrase-wide policy deterministically from a seed?

### Takeaway
Keep `Varying` in an outer session/generation intent, not in the core planner request. Step 3 supplies validated windows, canonical `Low, Middle, High` identities/order, and stable register-stream key primitives. Step 7 proves complete-phrase eligibility by running the bounded planner under separately resolved candidate core requests, then deterministically selects among successful windows and retains that resolved request/result pair.

### Cited Findings
- The first-release specification already defines `Varying` as a player-selectable request value whose seeded planner chooses one eligible non-varying policy for the whole phrase; it cannot switch policy by measure or note, nor relax other hard constraints. [First-release specification](../../../specs/first-release-master-spec.md)
- The project requires a given complete request and seed to reproduce the phrase, while display-transposition and theme changes cannot alter generation. [Project seeded-reproduction rule](../../rule_seeded_phrase_reproduction.atom.md)
- Constraint-based sequence generation can steer sequence generation under explicit requirements rather than relying on unconstrained next-event selection. [Pachet and Roy, Markov Constraints: Steerable Generation of Markov Sequences](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jc.2010.07.004)
- A four-measure phrase is a coherent formal unit rather than four unrelated bar-level choices, supporting one phrase-wide register resolution. [Music Theory for the 21st-Century Classroom: Phrase](https://musictheory.pugetsound.edu/mt21c/PhraseSection.html)

### Inferences
- Define outer `GenerationIntent` as validated profile identity/data, player register choice, normalized generation controls, and seed. Define core `GenerationRequest` as the stable generator contract with one explicit active range, `resolvedRegister`, and `resolvedWindow`; it never carries unresolved `Varying`.
- Step 3 validates profile/window data and returns candidate descriptors in fixed canonical order `[Low, Middle, High]`, each with stable identity/index. It also defines `registerTieKey = hash32("infinite-flow/register-window/v1" || seed || canonicalIntentWithoutDisplayState || windowIdentity)`. It does not run harmony/rhythm/pitch planning and cannot claim complete-phrase eligibility.
- For explicit Low/Middle/High, construct one resolved core request directly. For `Varying`, Step 7 constructs one candidate core request per structurally valid descriptor and runs the same bounded complete planner under each hard window. A window is eligible only when that pipeline yields an independently validated complete phrase; `search_limit_exceeded` is indeterminate, not ineligible. [First-release specification](../../../specs/first-release-master-spec.md); [Project melodic-control model](../melodic-control-models.md)
- After every candidate window has a determinate outcome, collect successful resolved request/result pairs and select the lowest unsigned `registerTieKey`; use canonical window index only for a hash collision. This uses the register stream independently of pitch draws, selects only from complete-phrase-eligible policies, and remains seed-sensitive/reproducible.
- Persist or return `requestedRegister: Varying`, selected `resolvedRegister`, `windowPolicyVersion`, `resolvedWindow`, seed, and the retained validated result. Identical outer intent/seed reproduces successful-window outcomes and selection. Display/theme state never enters candidate construction, feasibility, or the key.
- Full-pipeline tests belong to Step 7 and cover all successful-window subsets, each singleton, none, and search-limit indeterminacy. Step 3 tests only validation, fixed-register resolution, canonical identities/order, stable key fixtures, and independence from display state.

### Gaps
- No cited source prescribes a particular hash function, stream-label convention, or key ordering. Those choices must be fixed as a versioned engineering contract and changed only through an explicit reproducibility migration.
- Eligibility can vary by tonal collection, contour, cadence, profile grammar, leapiness, and recovery. Fixture tests should therefore include representative combinations rather than asserting an unsupported universal distribution among Low, Middle, and High. [Project melodic-control model](../melodic-control-models.md)

## What policy keeps profile-specific ranges decoupled from planner and display logic?

### Takeaway
Make the profile a validated declarative data record that supplies concert-MIDI endpoints and the versioned window-policy parameters. The planner consumes only the resolved range/window policy; notation consumes only the immutable concert timeline plus an independently selected display convention.

### Cited Findings
- The project architecture already specifies an instrument profile with a stable identity, concert-pitch playable range, register-window policy, and metadata; it requires invalid profile data to be rejected before consumers use it. [Project instrument-profile entity](../../entity_instrument_profile.atom.md)
- The generation boundary is specified to receive active concert range and register-window policy explicitly from the selected validated profile, without profile-specific melodic, harmonic, or rhythm branches. [Project phrase-generation profile-policy module](../../module_phrase_generation_profile_policy.atom.md)
- The notation contract specifies Concert, B-flat, and E-flat as display conventions only. Changing display cannot mutate the concert-pitch timeline, selected generator profile, range policy, seed, or controls. [Project notation-display rule](../../rule_notation_display_preserves_phrase.atom.md)
- Yamaha distinguishes tenor's B-flat and alto's E-flat transposition from piano concert pitch, reinforcing the separation between generated sounding pitch and written notation. [Yamaha: The saxophone is a transposing instrument](https://www.yamaha.com/en/musical_instrument_guide/saxophone/play/play003.html)

### Inferences
- Use one data shape for every profile, for example: `id`, `concertRange: { minMidi, maxMidi }`, and `registerWindows: { algorithm: "overlapping-three-window-v1", minimumSpan: 12, widthNumerator: 1, widthDenominator: 2 }`. B-flat tenor and E-flat alto differ only in approved profile data; C keyboard uses `{ minMidi: 48, maxMidi: 72 }`. Do not encode transposition, clef, or displayed octave labels into the planner-facing range policy. [Project instrument-profile entity](../../entity_instrument_profile.atom.md); [First-release specification](../../../specs/first-release-master-spec.md)
- Step 3 resolves declarative profile data into planner-neutral candidate descriptors: `{ sourceRange, requestedRegister, candidates: [{ id, canonicalIndex, window }], policyVersion }`. The outer orchestrator creates core requests with concrete `{ resolvedRegister, resolvedWindow }`; individual planners never inspect `profile.id` or unresolved `Varying`. [Project phrase-generation profile-policy module](../../module_phrase_generation_profile_policy.atom.md)
- Keep notation downstream: it maps accepted concert-MIDI events to concert, B-flat, or E-flat written notation after generation. A notation switch neither reruns window derivation nor becomes input to the register-choice hash. [Project notation-display rule](../../rule_notation_display_preserves_phrase.atom.md)
- Validate profile data independently with deterministic tests: `minMidi` and `maxMidi` are integers, `minMidi < maxMidi`, span is at least 24, algorithm name is recognized, the policy derives valid intersecting windows, and no window exceeds `concertRange`. A new physical-instrument decision is then a profile-data review plus these tests, not a change to contour, recovery, profile grammar, planner, or display code. [Project instrument-profile entity](../../entity_instrument_profile.atom.md)
- Keep source pitch range, profile grammar, contour, leapiness, recovery, and cadence as hard constraints during feasibility and final validation. Register policy narrows the candidate pitch domain but grants no exception to any of them; this preserves the project's established priority order and prevents a high-register label from becoming a hidden tension or style control. [Project tension-ranking rule](../../rule_tension_ranking_respects_constraints.atom.md)

### Gaps
- Superseded: ranges and profile-switch validation/regeneration behavior are settled in the master specification and summarized in the current profile policy.
- Player listening tests are still needed to calibrate whether the proposed labels and overlapping proportions feel distinct in the target neutral and jazz presets while all other controls are held constant. The current project research explicitly treats numerical melodic-policy budgets as hypotheses requiring audition. [Project melodic-control model](../melodic-control-models.md)
