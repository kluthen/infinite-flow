# Deterministic register-window policies for conservative saxophone and C-keyboard profiles

## How should a multi-octave active range be divided into overlapping or non-overlapping register targets without stranding contour/recovery?

### Takeaway
Use three overlapping hard windows, each at least an octave wide, derived from the profile's approved concert-MIDI endpoints. Do not divide the range into three disjoint thirds: the overlap is the route that lets an arch, rising, falling, or wandering phrase begin and end near a register boundary while retaining room for an allowed leap and its mandatory contrary recovery.

### Cited Findings
- Infinite Flow defines a phrase as one four-measure musical object and requires register, contour, range, leapiness, and recovery to remain higher-priority constraints than expressive ranking. [Project melodic-control model](../../docs/music/melodic-control-models.md)
- The existing melodic policy requires a direction change after a leap larger than a fourth and requires consecutive leaps to outline a triad; it explicitly frames these as Bach-chorale-derived conventions with known later exceptions. [Music Theory for the 21st-Century Classroom: Rules of Melody](https://musictheory.pugetsound.edu/mt21c/RulesOfMelody.html)
- The project operationalizes recovery as an opposite-direction 1-2-semitone step or 3-4-semitone contrary motion after a large leap or recognized chord-tone run, and requires the planner to reserve active range for contour and recovery before accepting an outward leap. [Project leap-recovery rule](../../docs/rule_leap_recovery.atom.md)
- The C MIDI-keyboard profile is already specified as concert MIDI 48 through 72 inclusive, a 24-semitone span; MIDI note 60 is conventionally middle C, although octave labels differ between manufacturers. [First-release specification](../../docs/first-release-master-spec.md); [Inspired Acoustics MIDI table](https://www.inspiredacoustics.com/en/MIDI_note_numbers_and_center_frequencies)
- Tenor saxophone is a B-flat transposing instrument and alto saxophone an E-flat transposing instrument, while a piano supplies the concert-pitch reference; this supports storing a common concert-pitch range policy rather than deriving it from displayed notation. [Yamaha: The saxophone is a transposing instrument](https://www.yamaha.com/en/musical_instrument_guide/saxophone/play/play003.html)

### Inferences
- Define a profile active range as inclusive integers `[lo, hi]`, with span `W = hi - lo`. Accept a profile only when `W >= 24`; this guarantees every profile covers at least two octaves (25 chromatic pitch positions) without asserting a physical saxophone compass. The approved conservative B-flat-tenor and E-flat-alto endpoints remain profile parameters: `[tenorLo, tenorHi]` and `[altoLo, altoHi]`. The C-keyboard instance is `[48, 72]`. [First-release specification](../../docs/first-release-master-spec.md); [Project instrument-profile entity](../../docs/entity_instrument_profile.atom.md)
- Derive one common policy version, `overlapping-three-window-v1`, for all profiles. Let `S = max(12, ceil(W / 2))`, measured in semitones; construct inclusive targets as `Low = [lo, lo + S]`, `Middle = [lo + floor((W - S) / 2), lo + floor((W - S) / 2) + S]`, and `High = [hi - S, hi]`. Every target therefore holds at least 13 chromatic positions, and adjacent targets overlap rather than strand boundary pitches. This is a deterministic product-policy inference from the project's contour and recovery requirements, not a published universal register taxonomy. [Project melodic-control model](../../docs/music/melodic-control-models.md)
- For the exact two-octave C profile, `S = 12` and the testable windows are `Low 48-60`, `Middle 54-66`, and `High 60-72`. Low/Middle and Middle/High each share seven pitch positions; Low and High meet at MIDI 60. The same formula supplies the future tenor and alto values only after their approved endpoints are entered. [First-release specification](../../docs/first-release-master-spec.md)
- Treat the selected window as a hard pitch filter for a complete phrase, in addition to the profile active range. Before notes are committed, the planner must test the complete selected window against source pitch range, profile grammar, selected contour, leap budget, recovery obligations, and cadence target. It must reject an outward candidate if its required recovery and remaining contour target have no legal route; it must not silently widen the window or relax recovery. This converts the pedagogical leap convention into a tractable feasibility constraint rather than a post-hoc preference. [Music Theory for the 21st-Century Classroom: Rules of Melody](https://musictheory.pugetsound.edu/mt21c/RulesOfMelody.html); [Project leap-recovery rule](../../docs/rule_leap_recovery.atom.md)
- Keep all three windows the same width. A narrow disjoint-third design would make `Low` and `High` materially less usable for octave-level jumps and the recovery they require, while asymmetric edge windows would make a register label mean different available melodic freedom. The proposed 50%-of-range width leaves at least a quarter-range overlap on a two-octave profile and scales predictably if an approved profile later becomes wider. [Project melodic-control model](../../docs/music/melodic-control-models.md)
- Test the policy as pure integer data: validate `W >= 24`; validate every derived endpoint lies in `[lo, hi]`; validate each window has span `>= 12`; validate adjacent windows intersect; and, for C `[48,72]`, assert the exact three windows above. Add generated-fixture assertions that every sounding source pitch lies in both the profile range and the resolved register window, all selected hard constraints hold, and a failed feasibility search reports failure rather than emitting an out-of-window fallback. [First-release specification](../../docs/first-release-master-spec.md)

### Gaps
- No source found establishes a universal numeric division of a saxophone's practical range into Low, Middle, and High for all players, mouthpieces, styles, and dynamics. The window proportions are deliberately an inspectable generator policy to audition with players, not instrumental-pedagogy fact.
- The exact conservative active endpoints for the future B-flat-tenor and E-flat-alto profiles remain undecided in the project; this note intentionally does not select them. [First-release specification](../../docs/first-release-master-spec.md)

## How should Varying choose a phrase-wide policy deterministically from a seed?

### Takeaway
Resolve `Varying` once, before note planning, from the ordered list of actually feasible Low/Middle/High policies. Select by a named hash-derived register stream, not by consuming the melody planner's random draws, then retain the resolved window ID with the result for exact reproduction and inspection.

### Cited Findings
- The first-release specification already defines `Varying` as a player-selectable request value whose seeded planner chooses one eligible non-varying policy for the whole phrase; it cannot switch policy by measure or note, nor relax other hard constraints. [First-release specification](../../docs/first-release-master-spec.md)
- The project requires a given complete request and seed to reproduce the phrase, while display-transposition and theme changes cannot alter generation. [Project seeded-reproduction rule](../../docs/rule_seeded_phrase_reproduction.atom.md)
- Constraint-based sequence generation can steer sequence generation under explicit requirements rather than relying on unconstrained next-event selection. [Pachet and Roy, Markov Constraints: Steerable Generation of Markov Sequences](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jc.2010.07.004)
- A four-measure phrase is a coherent formal unit rather than four unrelated bar-level choices, supporting one phrase-wide register resolution. [Music Theory for the 21st-Century Classroom: Phrase](https://musictheory.pugetsound.edu/mt21c/PhraseSection.html)

### Inferences
- Resolve candidates in the fixed canonical order `[Low, Middle, High]`. For each candidate, run the same bounded feasibility search used for explicit register selection, with the derived window included as a hard constraint. Its result is `eligible` only if at least one complete phrase satisfies every hard constraint; do not infer eligibility merely from window width. [First-release specification](../../docs/first-release-master-spec.md); [Project melodic-control model](../../docs/music/melodic-control-models.md)
- Canonicalize the generation-affecting request excluding the literal register value and excluding display/theme state. Compute `registerChoice = hash32("infinite-flow/register-window/v1" || seed || canonicalRequestWithoutRegister)`, then choose `eligible[registerChoice mod eligible.length]`. Use a specified stable hash and byte encoding in the implementation; do not rely on language runtime hashes or object-key order. The selected ID must be included in the subsequent planner seed derivation, for example `plannerSeed = hash32("infinite-flow/planner/v1" || seed || canonicalRequest || resolvedWindowId)`. [Project seeded-reproduction rule](../../docs/rule_seeded_phrase_reproduction.atom.md)
- Use independent named streams for register resolution and melody planning. This prevents an implementation refactor that adds a melody tie-break draw from changing which register `Varying` selects for the same request and seed. It also makes a fixture's failure local: tests can assert the resolved register before diagnosing the note sequence. This is an engineering determinism recommendation, not a claim from the cited composition literature. [Pachet and Roy, Markov Constraints: Steerable Generation of Markov Sequences](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jc.2010.07.004)
- Persist or return `requestedRegister: Varying`, `resolvedRegister: Low|Middle|High`, `windowPolicyVersion`, `resolvedWindow: [min,max]`, and the seed. Explicit `Low`, `Middle`, and `High` set `resolvedRegister` to themselves without the choice hash. This makes the result explainable while preserving the user-facing request. [Project seeded-reproduction rule](../../docs/rule_seeded_phrase_reproduction.atom.md)
- Test fixed seeds against a fixture table that covers every eligible-set shape: all three eligible, each pair, each singleton, and none. Assert that the selected result is in the eligible set; identical inputs reproduce the resolved window and phrase; changing only display mode does not alter either; and no-eligible produces the specified generation failure. [First-release specification](../../docs/first-release-master-spec.md)

### Gaps
- No cited source prescribes a particular hash function, stream-label convention, or modulo mapping. Those choices must be fixed as a versioned engineering contract and changed only through an explicit reproducibility migration.
- Eligibility can vary by tonal collection, contour, cadence, profile grammar, leapiness, and recovery. Fixture tests should therefore include representative combinations rather than asserting an unsupported universal distribution among Low, Middle, and High. [Project melodic-control model](../../docs/music/melodic-control-models.md)

## What policy keeps profile-specific ranges decoupled from planner and display logic?

### Takeaway
Make the profile a validated declarative data record that supplies concert-MIDI endpoints and the versioned window-policy parameters. The planner consumes only the resolved range/window policy; notation consumes only the immutable concert timeline plus an independently selected display convention.

### Cited Findings
- The project architecture already specifies an instrument profile with a stable identity, concert-pitch playable range, register-window policy, and metadata; it requires invalid profile data to be rejected before consumers use it. [Project instrument-profile entity](../../docs/entity_instrument_profile.atom.md)
- The generation boundary is specified to receive active concert range and register-window policy explicitly from the selected validated profile, without profile-specific melodic, harmonic, or rhythm branches. [Project phrase-generation profile-policy module](../../docs/module_phrase_generation_profile_policy.atom.md)
- The notation contract specifies Concert, B-flat, and E-flat as display conventions only. Changing display cannot mutate the concert-pitch timeline, selected generator profile, range policy, seed, or controls. [Project notation-display rule](../../docs/rule_notation_display_preserves_phrase.atom.md)
- Yamaha distinguishes tenor's B-flat and alto's E-flat transposition from piano concert pitch, reinforcing the separation between generated sounding pitch and written notation. [Yamaha: The saxophone is a transposing instrument](https://www.yamaha.com/en/musical_instrument_guide/saxophone/play/play003.html)

### Inferences
- Use one data shape for every profile, for example: `id`, `concertRange: { minMidi, maxMidi }`, and `registerWindows: { algorithm: "overlapping-three-window-v1", minimumSpan: 12, widthNumerator: 1, widthDenominator: 2 }`. B-flat tenor and E-flat alto differ only in approved profile data; C keyboard uses `{ minMidi: 48, maxMidi: 72 }`. Do not encode transposition, clef, or displayed octave labels into the planner-facing range policy. [Project instrument-profile entity](../../docs/entity_instrument_profile.atom.md); [First-release specification](../../docs/first-release-master-spec.md)
- At request validation, resolve declarative profile data into a planner-neutral value object: `{ sourceRange, registerRequest, registerCandidates, resolvedRegister?, resolvedWindow, policyVersion }`. Planner functions accept this object and never inspect `profile.id`. This preserves a single feasibility and candidate-selection implementation for tenor, alto, and keyboard profiles. [Project phrase-generation profile-policy module](../../docs/module_phrase_generation_profile_policy.atom.md)
- Keep notation downstream: it maps accepted concert-MIDI events to concert, B-flat, or E-flat written notation after generation. A notation switch neither reruns window derivation nor becomes input to the register-choice hash. [Project notation-display rule](../../docs/rule_notation_display_preserves_phrase.atom.md)
- Validate profile data independently with deterministic tests: `minMidi` and `maxMidi` are integers, `minMidi < maxMidi`, span is at least 24, algorithm name is recognized, the policy derives valid intersecting windows, and no window exceeds `concertRange`. A new physical-instrument decision is then a profile-data review plus these tests, not a change to contour, recovery, profile grammar, planner, or display code. [Project instrument-profile entity](../../docs/entity_instrument_profile.atom.md)
- Keep source pitch range, profile grammar, contour, leapiness, recovery, and cadence as hard constraints during feasibility and final validation. Register policy narrows the candidate pitch domain but grants no exception to any of them; this preserves the project's established priority order and prevents a high-register label from becoming a hidden tension or style control. [Project tension-ranking rule](../../docs/rule_tension_ranking_respects_constraints.atom.md)

### Gaps
- The project has not yet settled the exact B-flat-tenor and E-flat-alto active concert ranges or profile-switch behavior after a phrase exists. This design supports either decision but does not determine it. [First-release specification](../../docs/first-release-master-spec.md)
- Player listening tests are still needed to calibrate whether the proposed labels and overlapping proportions feel distinct in the target neutral and jazz presets while all other controls are held constant. The current project research explicitly treats numerical melodic-policy budgets as hypotheses requiring audition. [Project melodic-control model](../../docs/music/melodic-control-models.md)
