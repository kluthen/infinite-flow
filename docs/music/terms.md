# Music Terms

Status: draft v0.1

## Harmony

- **Tonic:** the home chord or pitch area; it creates rest or arrival.
- **Predominant:** a diatonic chord function that leads away from tonic toward dominant motion in tonal music.
- **Dominant:** a chord function that creates directed pull back toward tonic in tonal music. It is not required in modal music.
- **Cadence/endpoint:** the end behavior of a phrase. In tonal practice, tonic arrival commonly closes and dominant arrival remains open. First-release modal/static openness is a product continuation rule and does not require dominant harmony.
- **Chord tone:** a pitch belonging to the active chord.
- **Non-chord tone:** a pitch from the selected scale that is not part of the active chord. Its approach, metric position, duration, and resolution determine whether it feels stable or tense.

## Melody

- **Passing tone:** a stepwise pitch between two other pitches moving in the same direction.
- **Neighbor tone:** a step away from a stable pitch followed by a step back to it.
- **Appoggiatura:** a tense pitch entered by leap that resolves by step.
- **Contour:** the overall rising, falling, arching, or wandering shape of a phrase.
- **Register:** the selected Low, Middle, or High whole-phrase window inside an instrument profile's adopted active concert range.
- **Leapiness:** the tendency to move by steps versus larger melodic intervals.

## Rhythm

- **Subdivision:** how each beat is divided, such as quarters, eighths, or sixteenths.
- **Syncopation:** emphasis or onset on a weak part of the beat, or a sustain that crosses a stronger beat.
- **Metrical anchor:** broadly, an event that reinforces the perceived meter. For first-release validation it means exactly a note event starting at bar-relative tick `0` or `8` (beat 1 or 3), either a fresh attack or a tie continuation carrying a note through that beat; a rest at that tick does not count.
- **Silence density:** the amount of intentional rests; it creates breathing space and shapes musical gestures.

## Jazz Extension

- **Chromatic approach:** a note one semitone above or below a target, resolving directly to it.
- **Chromatic enclosure:** two short notes surrounding a target, followed by resolution to that target.
