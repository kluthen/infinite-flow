**Words used below.** A **triad** is a chord of three notes stacked in thirds (every other note of
the scale), such as C–E–G. A **seventh chord** is a triad with one more note on top, a seventh above
the chord's root: C–E–G–B, written Cmaj7. The **Seventh chords level** (the level) is the new
setting, Off, Sometimes, Favored or Strongly favored, saying how often the generator prefers a
chord's seventh form to its plain triad. A **harmony event** is one span of one chord in a phrase.
**Close position** means a chord's notes packed inside one octave. A **chord tone** is a note of the
chord sounding at that moment. A **tense note** is a scale note outside the final chord that the
next phrase resolves on its first note. The **replay key** is the text key printed to the console
from which a phrase can be replayed. The **data version** is the number of the layout the app's
data has on the device. Music terms are in the [lexicon](../music/lexicon.md).

### A1. `req_session_backing_track` 0.2 → 0.3: meaning changes

STABLE BUSINESS, on the contract. From Part D §D7.3.

- In plain words, today: the backing's block chords always play three notes, the highest triad
  packed inside one octave with its top note at or below E4. Proposed: they play every note of the
  chord the phrase uses, so a seventh chord sounds as four notes, packed the same way under E4. The
  bass, the rhythm of the attacks, the determinism and the no-download rule are unchanged.
- LOGIC, second bullet: "the chords play the highest close-position triad whose top note is at or
  below E4, attacked at each chord change and on each downbeat, never on beat 3." becomes:

  > the chords play every note of the chord the phrase uses, three for a triad and four for a
  > seventh chord, in the highest close position (the chord's notes packed inside one octave) whose
  > top note is at or below E4, attacked at each chord change and on each downbeat, never on beat 3.

- EXPECTATION, a new second item: "- The block chords hold every note of the phrase's chord, three
  for a triad and four for a seventh chord, in the highest close position whose top note is at or
  below E4."
- INTENT and TECHNICAL INTERFACE: unchanged.
- Why: the backing is the only part that sounds the whole chord; if it left the seventh out, the
  player would read G7 and hear G.

### A2. `rule_open_phrase_ending` 0.4 → 0.5: meaning changes (first option)

STABLE BUSINESS, on the contract. From Part D §D6.3 and §D12 item 2, first option: what ships unless
the listening check approves the seventh.

- In plain words, today: a phrase that ends on the dominant (the degree-5 chord) ends on "a dominant
  chord tone", and a joined phrase may end on a tense note, "a scale tone outside the final chord".
  Once chords can hold a seventh, both phrases would quietly change meaning. Proposed: every chord
  this rule names is read as its triad, so the final notes allowed are exactly today's: a phrase
  never ends on its last chord's seventh, except that a joined phrase may end on it as a tense note,
  which the next phrase resolves (F over G7, resolved to E).
- LOGIC: "When generated tonal motion ends on dominant, the final event is a dominant chord tone."
  becomes "When generated tonal motion ends on dominant, the final event is a note of the dominant
  triad (its root, third or fifth), never the dominant chord's seventh."
- LOGIC, after "never an asserted tonic closure.", a new sentence: "Every chord this rule names is
  read as its triad, also when the phrase plays it as a seventh chord: a final chord's seventh is
  never the final event of a phrase generated without carry-in."
- LOGIC: "a tense note (a scale tone outside the final chord)" becomes "a tense note (a scale tone
  outside the final chord's triad, the final chord's seventh included)".
- EXPECTATION, appended: "A phrase generated without carry-in never ends on its final chord's
  seventh; a phrase generated with carry-in may end on it as a tense note, which the next phrase
  generated with that carry-in resolves on its first note."
- INTENT and TECHNICAL INTERFACE: unchanged.
- Why: in jazz the seventh is a stable note, but left hanging at the very end of a phrase it may
  sound open or like a mistake; the owner's listening check (L1) decides later whether it may end a
  phrase.
- **For reference only, not for C1 (gate C2, if L1 approves):** the second option makes the final
  chord's seventh an ending note too, so any phrase may end on it (F over G7, B♭ over C7 in C
  Mixolydian), and a tense note becomes a scale note outside the whole four-note chord. Its exact
  wording is drafted when L1 approves it.
