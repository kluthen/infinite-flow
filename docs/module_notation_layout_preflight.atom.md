# Notation Layout Preflight

## INTENT
Guarantee the four-measure written phrase is drawn legibly in the staff layout the window's width selects, within the window's width and, below the tablet-landscape breakpoint, its height too, and report a phrase that cannot be drawn as one of two distinct typed layout failures: no room for the staff, or notes too wide.

## THE RULE / LOGIC
The staff layout is chosen from the viewport width alone, never from a phrase's content, and one layout is never tried before the other; at Idle it is the layout the window's width selects, and during a practice session it is the layout held at the session's start. At viewport widths at or above the 64rem tablet-landscape breakpoint, the one-system layout draws all four measures on one staff system, with clef, key signature and the 4/4 time signature at its start and chord symbols above their harmonic events. Below 64rem, the split layout draws bars 1-2 on a top system, with clef, key signature and time signature, and bars 3-4 on a bottom system, with clef and key signature repeated and no time signature; this holds for every phrase, however short.

The staff area always reserves two system positions, one above the other, in every state (no phrase yet, loading, ready, failed). Both positions have one block size, set from the viewport and the instruments' written ranges, never from a phrase: a named system height, in engraving units, times the largest scale the viewport allows; below 64rem, a window too short for the staff at the floor instead splits its whole height budget equally between the two positions. The named system height is the same whichever instrument is selected: it holds the tallest ink any one instrument's written range can produce, that range placed on the staff in the instrument's own clef (chord symbols over its highest notes, ledger lines under its lowest, stems, beams, whose widest group spans one register window of the range and so grows with a range wider than two octaves, and the start marker over the first note), counting the three built-in profiles and, once saved, my instrument, with at least about 0.8 of a staff space clear at each end for the tallest-reaching one, and not one instrument's highest ink stacked over another's lowest, which no phrase can reach. My instrument's written range, in its clef, counts like a built-in profile's, whichever instrument is selected: the height is the built-in profiles' height (16 staff spaces), or the tallest ink my instrument's range can produce plus the clearance at each end, rounded up to whole staff spaces, whichever is taller. So a range needing no more height than the tallest built-in profile's changes nothing, and one needing more makes both positions taller for every instrument choice. The same written range can need much more height in one clef than in the other. The height changes only when my instrument is saved or deleted, never from one phrase to the next. Since my instrument's written range lies within its clef's bound (written C2 to C7 in treble clef, written E0 to E5 in bass clef), the shared height is at most 24 staff spaces. A save of my instrument that changes the shared height also counts for phrases already made: the phrases pre-generated at Idle are checked again against the new height, as after a change of window size. Within each position the staff is placed from the written range, in its clef, of the instrument profile the phrase was written for, never from a table of profiles and never from the phrase's notes: a range that reaches high gets more room above the staff, one that reaches low more room below, so a refill or a new phrase never moves a staff line; only a profile switch can, or saving or deleting my instrument when that changes the height or my instrument's clef. Each position that shows notes holds its own engraving, a separate SVG. At Idle, in the one-system layout the phrase fills the top position and the bottom position stays blank; in the split layout the top position holds bars 1-2 and the bottom one bars 3-4, both drawn at the phrase's one scale with aligned measure columns, so bar 3 sits under bar 1 and bar 4 under bar 2.

A review never cuts a phrase. Its two system positions take, in staff spaces, the larger of the shared height and the height its own session's instrument needs, that instrument's written range read in the clef the session's phrases were played in; that size becomes a block size at the workspace staff's scale, so the notes keep the staff's size in a taller position. One size holds for the whole review, set before its first engraving, so the scroll never jumps. So a session played in my instrument's clef before a clef change, which may need more height than the shared height now has (written C2 to C4 needs 19 staff spaces in treble clef, 16 in bass clef), is drawn whole, in the clef it was played in.

Every measure is drawn at one equal width: the widest of the four measures' minimum widths, and never less than a named minimum measure width. A system needs the leading width (clef, key signature, and on the first system the time signature) plus its measure count (four, or two in the split layout) times that width, plus a fixed padding. The engraving scale is one uniform value for the whole phrase, never above a named maximum and never below the legibility floor. With no height budget it is the largest that fits the width. With one it is the largest that fits both the width and the height: the two system positions must fit the height budget, which is read from the staff frame's computed maximum block size minus its padding and border. The stylesheet builds that maximum from the small viewport height (browser toolbars showing) minus the row above the staff, so a browser toolbar showing or hiding never changes the scale and never redraws the staff.

The viewport the layout is planned in is two things: the layout drawn, and the window's height budget, which belongs to the window and not to the layout: present exactly below 64rem, absent from 64rem up. The height rule applies to whichever layout is drawn whenever there is a budget. At Idle the two coincide (split with a budget below 64rem, one system without one from 64rem up), so Idle behaviour does not depend on this. During a session, the held viewport pairs the layout held at start with the current window's height budget, so a held one-system layout in a window below 64rem is scaled to that window's height, and gives no room when the window is too short for its two positions at the floor. One system can also be engraved alone: the whole phrase is planned exactly as for drawing both systems, so the two halves keep one scale and aligned measure columns, and only the requested part (the whole phrase in the one-system layout, bars 1-2 or bars 3-4 in the split layout) is drawn into its position; a part that does not match the layout is a caller error.

A phrase that cannot be drawn at the legibility floor fails in one of two ways, each its own typed failure. No room: the window cannot hold the layout at the floor whatever notes the phrase holds, because it is too narrow for the leading width plus the fixed padding with every measure at the minimum measure width (the leading width depends on the key signature, which the selected key sets, never on the notes), or, when there is a height budget, too short for the two system positions. Notes too wide: the window has room, but this phrase's widest measure needs more width than the floor allows. The layout module only reports which one happened; what the workspace shows for each is decided by its caller. Other failures (notation fonts unavailable, no usable container, an engraving-library failure, drawn content wider than its container) stay typed, retryable notation errors. No partial staff is ever shown: the notation never scrolls horizontally, is never clipped, and is never split into more than two systems.

Engraving waits until the notation fonts and the container size are ready. It re-engraves when the phrase changes, or when the container width, the height budget or the layout changes; any other viewport change leaves the drawn staff untouched. Every successful engraving is paired with a semantic text description of the whole written phrase (the selected instrument profile's written pitch, clef, key, meter, and each measure's chord symbols, notes, rests, durations and ties) for assistive technology, attached at Idle to the top system's engraving; in the split layout the bottom system's engraving is hidden from assistive technology, since the same description already covers its measures. During a session each phrase on screen is described once, on the first of its systems shown. A failed or superseded engraving clears that description with the staff. The legibility floor is 5 CSS pixels per staff space, signed off by the user on 2026-09-24; the 12 CSS-pixel maximum is an engineering choice, not a product decision.

## TECHNICAL INTERFACE
Layout accepts the written phrase's measured widths, the score container width, the named system height the instruments' written ranges set, each in its clef (the built-in profiles' and, once saved, my instrument's), and the viewport `StaffViewport { layout, heightBudgetPx: number | null }`: the layout drawn, and the window's height budget in CSS pixels, non-null exactly below the 64rem tablet-landscape breakpoint (`NOTATION_PHONE_LAYOUT_MEDIA_QUERY`, a width-only query mirroring 64rem); at Idle the layout is one-system at or above it and split below it. `heldViewport(window, held)` returns `{ layout: held, heightBudgetPx: window.heightBudgetPx }`. It returns either a plan (layout, system count, uniform scale, system position block size, system and measure widths) or one of two typed failures: no room for the staff (too narrow or too short) or notes too wide. A phrase-independent companion gives the system positions' block size, or no room, from the viewport and that named system height alone. Engraving draws one SVG per system position that shows notes, or, through `engravePhraseSystem(host, phrase, phraseNumber, part, containerWidthPx, viewport)`, only the requested part's system (`'whole'` exactly in the one-system layout, else `'opening'` or `'closing'`; a mismatch throws), with the staff placed from the written range the written phrase carries (its instrument profile's), or throws a typed notation error whose code tells the two layout failures (`notation_no_room`, `notation_notes_too_wide`) apart from the retryable ones. `src/notation/notation-layout-policy.ts` plans that height from written ranges alone, never from a phrase: `planBeamGroupMaxSpanSteps(range)` gives the widest beam group, in letter-name steps, a range's phrases can produce (one register window: 7 steps for a range of up to two octaves, more for a wider one); `planStaffInk(range)` gives the ink a range can produce above and below the staff's top line; `planSystemPositionUnits(myInstrumentWrittenRange)` gives the shared block size in engraving units at scale 1, `NOTATION_SYSTEM_POSITION_UNITS` (16 staff spaces) when no my instrument is saved (null) and otherwise the larger of that and the range's ink plus the end clearance, rounded up to whole staff spaces; and `planStaffPlacement(range, systemPositionUnits)` places the staff in its position. Each reads the written range placed in its instrument's clef (the treble clef's top line F5 and middle line B4, the bass clef's A3 and D3). A review sizes its system positions from the larger of that shared size and the size its own session's instrument range needs in the clef its phrases were played in, at the workspace staff's scale.

## EXPECTATION
- At 64rem and above, all four measures render on one staff system in the top position, and the bottom position is reserved and blank; below 64rem, every phrase renders split, bars 1-2 on top and bars 3-4 below, with clef and key signature repeated on the bottom system and the time signature only on the top one.
- Both system positions are always reserved, with one block size that depends on the viewport and the instruments' written ranges, never on a phrase or on which instrument is selected; a review may take more (below).
- A saved my instrument whose written range, placed in its clef, needs no more height than the tallest built-in profile's (its tallest ink plus the clearance at each end within 16 staff spaces) leaves the block size unchanged; one needing more makes it taller for every instrument choice, by whole staff spaces. The block size changes only when my instrument is saved or deleted, never from one phrase to the next.
- With my instrument's written range inside its clef's bound, the shared height is at most 24 staff spaces.
- A save of my instrument that changes the shared height has the phrases pre-generated at Idle checked again against the new height.
- A review's system positions take the larger of the shared height and the height its session's instrument range needs in the clef its phrases were played in, at the workspace staff's scale, one size for the whole review: no phrase in a review is cut, a session played in my instrument's earlier clef included.
- A range's height is read in its own clef: in treble clef, written C2 to C4 needs 19 staff spaces; the same range in bass clef needs fewer, as it sits on that staff with at most two ledger lines.
- Every instrument's ink, my instrument's included, start marker included, stays at least about 0.8 of a staff space inside its system position; the staff sits higher in its position for an instrument whose range reaches low than for one whose range reaches high.
- Below 64rem, a shorter window lowers the scale; a window too short for two systems at the legibility floor gives "no room", whatever the phrase, in either layout, a session's held one-system layout included.
- A window too narrow for the layout's fixed parts at the floor gives "no room"; a window with room but a phrase whose measures are too wide gives "notes too wide"; the two are distinct typed outcomes.
- The scale is never below the floor or above the maximum, and all measures render at one aligned equal width.
- A browser toolbar showing or hiding never re-engraves the staff.
- Every successful engraving is paired with a text description of the whole phrase; a failed one clears it.
- A system engraved alone has the same scale and measure columns as when the phrase is drawn on both systems.
