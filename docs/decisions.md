# Decisions

Status: draft v0.7

## Decision Log

- **D1 - Client-side web application.** Settled from initial brief. The primary generation and playback loop runs in the browser.
- **D2 - A monophonic live player is the core participant.** Settled from initial brief. The product responds to played notes rather than generating a detached backing track.
- **D3 - Supportive feedback, not punitive scoring.** Settled from initial brief. Pitch and timing alignment are tracked to aid practice, not to grade the musician.
- **D4 - Long-term adaptation is deferred.** Settled from initial brief. It is a later objective rather than an initial-release dependency.
- **D5 - First deliverable is a finite four-measure generator.** Settled from follow-up. The controls produce one coherent four-measure proposal, and an explicit action regenerates all four measures together.
- **D6 - Live continuation is second deliverable.** Settled from follow-up; resolves O2. The system listens to one or two player measures, seeds generation, and periodically appends measures to a rolling staff.
- **D7 - MIDI is the sole initial input path.** Settled from follow-up; resolves O1. The second deliverable receives notes from a MIDI controller. Acoustic-microphone pitch capture is deferred.
- **D8 - Notes are the required first-deliverable output.** Settled from follow-up; resolves O9. Generated backing is desirable but non-blocking for the first deliverable.
- **D9 - Musical generators evolve behind decoupled contracts.** Settled from follow-up. The UI, MIDI input, notation, playback, evaluation, and individual musical planners must not depend on one another's internal logic.
- **D10 - Style-neutral default with an optional jazz profile.** Settled from follow-up; resolves O7. The jazz profile provides an early flexibility test without defining the product's default musical language.
- **D11 - Neutral profile grammar.** Settled from follow-up. The profile uses selected major, minor, or modal scales; mostly diatonic chord movement; chord tones and scale passing tones; and non-chord-scale, rhythmic, contour, and silence-based tension. It excludes substitutions, altered dominants, and chromatic enclosures.
- **D12 - Initial jazz vocabulary is chromatic approach and enclosure only.** Settled from follow-up; resolves O4. Altered dominants and harmonic substitutions are deferred.
- **D13 - First-release harmony uses generated diatonic movement.** Settled from follow-up. The player selects tonal center and scale or mode, then uses harmonic-motion and tension controls; direct chord-progression selection is deferred.
- **D14 - Initial pitch controls are contour, register, and leapiness.** Settled from follow-up. They form an independently mapped starting vocabulary to refine from generated-phrase feedback.
- **D15 - First-release meter is fixed 4/4.** Settled from follow-up. Pulse supplies stable BPM, while rhythm controls govern the events inside the meter; alternative meters and tempo variation are deferred.
- **D16 - Standard staff notation is required.** Settled from follow-up. The finite proposal appears on a conventional staff, and the second deliverable extends that notation into a rolling display.
- **D17 - Transposition is switchable presentation.** Settled from follow-up; partially resolves O11. Switching transposition changes written notation only; the generated and stored source remains concert pitch.
- **D18 - Initial transposition choices are Concert, B-flat, and E-flat.** Settled from follow-up; resolves O11. They are shown as instrument-family choices, not raw interval values.
- **D19 - Initial rhythm controls are subdivision, syncopation, and silence density.** Settled from follow-up. They map independently into rhythm-planner policy inside fixed 4/4 meter.
- **D20 - First-release pulse is user-selected stable BPM.** Settled from follow-up. It has no pulse-feel control or tempo variation. Generated-lead playback is not required; BPM-aligned backing is an optional enhancement.
- **D21 - Initial MIDI support is display-only.** Settled from follow-up. It may highlight an incoming note to prove controller integration, but does not yet evaluate or affect generation.
- **D22 - MIDI mismatch feedback hides the played pitch.** Settled from follow-up; resolves O12. Matching proposal notes may highlight; non-matches receive a gentle hint rather than displaying the incoming note.
- **D23 - Regeneration creates reproducible variation.** Settled from follow-up. It preserves controls and requests a new random seed; each result retains its seed for internal reproduction.
- **D24 - Arpeggio chaos remains a first-release control.** Settled from the initial brief and subsequent control discussions. It changes chord-tone ordering and interval contrast within profile and playability constraints; it does not authorize arbitrary pitches.
- **D25 - Initial playability policy is sax-only and independent of transposition.** Settled from follow-up; partially resolves O14. Changing written-pitch display does not regenerate the phrase or alter its active concert-pitch range.
- **D26 - The product does not assign a performance score.** Settled from follow-up; resolves O3. Initial MIDI feedback is a gentle match or mismatch indication, and later feedback remains descriptive rather than scored.
- **D27 - First-release sessions are ephemeral.** Settled from follow-up; resolves O8. A later feature may capture and export sessions.
- **D28 - Initial phrases end open.** Settled from follow-up; resolves O13. The four-measure phrase supports continued playing rather than a final tonic close; closed endings are deferred as an option.
- **D29 - The player-selected key and mode govern MIDI continuation.** Settled from follow-up; resolves O5. A MIDI seed initially contributes phrase character, not automatic tonal inference. Automatic key detection is deferred.
- **D30 - Initial playability range targets tenor saxophone.** Settled from follow-up; resolves O14. Generation is restricted to concert MIDI 45 through 58, corresponding to written `B3` through `C5` for B-flat tenor. Display transposition remains independent, and player-selected ranges are deferred.
- **D31 - First-release device and presentation defaults.** Settled from approved UI/UX decisions. Mobile and tablet support is landscape-only, with a portrait rotate-device notice; first load uses B-flat notation; the theme follows the operating-system preference and permits manual light/dark override.
- **D32 - First-release tonal and tempo domains.** Settled from approved UI/UX decisions. The player may select every chromatic tonal center with context-aware sharp/flat spelling, major, minor, or any church mode; the default is C Ionian. Tempo is stable for the phrase and selectable from 60 through 240 BPM in 10 BPM increments, defaulting to 90 BPM.
- **D33 - Profile presets and melodic-control models.** Settled from approved UI/UX decisions and melodic-control research. Neutral and jazz presets use their defined rhythm and shape defaults. Tension uses `Settled`, `Lift`, `Build`, and `Edge`, default `Lift`; leapiness uses `Smooth`, `Shaped`, `Leaping`, and `Angular`, default `Shaped`, with bounded intervals, budgets, and recovery rules that never override first-release hard constraints.
- **D34 - Varying is the jazz register default.** Settled from the confirmed first-release decision. `Varying` is a fourth player-selectable Register value. For each seeded generation request, it chooses one eligible `Low`, `Middle`, or `High` policy for the entire phrase; eligibility preserves the concert MIDI 45-58 range and all selected contour, profile, leapiness, and recovery rules. It never changes policy within the phrase or relaxes a hard constraint.
- **D35 - Presets replace complete control bundles.** Settled from the confirmed first-release rule; resolves the prior preset-switching gap. Selecting neutral or jazz replaces harmonic motion, note density, syncopation, silence density, contour, and register with that preset's complete defined bundle. A manual change afterward creates a custom current configuration under the selected profile's musical grammar and never mutates either preset definition. Selecting a preset again discards custom values and reapplies its complete bundle.
- **D36 - First-release enharmonic spelling minimizes accidentals.** Settled from the confirmed first-release rule; resolves the canonical-spelling gap. For each Concert, B-flat, or E-flat display mode, derive the written tonal center and collection from the unchanged concert-pitch phrase, select the enharmonic representation requiring the fewest accidentals, and prefer sharps when representations tie. Apply that representation consistently to tonal-center labels, key signatures, displayed note accidentals, and chord symbols. Display changes remain presentation-only and never alter the concert-pitch phrase or its generation inputs.
- **D37 - First-release instrument profile is a C MIDI keyboard.** Settled from the confirmed product decision. The selected C profile is declarative data with a concert MIDI 48-72 active range and an explicit register-window policy supplied to generation. This supersedes the first-release tenor-sax range conclusion in D30 and the MIDI 45-58 range cited in D34; the `Varying` register semantics in D34 otherwise remain settled.
- **D38 - Instrument profiles supply generator policy without branching generation.** Settled from the confirmed architecture decision. A validated declarative instrument profile provides its stable identity, concert-pitch playable range, register-window policy, and metadata. Generation receives the derived range/register policy explicitly and does not branch on profile identity or profile-specific metadata; adding a valid profile is a data and validation change, not a melodic, harmonic, or rhythm-logic change.
- **D39 - Instrument profile and notation display convention are independent.** Settled from the confirmed architecture decision. Concert, B-flat, and E-flat are separately selected notation display conventions. A display change can affect only written notation and cannot mutate the concert-pitch timeline, selected instrument profile, profile-derived generator policy, or generation controls.
- **D40 - First-release instrument profiles use adopted inclusive concert ranges and whole-phrase windows.** Settled from saxophone-range research; resolves O15 and supersedes the obsolete MIDI 45-58 assumptions in D30 and D34. C MIDI keyboard uses `48-72` with Low `48-60`, Middle `54-66`, and High `60-72`; B-flat tenor saxophone uses `46-70` with Low `46-58`, Middle `52-64`, and High `58-70`; E-flat alto saxophone uses `51-75` with Low `51-63`, Middle `57-69`, and High `63-75`. `Varying` deterministically selects one feasible window for the whole phrase and never changes window mid-phrase.
- **D41 - Jazz preset harmonic motion is gentle diatonic.** Settled from the adopted first-release preset policy. Selecting jazz replaces harmonic motion with gentle diatonic motion alongside its other complete preset values.
- **D42 - Notation transforms are display-convention data.** Settled from saxophone-transposition research. The single-staff view uses treble G2 clef. Concert-to-written transforms are Concert `+0 chromatic/+0 diatonic`, B-flat tenor `+14/+8` (major ninth), and E-flat alto `+9/+5` (major sixth). They apply to notes, keys, tonal labels, chord roots, and slash bass, but never chord quality or concert source events.
- **D43 - Instrument-profile switching preserves fitting phrases and regenerates non-fitting phrases.** Settled from the adopted profile-switch policy; resolves O16. Validate unchanged concert events against the new profile's active range. When all fit, retain the phrase and redraw it with the selected display convention. When any event does not fit, retain current controls, choose a fresh seed, and regenerate the complete phrase under the new profile. Never transpose source events to fit.
- **D44 - Instrument-profile switching validates range and resolved register window.** Settled from the established first-release policy; supersedes D43's validation criterion. On a profile switch, validate every event in the immutable concert phrase against both the new profile's inclusive active range and the whole-phrase register window required by the current `Register` selection. `Low`, `Middle`, and `High` select the corresponding new-profile window. For `Varying`, resolve the new profile's candidate window deterministically from the existing seed, using the established ordered register-resolution policy, before validation. Retain the phrase only when every event satisfies both constraints; otherwise retain controls, choose a fresh seed, and regenerate the complete phrase. Source events are never transposed to fit. The switch contract is testable with fixed-seed `Varying` resolution plus independent range-window retention and regeneration cases.
